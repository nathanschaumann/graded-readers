Little Red Riding Hood

A Basket for Grandmother
1. Little Red Riding Hood lived in a small village.
2. Her grandmother lived across the woods.
3. Grandmother gave her a red hood to wear.
4. She wore the red hood.
5. The village called her Little Red Riding Hood.
6. One day, Grandmother was sick.
7. Her mother filled a basket with food.
8. She put cake and bread into the basket.
9. "Take this to Grandmother," said her mother.
10. "Do not leave the path," her mother called.
11. "I will be safe," said Little Red Riding Hood.
12. Then she took the basket and went into the woods.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Little Red Riding Hood lives in a small village near her grandmother, who once gave her a red hood that gave her the name, and one day Grandmother falls sick.
7-12: Her mother packs a basket of cake and bread, tells her to bring it to Grandmother and not to leave the path, and Little Red Riding Hood promises to be safe and sets off into the woods.

The Wolf in the Woods
1. Little Red Riding Hood walked down the path.
2. A wolf came out of the woods.
3. "Good day," said the wolf with a smile.
4. "Why do you have that basket?" he called.
5. "This food is for my sick grandmother," she said.
6. Grandmother lived across the woods.
7. The wolf saw the flowers.
8. "Why not pick flowers for her?" he said.
9. Little Red Riding Hood left the path to pick flowers.
10. The wolf ran through the woods.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Little Red Riding Hood walks down the path through the woods, where a wolf comes out and greets her before asking why she carries a basket.
6-10: She explains the food is for her sick grandmother, who lives across the woods, and the wolf points out flowers, so she leaves the path to pick some while he runs through the woods to beat her there.

The Wolf at Grandmother's House
1. The wolf reached the small house.
2. He knocked on the door two times.
3. "Who is there?" called Grandmother from her bed.
4. "It is me, Little Red Riding Hood," said the wolf.
5. "Come in, the door is open," said Grandmother.
6. The wolf opened the door and went inside.
7. The wolf swallowed Grandmother in one bite.
8. He wore Grandmother's cap.
9. He climbed into Grandmother's bed to hide.
10. He pulled the blanket up to hide.
11. Then the wolf waited for Little Red Riding Hood.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The wolf reaches Grandmother's house first, knocks on the door, and tricks Grandmother into inviting him inside by calling out in Little Red Riding Hood's voice.
6-11: The wolf goes in and swallows Grandmother, then puts on her cap, climbs into her bed to hide, pulls up the blanket, and waits for Little Red Riding Hood to arrive.

What Big Teeth You Have
1. Soon Little Red Riding Hood reached the quiet house.
2. She knocked gently on the wooden door two times.
3. "Come in, the door is open," said the wolf.
4. Little Red Riding Hood walked slowly through the door.
5. She saw her grandmother under the blanket.
6. But her grandmother's face looked strange today.
7. "Grandmother, what big eyes you have!" she said.
8. "All the better to see you with," said the wolf.
9. "Grandmother, what big ears you have!" she said.
10. "All the better to hear you with," said the wolf.
11. "Grandmother, what big teeth you have!" she said.
12. "All the better to eat you with!" said the wolf.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Little Red Riding Hood reaches the quiet house, knocks, and is told to come in, but when she sees what she thinks is her grandmother under the blanket, the face looks strange today.
7-12: She asks about the big eyes, the big ears, and the big teeth she sees, and each time the wolf, still disguised as Grandmother, gives a strange reply, ending with the threat to eat her.

Saved by the Huntsman
1. The wolf jumped up and swallowed Little Red Riding Hood.
2. The wolf's stomach was full, and he fell asleep.
3. A huntsman walked past the small house.
4. He heard snores from inside the house.
5. The huntsman thought Grandmother lived in that house.
6. He knocked, but no one came to the door.
7. The huntsman opened the door and looked inside.
8. He saw the wolf asleep in Grandmother's bed.
9. The wolf's stomach looked big.
10. The huntsman thought Grandmother might be inside.
11. He took out a knife and cut open the wolf.
12. Little Red Riding Hood jumped out safe!

Paragraphs:
1-5: The wolf swallows Little Red Riding Hood and falls asleep, full and heavy, and a passing huntsman hears snores from the house where he knows Grandmother lives.
6-12: Getting no answer at the door, the huntsman goes inside, finds the wolf asleep with a big stomach, and cuts it open with his knife, and Little Red Riding Hood jumps out safe.

The End of the Wolf
1. Then Grandmother climbed out of the wolf too.
2. She was safe.
3. Little Red Riding Hood hugged her grandmother.
4. The huntsman found heavy stones.
5. Grandmother and Little Red Riding Hood filled the wolf.
6. They put the heavy stones inside him.
7. Soon the wolf woke up.
8. He wanted to run from the house.
9. But the stones were too heavy to carry.
10. The wolf fell down and died in the woods.
11. Little Red Riding Hood was safe.
12. The huntsman smiled.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Grandmother climbs out of the wolf unharmed, and Little Red Riding Hood hugs her, while the huntsman finds heavy stones so the two of them can fill the sleeping wolf.
7-12: The wolf wakes and wants to run from the house, but the stones are too heavy to carry, and he falls down dead in the woods, leaving Little Red Riding Hood safe and the huntsman smiling.

Safe at Home
1. They all went inside Grandmother's small house.
2. Little Red Riding Hood gave her the basket.
3. Grandmother ate the cake and the bread.
4. "Thank you," said Grandmother to the huntsman.
5. "It was my pleasure," said the huntsman.
6. The huntsman left.
7. "I will never leave the path," she said.
8. "That is a good lesson," said Grandmother.
9. Little Red Riding Hood smiled and kissed her grandmother.
10. From that day on, she never left the path.

Paragraphs:
1-6: They go inside, and Little Red Riding Hood gives Grandmother the basket of cake and bread; Grandmother thanks the huntsman for saving them, and he leaves.
7-10: Little Red Riding Hood promises to never leave the path, Grandmother calls it a good lesson, and from that day on, Little Red Riding Hood never leaves the path again.
