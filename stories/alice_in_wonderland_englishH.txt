Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

The White Rabbit / Down the Rabbit Hole
1. On a hot afternoon, Alice sat with her sister on the river bank.
2. Her sister read a book with no pictures in it.
3. A book with no pictures was no good to Alice.
4. The sun was hot, and soon Alice was half asleep in the grass.
5. A white rabbit ran past her in the grass.
6. The rabbit took a watch out of its pocket.
7. It looked at the watch as it ran.
8. "I am late!" said the rabbit.
9. Alice got up.
10. She had never seen a rabbit with a watch, or heard one talk.
11. Alice was curious, and she ran after the rabbit across the field.
12. The rabbit ran on and on and did not stop.
13. It went down a big hole under the hedge.
14. Alice went down into the dark hole after it.
15. She did not stop to think about the way out.
16. A little way down in the hole, the ground under her was gone.
17. Alice fell.
18. She fell down and down in the dark hole.
19. Alice fell and fell down the dark hole, but the fall did not hurt her.
20. The hole was deep, but it was not all dark.
21. The sides of the hole were full of little cupboards.
22. She fell past the little doors of the cupboards.
23. As she fell past a cupboard, Alice took a jar from it, but the jar was empty.
24. Alice did not drop the empty jar.
25. She put the jar into the next cupboard as she fell past it.
26. At last the fall ended, and Alice came down soft on dry leaves, and all was still.
27. She was not hurt at all, and she got up fast.
28. The white rabbit was still in front of her, far down a long hall.
29. "I am so late!" said the rabbit.
30. Alice ran after it, but soon the white rabbit was gone.
31. Alice stood alone in the hall, and lamps hung from its roof.
32. There were doors all around the hall.
33. Alice went to the doors, but the doors did not open.
34. Every door in the hall was locked.

Paragraphs:
1-4: On a hot afternoon Alice sits with her sister on the river bank, bored by a book with no pictures, and the hot sun soon has her half asleep in the grass.
5-10: A white rabbit runs past, takes a watch from its pocket, says it is late, and Alice gets up amazed, since she has never seen a rabbit with a watch or heard one talk.
11-15: Curious, Alice chases the rabbit across the field and follows it down a big hole under the hedge, without stopping to think about the way out.
16-18: A little way in, the ground vanishes under her, and Alice falls down and down into the dark, and the fall does not stop.
19-23: Alice falls slowly down a deep hole whose sides are full of little cupboards, and as she falls she takes a jar from one of them, but the jar is empty.
24-25: She does not drop the jar, since the white rabbit could be far below her, and instead puts it into the next cupboard as she falls past — the fall leaves time for all of it.
26-30: The fall ends softly on dry leaves; Alice gets up unhurt, sees the white rabbit far ahead saying it is so late, and runs after it until it is gone.
31-34: Alice stands alone in a long hall with lamps hung from the roof and doors all around, and though she walks from door to door on both sides, every door is locked.

The Little Gold Key / Too Small, Then Too Big
1. In the hall stood a little glass table.
2. On the table was a little gold key.
3. Alice took the key, but it was far too little for the big doors.
4. At one end of the hall, Alice saw a curtain.
5. Behind the curtain was a little door.
6. Alice turned the gold key in the little door, and the door opened.
7. She went down on her knees and looked through it.
8. Through the little door, Alice saw a beautiful garden.
9. She saw bright flowers and little rivers of water.
10. But the little door was too little for Alice.
11. Alice shut the little door, for she could not get through it, and it was locked again.
12. She stood up and put the key back on the glass table.
13. A little bottle stood on the table now.
14. The bottle was not there before.
15. A little paper hung from the bottle, with the words DRINK ME on it.
16. Alice was curious, and she drank all that was in the bottle.
17. At once she began to shrink, and she shrank and shrank.
18. Soon Alice was the right size for the little door.
19. Alice ran back to the little door, but the door was still locked.
20. She had shut it herself, and the key was back on the glass table.
21. The little glass table stood high over her head.
22. Alice tried to get up the glass leg of the table.
23. Her hands slipped on the glass each time.
24. Under the table she saw a little glass box with a cake in it.
25. On the cake were the words EAT ME.
26. Alice ate a little of the cake, but at first she did not grow or shrink.
27. She ate all of the cake.
28. At once Alice began to grow, and she grew fast, up and up.
29. Soon her head came up to the roof of the hall.
30. Alice was a giant now in the hall of doors.
31. She could take the little key from the glass table now.
32. But she could not put the little key into the little door.
33. Her hand was far too big for the little door.
34. Alice got down on the floor and looked through the key hole with one eye.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Alice finds a little glass table in the hall with a gold key on it; the key is far too little for the big doors, but at one end of the hall she finds a little door behind a curtain.
6-11: The gold key turns in the little door, and through it Alice sees a beautiful garden of bright flowers and little rivers, but the door is far too little for her, so she shuts it, and it locks again.
12-18: Alice puts the key back on the glass table, where a little bottle now stands that was not there before; its paper says DRINK ME, and when she drinks it all, she shrinks down to the right size for the little door.
19-23: Alice runs back to the little door, but it is locked, and the key she put back is high on the glass table; she tries to get up the glass leg, but her hands slip each time, and she sees she will never reach it that way.
24-27: Under the table Alice finds a little glass box with a cake in it that says EAT ME, and when a first little taste changes nothing, she eats it all.
28-31: Alice grows fast until her head comes up to the roof of the hall; now she is a giant, and she can easily take the little key from the table.
32-34: But her hand is now far too big to put the key into the little door, and all she can do is look at the garden through the key hole with one eye — big or small, she cannot get in.

The Pool of Tears / The Caterpillar on the Mushroom
1. Alice sat down on the floor of the hall and cried and cried.
2. Her tears were big tears, for Alice was a giant still.
3. Soon there was a wide pool of tears all around her on the floor.
4. The white rabbit came back into the hall.
5. It had a little fan in one hand.
6. The rabbit saw the giant Alice there, and it was afraid.
7. It dropped the little fan and ran from the hall.
8. Alice took the fan up and waved it before her hot face.
9. As she waved the fan, Alice began to shrink down and down.
10. Alice dropped the fan at once, and she shrank no more.
11. The key was too big for her little hands now, and it fell by the little door.
12. Alice ran for the little door, but her feet slipped on the wet floor, and she fell into the pool.
13. The water of the pool went over her head.
14. Alice swam and swam, but the pool had grown wide around her.
15. When her feet found the ground at last, the hall was gone.
16. Alice walked up out of the water and into the grass.
17. She stood at the edge of a wood, small and wet from her hair to her feet.
18. Alice walked into the wood.
19. She was so small now that the grass of the wood stood high over her head.
20. Soon Alice came to a mushroom that was as high as her head.
21. On top of the mushroom sat a big caterpillar.
22. Alice looked up at it, and the caterpillar looked down at Alice.
23. It looked at her for a long time and said nothing at all.
24. "Who are you?" the caterpillar asked at last.
25. "I do not know now," said Alice.
26. "Today I was big, and today I was small."
27. "What size do you want to be?" the caterpillar asked.
28. "I want my own right size," said Alice.
29. As it went down into the grass at last, the caterpillar said, "One side will make you grow."
30. "The other side will make you shrink."
31. "One side of what?" Alice asked it.
32. "Of the mushroom," said the caterpillar, and it was gone in the grass.
33. Alice put her arms around the mushroom as far as they would go.
34. She took a little piece from each side of it, one piece in each hand.
35. But the two sides of the mushroom looked the same.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Still a giant, Alice sits on the floor of the hall and cries, and her giant tears soon make a wide pool; the white rabbit comes back with a little fan, but at the sight of the giant Alice it is afraid, drops the fan, and runs.
8-11: Alice waves the fan before her hot face and begins to shrink, sees it is the fan that is making her small, and drops it just in time; the key is now too big for her little hands and falls by the little door.
12-13: Small again, Alice runs for the little door, but she slips on the wet floor into the pool of her own tears, cried when she was a giant, and the water goes over her head.
14-17: She swims and swims across the grown-wide pool until her feet find ground, and when she walks out of the water, the hall is gone and she stands at the edge of a wood, small and wet from her hair to her feet.
18-21: Alice walks into the wood, so small now that the grass stands high over her head, and comes to a mushroom as high as her head with a big caterpillar sitting on top.
22-26: The caterpillar looks down at Alice a long time without a word, then asks who she is, and she says she does not know now, since today she has been both big and small.
27-32: The caterpillar asks what size she wants to be, and as it goes down into the grass it says that one side will make her grow and the other will make her shrink — one side of the mushroom — and then it is gone before she can ask more.
33-35: Alice reaches around the mushroom and takes a little piece from each side, one in each hand, but the two sides look the same, and she does not know which piece is which.

The Two Pieces / The Cat That Faded Away
1. Alice bit a little from the piece in her right hand.
2. At once she began to grow, and she grew fast, up and up and up.
3. Her head went up above the green tops of the trees.
4. Alice was wet from the pool, but the hot sun soon made her dry.
5. She looked for the white rabbit, but she did not see it.
6. Far away, over the trees, Alice saw the roof of a little house.
7. But she did not want to be a giant in the wood.
8. Alice bit a little from the piece in the other hand.
9. She shrank so fast that the grass was soon high over her head again.
10. After that, Alice was careful.
11. She took little bites, first from one piece and after that from the other piece.
12. One little bite made her grow, and the next little bite made her shrink.
13. At last Alice stood at her own right size in the wood again.
14. The grow piece went into her right pocket, the shrink piece into her left.
15. Alice was curious about the little house far off in the wood.
16. She went on through the green trees with a piece in each pocket.
17. The road went on under the trees.
18. As Alice walked, a smile appeared in the air over the road.
19. It was a wide smile, and there was no cat around it.
20. Around the smile, a cat appeared, a little at a time.
21. First came the head of the cat, and after that the rest of it.
22. Soon a big cat sat in the tree over the road, and it smiled down at Alice.
23. "Which is the right way to go?" Alice asked the cat.
24. "Where do you want to go?" the cat asked in a soft voice.
25. "I do not know," said Alice.
26. "If you do not know, any road will do," said the cat.
27. "That way lives a hatter," it said.
28. "The other way lives a hare."
29. "They are both strange," the cat said.
30. Alice wanted to see the hare, so she took that road.
31. When Alice looked back, the cat faded away, a little at a time.
32. Its tail went first, and after that the rest of it.
33. At last only the wide smile hung in the air over the road.
34. The smile hung there alone for a time, and after that it faded away too.

Paragraphs:
1-3: Alice bites the piece in her right hand and grows fast, up above the tops of the trees into the blue sky, a giant over the wood again.
4-7: The hot sun up there soon dries her, and though she looks for the white rabbit, all she sees far away is the roof of a little house — and she does not want to be a giant in the wood.
8-12: She bites the other piece and shrinks too far, down under the grass again, so after that she is careful, taking little bites from one piece and then the other, growing and shrinking a little at a time.
13-16: At last Alice stands at her own right size; she puts the grow piece in her right pocket and the shrink piece in her left, so that she can be big or small when she wants, and walks on through the trees, curious about the little house.
17-22: On the road under the trees, a wide smile appears in the air with no cat around it, and then a cat appears around the smile a little at a time, head first, until a big smiling cat sits in the tree.
23-29: Alice asks which is the right way to go, and the cat asks where she wants to go; when she does not know, it says any road will do, and tells her a hatter lives down one road and a hare down the other, and that both are strange.
30-34: Alice takes the road to the hare, and when she looks back, the cat fades away a little at a time, tail first, until only the wide smile hangs alone in the air — and after a time that fades too, leaving Alice to say to herself what a strange thing a smile with no cat is.

The Tea Party That Never Ended / The Door in the Tree
1. Soon Alice came to the little house, and before it, under a tree, stood a long table.
2. Cups and cups stood all down the long table.
3. The hatter and the hare sat at one end, and a dormouse sat with them, fast asleep at the table.
4. The hatter and the hare rested their arms on the dormouse and talked over its head.
5. "No room at the table!" called the hare when it saw Alice.
6. "There is room for me," said Alice.
7. She sat down in a big chair at the end of the long table.
8. "What do a watch and a tea table both have?" the hatter asked her.
9. "I do not know," said Alice at last.
10. "I do not know it at all," said the hatter.
11. The hatter took out a watch and looked at it for a long time.
12. "It is six, and it is always six," said the hatter.
13. "Six is tea time, so it is always tea time at this table."
14. "We drink tea all day," said the hare.
15. When a cup was dirty, they all moved one chair down the table.
16. Alice got no tea at all at the tea party.
17. "This is the strangest tea party I have ever seen," said Alice, and she got up.
18. She walked away from the table and did not look back at it.
19. Behind her, the hatter and the hare only moved down one more chair.
20. Alice walked on from the tea party into the wood.
21. One big tree had a little door in its side.
22. She opened the door in the tree and walked in.
23. In the tree there was no tree at all.
24. Alice was back in the long hall!
25. The lamps still hung from the roof, and the glass table stood where it had stood before.
26. The little gold key was still on the floor where it had fallen.
27. This time, Alice knew what to do.
28. She took the little gold key and went down on her knees at the little door.
29. Alice turned the gold key, and the little door opened once more.
30. Alice held the little door open with one hand.
31. With the other hand, she took the little shrink piece from her left pocket.
32. She bit it once and shrank, with her hand still on the open door.
33. When the little door stood as high as her head, Alice walked through it into the garden.
34. The bright flowers of the garden stood high over her head once more.
35. Alice took the grow piece from her right pocket and ate a little bite.
36. She grew back to her own right size there in the garden.

Paragraphs:

Painting the Roses Red / Croquet with the Queen
1. Not far down the garden, Alice came to a tree full of white roses.
2. Three gardeners stood at the tree, and they painted the white roses red.
3. But many of the roses on the tree were still all white.
4. "What is all the red for?" Alice asked the three gardeners at the tree.
5. "We planted white roses by mistake, and the queen wanted red," said one gardener.
6. "If the queen sees white roses in her garden, she will have our heads cut off."
7. Alice heard a horn far away across the garden.
8. The gardeners heard it too and went down flat on their faces.
9. Soldiers marched into the garden of red and white roses, two by two.
10. Each soldier was a card with two arms and two legs.
11. The cards were flat and white, with red hearts on them.
12. After the card soldiers came the King and Queen of Hearts.
13. The Queen of Hearts stopped at the tree and looked at the white and red roses.
14. "The roses are still white!" the queen shouted.
15. "Off with their heads!" she shouted at the gardeners.
16. But the queen had turned away to the next tree of roses.
17. The gardeners ran to Alice, and the three flat cards went into a big garden pot by the tree.
18. The soldiers looked for them but could not find them, and soon the queen went on down the garden.
19. "Can you play croquet?" the queen asked Alice.
20. "I can," said Alice, and she walked after the queen to the croquet ground.
21. The croquet ground had many players, and the queen and the king played too.
22. Every ball and every hoop in the game could get up and walk.
23. The balls of the game were hedgehogs, and each hedgehog was a ball.
24. The players hit the balls of the game with flamingos.
25. Alice put the head of her flamingo down to the ground to hit her hedgehog.
26. But the flamingo turned its long neck up and looked at her.
27. Alice laughed at it, and the flamingo would not go down any more.
28. When its head went down once more, her hedgehog had stood up and walked away.
29. The hoops of the game were card soldiers, down on their hands and feet.
30. When a ball came to them, the card soldiers stood up and walked away too.
31. All the players played at once, and they took the hedgehogs from each other.
32. "Off with his head!" the queen shouted at one player, and at the next player after him.
33. But no head ever came off in the garden.
34. The king walked behind the queen and said to each player, "You are safe."
35. The game was over when every hedgehog had walked away from the ground.
36. As the players stood still, the white rabbit ran in and held a horn up high.
37. All the cards went out of the garden after the rabbit.
38. Alice went out of the garden after them.

Who Stole the Tarts?
1. The cards all went in two long rows to the court of the King and Queen of Hearts.
2. The king and the queen sat on the high seats above everyone in the court.
3. The king was the judge, and he had a white wig on his head.
4. Small animals sat in two rows at one side of the court that day.
5. They were the jury, and they wrote down all that they saw and heard.
6. In the court, before everyone, stood a little table full of the queen's tarts.
7. The Knave of Hearts stood before the high seats, with a card soldier on each side of him.
8. With a long paper in his hand, the white rabbit called, "Quiet in the court!"
9. "The Queen of Hearts made the tarts on a hot day," read the rabbit from the paper.
10. "The Knave of Hearts stole the tarts and took them all far away from the court."
11. "I did not steal them!" said the Knave of Hearts to all the court.
12. "The court will now hear the first witness," said the king on his high seat.
13. The first witness of the day was the hatter, with a cup of hot tea in his hand.
14. "I was not done with my tea," said the hatter.
15. "It is always tea time for me," he said to the king.
16. "What do you know about the tarts?" the king asked the hatter from his high seat.
17. "Nothing at all," said the hatter, and he looked down at his cup of tea.
18. The hands of the hatter shook, and his tea ran over the edge of his cup.
19. "You can go," said the king, and the hatter ran out of the court and far away.
20. The white rabbit looked at the long paper again, and Alice was next on the paper.

Paragraphs:
1-5: All the cards go to the court of the King and Queen of Hearts, where the king and queen sit on high seats above everyone; the king is the judge in a white wig, and small animals sit in two rows as the jury, writing down all they see.

Alice Is Called / Only a Deck of Cards
1. Alice stood up, and everyone in the court looked at her at once.
2. "What do you know about the tarts?" the king asked her before all the court.
3. "Nothing at all about them," said Alice to the king.
4. "That is important," said the king to the jury.
5. "The king means it is not important," said the white rabbit, and he looked at the king.
6. The small animals of the jury wrote down both of the words.
7. The white rabbit stood up again with one more paper in his hand.
8. "It is a poem about the tarts," said the rabbit to the court.
9. But the Knave of Hearts was not in the poem at all.
10. "This poem means nothing at all," said Alice to all the court.
11. "If it means nothing, we can all go home now," said the king.
12. "The jury will now say the verdict," said the king, and the small animals of the jury stood up.
13. "No!" the queen shouted, and the court went quiet all at once.
14. "Punishment first, and the verdict after," said the queen to all the court.
15. "That is not right," said Alice, and she stood still.
16. "The punishment cannot come before the verdict," Alice said to the queen and the king.
17. "Off with her head!" the queen shouted at Alice, and the court went quiet again.
18. Two rows of card soldiers marched at Alice to take hold of her.
19. Alice put her hand into her right pocket, and the last piece of the mushroom was still there.
20. Alice ate it all at once, and she began to grow and grow, up and up.
21. Alice grew and grew, up and up, before the high seats of the little court.
22. Her head went up above the high seats and above the court.
23. The king and the queen looked small under her now, and the court looked small too.
24. The card soldiers stopped where they stood, every one of them.
25. The small animals of the jury ran from their seats and away out of the court.
26. The white rabbit dropped his long paper and ran from the court.
27. "Off with her head!" the queen shouted up one more time at the giant Alice.
28. But not one card soldier moved a step.
29. The queen looked up at the giant Alice and went one step back.
30. "I am not afraid of you now," said Alice from high above them.
31. "You are only a deck of cards, all of you!"
32. At that, the whole deck of cards flew up into the air at once.
33. The king, the queen, the knave, and all the card soldiers were up in the air.
34. The whole court of cards came down at Alice from the air.
35. Alice cried out and hit at them as they fell all around her in the air.
36. But the cards came down soft and dry on her face, again and again.
37. Alice shut her eyes as the soft cards fell all around her.

The River Bank Again
1. Alice opened her eyes.
2. Her head was in her sister's lap, on the grass of the river bank.
3. Dry leaves fell from the tree, and they fell soft on her face.
4. Her sister took the dry leaves from Alice's hair.

15. In her dream, Alice got no tea at all, so this time she would drink her tea at home.
