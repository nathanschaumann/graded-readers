Sinbad the Sailor

The Porter at the Gate / The Island That Was a Whale
1. A poor porter lived in Baghdad, and his name was Sinbad.
2. Each day he carried heavy loads in the hot streets.
3. One day he set his load down at the gate of a great house.
4. He heard a great feast inside.
5. "The master of this house rests all day," the porter said.
6. "I walk the hot streets under heavy loads, and my food is bread."
7. "Why must one man walk all day, and one man rest and eat?"
8. A servant heard him and went inside.
9. He came back and said, "My master asks for you."
10. The porter went in and stood in a great hall full of guests.
11. A rich man sat at the feast.
12. "What is your name?" the man asked.
13. "I am Sinbad the porter," said the porter.
14. "Sinbad is my name too, for I am Sinbad the Sailor," said the man.
15. "You think the sea gave me my gold and this great house."
16. "Seven times I went to sea, and seven times the sea nearly killed me."
17. "Sit and eat, and hear the tales of my seven voyages."
18. The porter sat down, and Sinbad the Sailor began the first tale.
19. The father of Sinbad the Sailor was a rich man of Baghdad.
20. He died and left young Sinbad his gold, his houses, and his land.
21. Young Sinbad feasted with rich young men, and in one year the gold was gone.
22. One morning he saw what was left, and he was afraid.
23. He sold the houses and the land and gave the gold for goods to trade.
24. With his goods he went on a ship full of merchants.
25. The ship sailed for many days to far lands and far islands.
26. At the far islands Sinbad sold his goods, and the trade made him rich.
27. One day the ship came to an island for water.
28. The men went ashore with food and a wooden tub, and made a fire on the sand.
29. But the island was not land at all.
30. It was a great whale, and it had slept so long that sand and trees grew on its back.
31. The fire woke the whale, and the sand began to move under the men.
32. "To the ship!" said the captain.
33. The whale dived, and the island went down under the water.
34. The men went back to the ship, but the waves had carried Sinbad far from it.
35. He took hold of the wooden tub, and the ship went on and left him.
36. All night Sinbad held the tub.
37. In the morning the waves left him on the sand of a far island.

1-4: A poor porter named Sinbad carries loads through the hot streets of Baghdad, and one day he sets his load down at the gate of a great house, where a feast is going on inside.

34-37: The men get back to the ship, but the waves carry Sinbad too far; he takes hold of the wooden tub as the ship leaves him, and in the morning the waves leave him on the sand of a far island.

The King's Harbor / Left on the Green Island
1. Men of the island king found Sinbad on the sand.
2. The men gave him food and water and took him to the king.
3. Sinbad told the king of the whale, the tub, and the lost ship.
4. The king was glad and gave Sinbad work at the harbor.
5. Each day he saw the ships that came in from the sea.
6. He wanted to go home to Baghdad.
7. One day a ship came in that Sinbad knew.
8. It was his own ship, the ship that had left him on the whale.
9. "I am Sinbad," he said to the captain.
10. "Sinbad went down with the whale," said the captain.
11. "Who are you?"
12. "Open the bales in your ship, and I will say what is in each one."
13. Sinbad said what was in each bale before the men opened it.
14. At that the captain knew him, and he gave back all the goods.
15. Sinbad sold his goods and was a rich man again.
16. Sinbad gave rich gifts to the king, and the king gave rich gifts to Sinbad.
17. He sailed home to Baghdad with more gold than he had lost.
18. "That was the first voyage," said Sinbad the Sailor.
19. He gave the porter 100 pieces of gold and said, "Come again and hear more."
20. On the second night, the porter came back, and Sinbad the Sailor told of the second voyage.
21. For a year Sinbad rested in his great house, but in time he wanted the sea and far lands again.
22. He took goods and went to sea with a ship of merchants.
23. One day the ship came to a green island.
24. Sinbad ate and slept under a tree.
25. He woke, and the ship was gone.
26. No man had seen him under the tree, and the merchants had sailed on.
27. Sinbad climbed a high tree and saw no ship on the sea.
28. He saw a great white thing far over the trees.
29. The sun went low, and a great bird came down over the island.
30. Its great wings made the island dark.
31. The bird sat down on the white thing, and Sinbad knew it was an egg.
32. He knew the bird too, from tales the sailors told: it was a roc.
33. The roc was a bird so great that it could take an elephant in its claws.
34. A roc could fly him from the island.
35. In the dark, Sinbad tied himself with his turban to the leg of the bird and did not move.
36. In the morning the roc flew high over far lands, with Sinbad on its leg.
37. It came down in a great valley, and Sinbad made himself free of the leg.
38. The roc took a great snake in its claws and flew over walls that no man could climb.

The Valley of Diamonds / The Merchants' Trick
1. The ground of the valley was all stones.
2. Sinbad took up a stone, and it was a diamond.
3. The valley was full of diamonds, more than a man could hold.
4. In Baghdad, one stone from this valley could make a poor man rich.
5. But a man could not eat diamonds, and Sinbad wanted bread and water more than stones.
6. The walls of the valley had holes, and in the holes lived great snakes.
7. Each snake was as long as a ship.
8. By day the snakes hid in the holes, for fear of the great birds.
9. The sun went low, and the snakes came out of the holes.
10. Sinbad found a small hole with no snake in it.
11. He went in and put stones all around him.
12. All night long the snakes went around and around the stones, and Sinbad did not sleep.
13. In the morning the snakes went back into the holes.
14. Sinbad came out from the stones.
15. A great piece of meat fell down on the stones.
16. Soon one more piece fell, and one more.
17. Sinbad saw men high on the walls of the valley, small and far.
18. He knew this valley from a tale merchants told in Baghdad.
19. The meat was a trick of the diamond merchants.
20. Sinbad sat down by the meat to wait.
21. No man could go down into the valley of diamonds and come out again.
22. The diamond merchants had a trick: they went up on the walls and threw down great pieces of raw meat.
23. The meat was soft and wet, and where it fell, diamonds stuck to it.
24. Great eagles had nests on the tops of the walls.
25. Each eagle came down for the meat and took it up to its nest.
26. The diamonds went up with the meat.
27. At the nests the merchants shouted and threw stones, and the eagles flew away.
28. The merchants took the diamonds out of the meat.
29. First Sinbad filled his coat with diamonds.
30. He tied himself with the turban under the greatest piece of meat.
31. A great eagle came down, took the meat in its claws, and flew up with meat and man.
32. It put them down in its nest, high on the wall.
33. A merchant ran to the nest, shouting and throwing stones, and the eagle flew away.
34. Under the meat he found not diamonds but a man.
35. The merchant fell back, afraid.
36. "Do not fear me," said Sinbad, "for I have diamonds for you and for me."
37. Sinbad filled the merchant's hands with diamonds, and the merchant was glad.
38. The other merchants came, and Sinbad told his tale and gave diamonds to each one.
39. He went home with the merchants over land, and came into Baghdad with his coat full of diamonds.

The Giant's House / Rafts in the Night
1. "The sea will kill you," said Sinbad's friends.
2. But Sinbad sailed again.
3. A great storm came over the sea, and it went on for a day and a night.
4. It threw the ship onto the stones of an island, and the ship went to pieces.
5. Sinbad and some of the men came out of the sea to the island.
6. On the island stood a great house of dark stone, with an open gate.
7. The men went into the house and sat down.
8. At dark the ground shook, and the giant came to the house.
9. He stood as high as 3 men, and his teeth were long.
10. The men hid in the dark of the house.
11. No man could fight him, for he was too strong.
12. The giant took one of the men and ate him.
13. The giant slept by the gate, and no man could go out.
14. In the morning the giant went away over the island.
15. The men stood by the sea and wept.
16. "We will leave this island, or we will die on it, man by man," said Sinbad.
17. Some men said they would fight the giant in his sleep.
18. "A fight would wake him, and no man would live," said Sinbad.
19. "We will make rafts from the wood of the lost ship."
20. The men made rafts from the wood, tied them with ropes, and hid them under trees.
21. At dark the men went back into the house, so the giant would not look for them.
22. In the night the giant took one more man.
23. On the third day the rafts were done, and the men pushed out on the water.
24. When the giant came home, he found no man in the house.
25. He went to the water and threw stones at the rafts.
26. The stones went into the sea, and the rafts were too far out.
27. For 2 days and 2 nights the rafts were on the sea, and the food was gone.
28. A trade ship found them at last and took the men on.
29. The captain was a good man and gave them food and water.
30. The ship traded from port to port, and Sinbad did a sailor's work for his way home.
31. He came home to Baghdad with a purse of pearls the captain gave him for his work.
32. When the tale was done, the porter asked, "Why did you go back to the sea?"
33. "At home I forgot the fear, but I did not forget the wonder," said Sinbad the Sailor.

The Wall of Wood / The Broken Egg
1. On the fourth voyage the sea was calm for many days.
2. In the dark of night the ship ran on stones and went down.
3. The waves put Sinbad on a far shore, and he saw no other man in the water.
4. On the island were fruit trees and water.
5. But when dark came, great snakes came out of holes in the ground.
6. That night Sinbad went high into a tree and did not sleep.
7. By day he made a wall of wood and sticks, too high for the snakes.
8. He left one way in, and at dark he put wood over it.
9. All night the snakes went around and around the wall, but not one came in.
10. When morning came, the snakes went into the ground, and Sinbad slept.
11. He lived so for many days, with fruit by day and the wall by night.
12. At last he saw a ship far out on the sea.
13. He made a great fire on the shore and put green wood on it.
14. The men on the ship saw the fire and came in for him.
15. The captain heard his tale and gave him silk to trade on the way home.
16. Sinbad came home from the fourth voyage with little gold, but with his life.
17. The fifth time, Sinbad took a ship of his own to sea, and other merchants came on it too.
18. At an island the men went ashore and came to a great egg.
19. Sinbad knew what it was, for he had seen the egg of a roc before.
20. "Leave the egg," Sinbad said, "for the great birds will come back for it."
21. But the merchants would not hear him.
22. They broke the egg with stones and ate the young bird.
23. The day went dark, for the father and the mother had come back.
24. The men took the ship out, but the birds came again, each one with a great stone in its claws.
25. One stone fell by the ship, and the sea went over it.
26. The other stone fell on the ship and broke it in 2.
27. The other men went down with the ship and did not come up.
28. Sinbad took hold of a piece of the ship's wood.
29. He came ashore on an island of fruit and streams, and ate the fruit.
30. By a stream sat an old man with a white beard.
31. With his hands he made signs at the stream, for he did not speak.
32. Sinbad took the old man up on his shoulders and went into the stream.
33. Over the stream, Sinbad went to set him down on the stones.
34. But the old man put his legs over Sinbad's shoulders and held on to his neck.
35. The legs were thin, but they were strong, and they would not let go.
36. From that day, the old man sat on Sinbad's shoulders by day and by night, and would not come down.

The Gourd of Wine / The Shore of Lost Ships
1. The old man made Sinbad walk under the fruit trees all day.
2. He ate the fruit from Sinbad's hands and gave him a little.
3. He was heavy, and Sinbad grew thin under him.
4. In sleep too, the old legs held Sinbad's neck.
5. Grapes grew all over that island.
6. One day, under the trees, Sinbad took a dry gourd from the ground.
7. He put the juice of the grapes in the gourd.
8. He left it in the sun for some days, and the sun made the juice strong.
9. The juice was wine, and Sinbad drank a little and sang.
10. The old man put out his hands for the gourd, and Sinbad gave it to him.
11. The old man drank the wine down, and he drank it all.
12. Soon the old man slept, and his legs let go.
13. Sinbad took him from his shoulders and set him down soft on the ground.
14. Sinbad walked, and Sinbad ran.
15. At the shore was a ship, and the men took Sinbad on.
16. Sinbad told the men of the old man, and one old sailor knew the tale.
17. "That was the Old Man of the Sea," the old sailor said.
18. "No man he took was free of him — you are the first."
19. In every port Sinbad told his tale, and merchants gave him goods for the wonder of it.
20. He came home to Baghdad with a full purse.
21. Merchants came to Sinbad and asked him to go on a sixth voyage with them.
22. Far out on that voyage, the captain lost the way.
23. A great wind took the ship into seas no man on it knew.
24. One morning the captain threw down his turban and wept.
25. "A current takes the ship," he said.
26. "It runs to a black mountain, and no ship comes back from it."
27. The fast current took the ship on the stones under the mountain and broke it.
28. No man but Sinbad came out of the sea alive.
29. The shore was full of the goods and the wood of lost ships.
30. The cliffs went up and up, and no man could climb them.
31. On that shore was a river, and every river Sinbad knew ran into the sea.
32. This river ran from the sea into the land, to a great cave deep under the mountain.
33. In the lost ships was food for some days.
34. Sinbad was afraid of the dark way, but more afraid of the empty shore.
35. He would go with the river.
36. He made a raft from the wood of the wrecks and tied it with ropes.
37. On the raft he put silk and jewels from the lost ships.
38. He took the raft into the river and went in under the mountain.
39. The day grew small at his back, and was gone.

1-4: The old man rides Sinbad under the fruit trees all day, eating from his hands and giving him little, and even in sleep the old legs hold Sinbad's neck.

Serendib / The King's Errand
1. Under the mountain the dark was deep, and Sinbad could not see his hands.
2. The stone of the mountain came low over him, and he made himself small on the raft.
3. The river ran on and on in the dark, and at last Sinbad slept.
4. He woke in the light of day, on a slow river in a land of green fields.
5. They gave him food and brought him to the king.
6. The land was Serendib, and the king of Serendib was good to strangers.
7. The king knew Sinbad's name, for merchants brought his tales over the sea.
8. Sinbad told the king all his tales, from the whale to the river under the mountain.
9. Of all the tales, the king asked for the valley of diamonds 2 times.
10. In time Sinbad wanted to go home, and the king gave him great gifts.
11. One gift was a cup made from one red stone, and with it went spice and silk.
12. The king sent him home on a ship of Serendib, with the gifts and the goods of the raft.
13. Sinbad came home from the sixth voyage with more than from all the voyages before it.
14. "6 voyages," said the porter, and he counted on his fingers.
15. "One voyage more," said Sinbad.
16. The porter went home, and came back for the last tale.
17. "I am done with the sea," Sinbad said when he came home the sixth time.
18. But the king of Baghdad sent for Sinbad and said, "Take my gifts to the king of Serendib."
19. No man says no to a king.
20. Sinbad went to sea one more time and gave the gifts to the king of Serendib.
21. On the way home, pirates took the ship.
22. They sold the men in a port, and a rich master bought Sinbad.
23. "Can you shoot with a bow?" said the master, and Sinbad said he could.
24. The master gave him a bow and arrows and brought him to a great tree by a road.
25. "Shoot the elephants of the road, and bring me the great teeth."
26. "For the teeth are ivory, and ivory makes men rich."
27. Sinbad waited in the tree with the bow.
28. The elephants came down the road slow, the young ones by the old.
29. Sinbad put the arrow to the bow, and he put the bow down.
30. He did not want to kill the great beasts.
31. Day after day, Sinbad brought the master no teeth.
32. "Shoot," said the master, "or you will go back to the port."
33. In the morning, all the elephants of the road came around his tree.
34. The greatest elephant put its trunk around Sinbad and lifted him from the tree.
35. It set him on its back and took him into the hills.

The Elephants' Ground / The Two Sinbads
1. The elephant went into the green hills with Sinbad on its back.
2. It came to an open ground in the hills and put Sinbad down.
3. All that ground was white with the teeth and the bones of elephants dead many years.
4. A man could not count the teeth of that ground in a day.
5. Old elephants came to this ground to die, year after year, and no man knew it.
6. This was the graveyard of the elephants.
7. The men could take the teeth of the dead, and never kill elephants.
8. The elephant took Sinbad back down the hills to the tree again.
9. Sinbad put his hand on its trunk, and the elephant went slow into the trees.
10. Sinbad told the master all that he had seen in the hills.
11. The master went with him into the hills, and it was all true.
12. "You have made me a rich man," said the master.
13. "From this day you are a free man."
14. For a month his men went into the hills and carried heavy ivory down to the master's house.
15. The master gave Sinbad ivory of his own and put him on a ship for home.
16. Sinbad came home to Baghdad from the seventh and last voyage a rich man.
17. He gave gold to the poor of Baghdad and rested in his great house.
18. Sinbad never went to sea again.
19. When the last tale was done, the hall was quiet.
20. For a long time the porter said nothing.
21. "Seven times you went to sea," the porter said at last.
22. "Seven times the sea nearly killed you, and seven times you came home."
23. "No man in the world tells seven such tales of the sea."
24. "At your gate I put my load down and said that the master of the house rests all day."
25. "I know what work bought that rest."
26. "Your gold did not come to you by luck."
27. At that, Sinbad the Sailor laughed, and the guests laughed with him.
28. "No, my friend," he said.
29. "It came by work, as your bread comes to you."
30. He put a purse of 100 gold pieces in the porter's hands.
31. It was the seventh purse of gold that came home with the porter.
32. "Come and eat at my table when you will," said Sinbad the Sailor.
33. The porter came again, and again, and always ate at that table with him.
34. Porter and sailor had one name between them, and in the end one table.
35. When men in the streets said gold came by luck, the porter told them of the seven voyages.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The elephant carries Sinbad into the green hills to an open ground, white with the teeth and bones of elephants dead many years, more than a man could count, and the ground is quiet.
5-7: Old elephants come to this ground to die, year after year — it is the graveyard of the elephants — and Sinbad sees why he was brought: men can take the teeth of the dead and never kill an elephant for them.
8-11: The elephant carries Sinbad back to the tree, Sinbad lays his hand on its trunk, and it goes slow away into the trees; he tells the master all, and when the master goes to see, it is all true.
12-18: The master calls himself a rich man, sets Sinbad free, and after a month of carrying ivory gives Sinbad ivory of his own and a ship for home; Sinbad comes home from the seventh and last voyage rich, gives gold to the poor of Baghdad, and never goes to sea again.
19-23: When the last tale is done the hall is quiet, and the porter, who has not spoken, says at last that Sinbad went to sea seven times, nearly died seven times, and came home seven times — no man in the world tells seven such tales.
24-29: The porter recalls what he said at the gate about the master who rests all day, and says he knows now what work bought that rest, for the gold did not come by luck; Sinbad laughs and agrees — it came by work, as the porter's bread comes.
30-33: Sinbad puts a seventh purse of 100 gold pieces into the porter's hands, the guests smile at the porter's thanks, and Sinbad asks him to eat at his table when he will, which the porter does again and again.
34-35: Porter and sailor share one name and, in the end, one table; the two Sinbads grow old as friends, the porter never eats dry bread again, and when men in the streets say gold comes by luck, he tells them of the seven voyages.
