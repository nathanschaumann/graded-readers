The Nutcracker and the Mouse King

Christmas Eve
1. It was Christmas Eve at Clara's house.
2. Clara and her brother stood by a tree.
3. Clara's parents welcomed many guests into the house.
4. Many guests stood by the tree too.
5. Clara's brother wanted to open his presents.
6. "Not yet," said Clara's mother, "we must wait for our guests."
7. Clara's brother did not want to wait.
8. Clara opened the door.
9. Her godfather stood at the door with a box.
10. Her godfather was an old clockmaker.
11. He made wonderful toys.
12. Clara took her godfather inside.
13. "Come in and get warm," said Clara's mother.
14. Clara's brother looked at the box.
15. "What is inside the box?" he said.
16. Clara wanted to see inside the box too.
17. "You must wait and see," said the old clockmaker.

Paragraphs:
1-4: On Christmas Eve, Clara and her brother stand by the tree while Clara's parents welcome many guests into the house.
5-7: Clara's brother wants to open his presents, but his mother says they must wait for the guests, and he does not want to wait.
8-13: Clara opens the door to her godfather, an old clockmaker who makes wonderful toys, standing there with a box, and she takes him inside, where her mother welcomes him.
14-17: Clara's brother asks what is inside the box, and Clara wants to see inside it too, but the old clockmaker tells them they must wait and see.

A Gift for Clara
1. The old clockmaker opened his box.
2. Inside the box were many small toys.
3. Many guests looked at the wonderful toys.
4. The old clockmaker turned to Clara.
5. "I have a wonderful gift for you," he said.
6. He gave Clara a small wooden man.
7. The small wooden man had a big jaw.
8. He wore a red coat.
9. "This is a nutcracker," said the old clockmaker.
10. "He can crack nuts with his jaw," he said.
11. Clara took the nutcracker in her arms.
12. She found a small nut in a bowl.
13. Clara put the nut in the nutcracker's jaw.
14. The nutcracker cracked the nut.
15. "Be careful with his jaw," said the old clockmaker.
16. Clara laughed and held the nutcracker close.
17. But her brother wanted to hold the nutcracker too.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The old clockmaker opens his box of many small toys for the guests, then turns to Clara.
5-10: He gives her a small wooden man with a big jaw and a red coat, a nutcracker that can crack nuts with his jaw.
11-14: Clara takes the nutcracker, finds a small nut in a bowl, puts it in his jaw, and the nutcracker cracks it.
15-17: The old clockmaker warns her to be careful with the jaw; Clara laughs and holds the nutcracker close, but her brother wants to hold it too.

The Nutcracker Is Broken
1. Clara gave the nutcracker to her brother.
2. "Be careful with him," said Clara.
3. He held the nutcracker in his arms.
4. Her brother found a big nut in the bowl.
5. The nut was too big for the jaw.
6. "This nut is too hard for him," said Clara.
7. But her brother put the nut in the jaw.
8. He pushed hard on the jaw.
9. The nutcracker's jaw broke.
10. Clara cried and took the nutcracker back.
11. "You broke my nutcracker!" said Clara.
12. "I am sorry," said her brother.
13. Clara's mother told him to be careful next time.
14. Clara found a ribbon.
15. She wrapped it around the nutcracker's jaw.
16. Clara put the nutcracker in a small wooden bed.
17. When the guests went home, Clara's mother said it was time for bed.
18. Clara went to her room, but she looked back at him.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Clara gives the nutcracker to her brother and warns him to be careful, and he finds a big nut in the bowl, too big for the jaw.
6-9: Clara says the nut is too hard for him, but her brother forces it in and pushes hard, and the nutcracker's jaw breaks.
10-13: Clara cries and takes the nutcracker back, her brother says he is sorry, and their mother tells him to be careful next time.
14-18: Clara wraps a ribbon around the broken jaw, puts the nutcracker in a small wooden bed, and goes to her room when the guests leave, looking back at him.

Midnight
1. Night came, and the house was dark.
2. Clara's parents and her brother were in bed.
3. But Clara could not sleep at all.
4. She wanted to look at the nutcracker's broken jaw.
5. Clara went down to the tree in the dark.
6. She found him in his small bed.
7. A clock struck midnight.
8. Mice came from under the floor and the door.
9. The floor was dark with mice.
10. One mouse had a small crown.
11. He was the mouse king.
12. The mouse king came behind his mice.
13. Clara went behind a chair in the dark.
14. The nutcracker grew as big as Clara.
15. His small bed fell to the floor.
16. The ribbon was still on his broken jaw.
17. All the mice grew big too.
18. The nutcracker stood up.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Night comes and the house is dark, with everyone in bed, but Clara cannot sleep, so she goes down to the tree in the dark and finds the nutcracker in his small bed.
7-11: A clock strikes midnight, and mice come from under the floor and the door until the floor is dark with them, and one mouse with a small crown is the mouse king.
12-13: The mouse king comes behind his mice, and Clara goes behind a chair in the dark.
14-18: The nutcracker grows as big as Clara, his small bed falls to the floor, the ribbon still on his broken jaw; all the mice grow big too, and the nutcracker stands up.

The Mouse King's Army
1. Clara still wore her slippers.
2. The toy soldiers stood by the tree.
3. They had grown big too.
4. "Here come the mice!" said the nutcracker.
5. The mice ran at the soldiers.
6. The wooden soldiers fell down hard.
7. Many mice fell down too.
8. The mice ran away.
9. The mouse king did not run.
10. He fought the nutcracker.
11. The mouse king pushed him back.
12. The nutcracker fell to the floor.
13. Clara watched from behind the chair.
14. The mouse king stood over the nutcracker.
15. The nutcracker could not get up.
16. Clara was afraid for him.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Clara still wears her slippers, and the toy soldiers by the tree have grown big too, as the nutcracker calls out that the mice are coming.
5-8: The mice run at the soldiers; the wooden soldiers fall down hard, many mice fall down too, and the mice run away.
9-12: But the mouse king does not run: he fights the nutcracker, pushes him back, and the nutcracker falls to the floor.
13-16: Clara watches from behind the chair as the mouse king stands over the nutcracker, who cannot get up, and she is afraid for him.

Clara's Slipper
1. Clara ran to the nutcracker.
2. She looked down at her slippers.
3. Clara took off one slipper.
4. She threw the slipper at the mouse king.
5. The slipper struck the mouse king.
6. The mouse king fell down.
7. All the mice ran away.
8. The mouse king ran away too.
9. The nutcracker stood.
10. Clara was not afraid now.
11. He looked at Clara.
12. The soldiers went back to the tree.
13. Then the nutcracker turned into a young man.
14. He still wore his red coat.
15. "Thank you for saving me," he said.
16. The young man had kind eyes.
17. She said nothing.
18. He said he had a story to tell.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Clara runs to the nutcracker, looks down at her slippers, takes one off, and throws it at the mouse king, and it hits him, and he falls down.
7-12: All the mice run away, and the mouse king runs away too, so the nutcracker stands, and Clara is not afraid now; he looks at Clara, and the soldiers go back to the tree.
13-18: Then the nutcracker turns into a young man, still wearing his red coat, and he thanks Clara for saving him; the young man has kind eyes, and she says nothing, so he tells her he has a story to tell.

The Nutcracker's Tale
1. The young man told Clara a story.
2. "My uncle is your godfather," he said.
3. "A king had a beautiful princess."
4. "One night, mice ate the king's cake."
5. "The king killed the mouse queen's mice."
6. "The mouse queen swore revenge on the king."
7. "She cursed the princess to be ugly."
8. "But a hard nut could break the curse."
9. "My uncle took me to the princess to crack it."
10. "I cracked the hard nut and gave it to the princess."
11. "The princess ate it and was beautiful again."
12. "But I had to step back, and I stepped on the mouse queen."
13. "The mouse queen died and cursed me."
14. "I turned into a small nutcracker with a big jaw."
15. "My uncle could not break the curse."
16. "Her son, the young mouse king, swore revenge on me."
17. "His mice came for me at midnight."
18. "But you broke the curse," said the young man.

Paragraphs:
1-3: The young man tells Clara a story: his uncle is her godfather, he says, and a king had a beautiful princess.
4-8: One night mice ate the king's cake, so the king killed the mouse queen's mice, and the mouse queen swore revenge on the king and cursed the princess to be ugly, but a hard nut could break the curse.
9-11: His uncle took him to the princess to crack it, and he cracked the nut and gave it to the princess, and she ate it and was beautiful again.
12-15: But he had to step back and stepped on the mouse queen, who died and cursed him, so he turned into a small nutcracker with a big jaw, and his uncle could not break the curse.
16-18: Her son, the young mouse king, swore revenge on him, and his mice came for him at midnight, but Clara, he says, broke the curse.

Through the Sleeve
1. The young man held out his hand to Clara.
2. "Come and see my home," he said.
3. Clara wanted to see his home.
4. The toy soldiers were small again by the tree.
5. He took her to a coat on a hook.
6. "Climb into the sleeve," said the young man.
7. Clara climbed into the sleeve.
8. He climbed in too.
9. Clara could not see him in the dark.
10. She held his hand and did not look back.
11. The sleeve turned into a passage.
12. They went down and down the passage.
13. The passage grew warm.
14. Clara saw a light.
15. The light came from a doorway.
16. The doorway was made of candy.
17. The young man smiled, and Clara smiled back.
18. Beyond the doorway was the candy kingdom.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The young man holds out his hand and asks Clara to come and see his home, and she wants to see it; the toy soldiers are small again by the tree as he takes her to a coat on a hook.
6-10: He tells her to climb into the sleeve, and Clara climbs in, and he climbs in too; she cannot see him in the dark, so she holds his hand and does not look back.
11-13: The sleeve turns into a passage, and they go down and down the passage, which grows warm.
14-18: Clara sees a light coming from a doorway made of candy, and the young man smiles at her and she smiles back, for beyond the doorway is the candy kingdom.

The Candy Kingdom
1. Clara and the young man went through the doorway.
2. They stood still in the candy kingdom.
3. Trees of chocolate grew all around them.
4. A river of cream ran by the trees.
5. They went by the river.
6. Clara took a chocolate from a tree and ate it.
7. This kingdom was the young man's home.
8. He was glad to be back.
9. He still knew every tree by the river.
10. They saw a great palace.
11. The palace was made of cookies and cake.
12. Clara wanted to look inside.
13. They went to the palace.
14. "This palace is wonderful," said Clara.
15. The young man smiled at her.

The Palace of Sweets
1. Clara and the young man went into the palace.
2. Many people stood inside.
3. Music played through the palace.
4. The people welcomed Clara and the young man.
5. They made a great feast for them.
6. The feast had fruit, cookies, and cake.
7. Clara ate the fruit and the cookies.
8. "Thank you all," said the young man.
9. "Clara saved us from the mouse king," he said.
10. The people were glad for Clara.
11. Many people danced.
12. The young man danced with Clara.
13. Clara smiled at the people.
14. The feast went on all night.
15. But Clara grew tired.
16. She wanted to go home now.

The Journey Home
1. "It is time to go home," said the young man.
2. Clara was too tired to go far.
3. The young man took her back to the doorway.
4. They went into the passage again.
5. The candy doorway closed behind them.
6. The passage was dark now.
7. His red coat was warm in the dark.
8. Clara held the young man's hand.
9. Her eyes grew heavy.
10. She could not hold them open.
11. The passage turned into the sleeve again.
12. Clara was sleepy.
13. She closed her eyes.
14. Clara did not remember climbing out.
15. The young man's voice grew far away.
16. It grew quiet around her.
17. It grew dark.

The Nephew's Visit
1. That day, the old clockmaker came back.
2. His young nephew came with him.
3. They stood at the door.
4. "Come in and get warm," said Clara's mother.
5. Clara's brother liked the young nephew.
6. Clara looked at the nephew.
7. He bowed to Clara.
8. His eyes were kind.
9. The nephew smiled at Clara.
10. Clara knew that smile from somewhere.
11. It looked like the young man's from that night.
12. She held her small nutcracker close.
13. He looked at the nutcracker in her arms.
14. "A fine nutcracker," he said.
15. Clara smiled and said nothing.
16. The old clockmaker watched them.
17. Somewhere a clock struck in the quiet house.
