The Odyssey

Ithaca Waits / The Lotus Land / The Cave of the Cyclops
1. The long war at Troy was over.
2. The kings of Greece sailed home over the sea.
3. But one king did not come home.
4. His name was Odysseus, and his home was Ithaca.
5. In Ithaca his wife Penelope waited for him.
6. Penelope: My king has been gone for many years.
7. Telemachus: Now I am a man, but my father is gone.
8. Bad men came to the hall of Odysseus.
9. Suitor: Odysseus is dead, Penelope.
10. Suitor: Take one of us for your king today.
11. Penelope: I will not, while my king may live.
12. Suitor: Then we will eat your food and drink your wine.
13. The bad men ate the food and drank the wine every day.
14. Telemachus: I will sail out and look for the king.
15. Athena, a goddess, came down to Telemachus.
16. Athena: Be brave, for your father is not dead.
17. Athena: Odysseus wants to come home over the sea.
18. Odysseus had left Troy with his men.
19. Twelve ships sailed with him over the sea.
20. A storm blew the ships to a far land.
21. Odysseus: Go and see what men live here.
22. Three men walked in and met a kind people.
23. These people ate only the fruit of the lotus.
24. Lotus Man: Eat this sweet fruit and stay with us.
25. The three men ate the lotus and forgot their home.
26. Odysseus: Why do my men not come back?
27. Odysseus walked in and found them with the lotus.
28. Lost Man: I will not go from here.
29. Odysseus: This sweet fruit made you forget your home.
30. Odysseus: You forget your wife over the sea.
31. Odysseus pulled the three men back to the ship.
32. Odysseus: Row hard, men, and leave this land.
33. The ships sailed on over the sea.
34. Next the ships came to an island.
35. Odysseus: A great cave is up on the land.
36. Odysseus: Take some men and some wine.
37. Odysseus went up to the cave with his men.
38. In the cave they found food to eat.
39. Man: Take this food and go to the ship.
40. Odysseus: No, I want to see the one who lives here.
41. The cave was the home of a giant.
42. Men called the giant a Cyclops.
43. The Cyclops had one eye and ate men.
44. He came home with his sheep.
45. He put a great stone over the cave.
46. Cyclops: Who are you, and why are you here?
47. Odysseus: We are men of Greece, on our way home.
48. Odysseus: Be kind to us, as the gods say.
49. Cyclops: I want nothing from you and your gods.
50. The Cyclops took two men and ate them.
51. Cyclops: Sleep now, and I will eat more men.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The war at Troy is over and the Greek kings sail home, but King Odysseus does not, so his wife Penelope and son Telemachus grieve for him in Ithaca.
8-12: Bad men crowd the hall, and a suitor tells Penelope her husband is dead and she must take a new king, but she refuses while he may live.
13-17: The bad men waste the king's food, so Telemachus sets out to look for his father, and the goddess Athena tells the boy that Odysseus still lives.
18-23: Odysseus sailed from Troy with twelve ships, and a storm drove them to a far land, where he found a kind people who eat only the lotus fruit.
24-28: Three of the men taste the sweet lotus fruit and forget their home, and when Odysseus finds them they beg to stay and eat the fruit forever.
29-33: Odysseus reminds the men of their home and wife, then pulls them back to the ship and sails away over the sea.
34-40: The ships reach an island, and Odysseus takes some men and wine up to a great cave, ignoring a warning to grab food and run to the ship.
41-45: The cave is the home of a one-eyed giant called a Cyclops, who comes back with his sheep and seals the cave with a great stone.
46-51: Odysseus asks the giant to be kind, but the Cyclops cares nothing for the gods, eats two men, and says he will eat more, while the trapped men cannot get out.

The Name Nobody / Under the Rams / The Bag of Winds
1. The Cyclops ate two more of the men.
2. He took his sheep out of the cave.
3. Odysseus: We cannot move that great stone.
4. Odysseus: But I will hurt this giant and get away.
5. Odysseus and his men made a great pole with a sharp end.
6. At night the Cyclops came to the cave.
7. Odysseus: But I will give him wine to drink.
8. Odysseus: Here, take this wine and drink.
9. Cyclops: Give me more of this wine.
10. Odysseus: Here is more wine, great Cyclops.
11. Cyclops: Tell me your name, man.
12. Odysseus: My name is Nobody.
13. Cyclops: Then Nobody is the last man I will eat.
14. The giant drank more wine and fell to sleep.
15. Odysseus and his men made the pole hot in the cave.
16. They put the sharp end into his one eye.
17. The Cyclops woke and called out.
18. Cyclops: Nobody has put out my eye!
19. Giant: If nobody hurt you, why call us here?
20. The other giants went away and gave no help.
21. The Cyclops could not see, and sat in the cave door.
22. Cyclops: No man will get out now.
23. Odysseus looked at the sheep in the cave.
24. Odysseus: We will go out under the sheep.
25. He tied his men under the sheep.
26. At day the sheep walked out to eat.
27. The Cyclops put his hand on each sheep, not the men.
28. One by one the men came out.
29. Odysseus: Run to the ship with the sheep.
30. They rowed out to sea with the sheep.
31. Odysseus: You ate my men, Cyclops!
32. The giant threw a great rock at the sea.
33. Cyclops: Tell me the name of the man who did this.
34. Odysseus: Odysseus of Ithaca put out your eye.
35. Cyclops: Poseidon, keep this man from his home.
36. Poseidon, the god of the sea, heard him.
37. From that day the sea god kept Odysseus from home.
38. The ships came to the island of Aeolus.
39. Aeolus was the god of the winds.
40. Aeolus: I will help you get home, king.
41. He put all the winds into a great bag.
42. Aeolus: Do not open this bag, and one wind will take you home.
43. For nine days the ship went home over the sea.
44. Odysseus held the bag and did not sleep.
45. On the last day they came near home.
46. Then Odysseus fell to sleep.
47. Man: What is in that great bag?
48. Man: There is gold from Troy in it.
49. Man: Let us open it and take the gold.
50. The men opened the great bag.
51. All the winds came out.
52. A wind blew the ship away from home.
53. Odysseus woke and saw home go away.
54. Odysseus: My own men have done this to us.
55. The wind blew them back to Aeolus.
56. Aeolus: The gods are not with you, so go.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The Cyclops eats two more men, so Odysseus makes a great pole with a sharp end and waits for the giant to come back to the cave at night.
7-12: Odysseus gives the giant wine and tells him his name is Nobody, and the giant drinks more and falls to sleep.
13-20: The men heat the pole and drive it into the giant's one eye, and when the Cyclops calls out that Nobody hurt him, the other giants think no one is there and go away.
21-25: The blind Cyclops guards the door so no man can get out, but Odysseus ties his men under the giant's sheep.
26-31: At dawn the sheep walk out and the Cyclops feels only their backs, so the men come out, drive the sheep aboard, and row out to sea.
32-37: The Cyclops hurls a great rock, then asks the name of the man who blinded him, and when Odysseus tells him, the giant begs Poseidon to keep the man from home.
38-42: The ships reach the island of Aeolus, god of the winds, who puts every wind into a great bag so one wind can take Odysseus home.
43-49: For nine days the ship goes toward home, but as the tired king sleeps his men think gold is in the bag and decide to open it.
50-56: The winds burst out and blow the ship far from home, and Aeolus, seeing the gods are against Odysseus, tells him to go.

The Witch Circe / The Song of the Sirens / Between Two Deaths
1. Next the ships came to the island of Circe.
2. Circe had magic and lived in a house of stone.
3. Odysseus: Go and see this land.
4. His men came to the house of Circe.
5. Circe: Come in, men, and eat with me.
6. She gave them food with magic in it.
7. Circe put out her hand, and the men became pigs.
8. Circe: Now you are pigs, and here you will stay.
9. One man ran back to tell Odysseus.
10. Man: Circe has made our men into pigs.
11. Odysseus: I will go and get my men back.
12. The god Hermes came down to him.
13. Hermes: Eat this plant, and her magic cannot hurt you.
14. Odysseus ate the plant and came to the house.
15. Circe put out her hand, but he did not become a pig.
16. Odysseus: Make my men men again, Circe.
17. Circe: You must be Odysseus, so I will free your men.
18. She gave the pigs her hand, and they became men.
19. Odysseus and his men were with Circe for a year.
20. When the year was over, the men wanted home.
21. Odysseus: Circe, how do we get home from here?
22. Circe: You will pass the rocks of the Sirens.
23. Circe: Their song makes men die on the rocks.
24. Circe: Put soft wax in the ears of your men.
25. Circe: If you want to hear, be tied to the mast.
26. Odysseus put wax in the ears of his men.
27. Odysseus: Tie me to the mast and do not set me free.
28. The men tied him and rowed hard.
29. Then the song of the Sirens came over the sea.
30. Siren: Come to us, Odysseus, and hear our song.
31. Odysseus wanted the song so much that he cried out.
32. Odysseus: Set me free now!
33. But his men rowed on and held Odysseus down.
34. At last the ship passed the rocks.
35. The men took the wax out and set Odysseus free.
36. Circe had told them of Scylla and Charybdis.
37. Circe: Scylla lives in the high rocks.
38. Circe: She has six heads, and each takes a man.
39. Circe: Charybdis pulls the sea and ships down.
40. Odysseus: How can we live between the two?
41. Circe: Go near Scylla, and lose six men, not all.
42. The ship came to the sea between them.
43. Odysseus: Keep away from Charybdis, and row hard.
44. He did not tell his men of Scylla in the rocks.
45. As they came near, Scylla put down her heads.
46. Her six heads took six men from the ship.
47. Man: Odysseus, help us!
48. Odysseus: I could do nothing for them.
49. It was a hard thing for Odysseus to see.
50. But the ship came away, and the men still lived.
51. Odysseus: Row on, for we are not all dead.

Paragraphs:
1-8: The ships reach the island of Circe, who has magic, and she gives the men food that turns them into pigs, then pens them by her house.
9-14: One man warns Odysseus, and as he goes to get his men back, the god Hermes gives him a plant so that Circe's magic cannot hurt him.
15-19: Circe's magic fails on Odysseus, so she frees his men, turns the pigs back, and the crew stays with her for a year.
20-25: After a year the men want home, and Circe warns of the Sirens, whose song makes men die on the rocks, telling Odysseus to use wax and the mast.
26-30: Odysseus stops his men's ears with wax and has them tie him to the mast, and soon the song of the Sirens comes over the sea.
31-35: Odysseus wants the song so much that he cries to be free, but his men row on until the ship is past, then they set him free.
36-41: Circe warns of Scylla, who has six heads in the high rocks, and Charybdis, who pulls whole ships down, and she tells Odysseus to go near Scylla.
42-46: Odysseus steers away from Charybdis but hides the truth about Scylla, and as they pass she puts down her heads and takes six men.
47-51: The six men call to Odysseus, but he can do nothing to save them, and the rest of the ship comes away alive.

The Cattle of the Sun / The Island of Calypso / The Kind Phaeacians
1. Next the ship came to the island of the Sun.
2. On the land were the cattle of the sun god.
3. Odysseus: Circe told us not to touch these cattle.
4. Odysseus: If we do, we will not see home.
5. Odysseus: Eat only the food we have on the ship.
6. But a storm kept the ship on the land for days.
7. Soon all the food on the ship was gone.
8. The men looked at the cattle of the sun god.
9. Man: We will die here if we do not eat.
10. While Odysseus slept, the men killed the cattle.
11. They made a fire and ate the cattle.
12. Odysseus woke and saw the fire.
13. Odysseus: What have you done, men?
14. Odysseus: The sun god will make us pay.
15. The sun god saw his cattle dead and called to Zeus.
16. Sun God: Make these men pay for this.
17. Zeus: I will break their ship in the sea.
18. When the storm went, the ship sailed out.
19. Zeus threw down fire and broke the ship.
20. All the men died but Odysseus.
21. Odysseus held to the mast on the sea for nine days.
22. The sea took Odysseus to an island.
23. On the island lived Calypso.
24. Calypso saw Odysseus on her land.
25. She was good to him in her home by the sea.
26. Calypso: Stay here with me for all your days.
27. Calypso: I will make you a god, and we will live here.
28. Odysseus: But my home is Ithaca, with my wife.
29. For long years Calypso kept him here.
30. Each day Odysseus went to the sea.
31. Then Athena went to Zeus.
32. Athena: Zeus, why must Odysseus stay here so long?
33. Athena: Send Hermes, and let this man go home.
34. Hermes came down to the island of Calypso.
35. Hermes: Zeus says you must let this man go home.
36. Calypso: I cannot say no to Zeus.
37. Calypso: Make a boat, and go home over the sea.
38. Odysseus cut down trees and made a boat.
39. He went away over the sea to his home.
40. But Poseidon saw Odysseus on the sea.
41. Poseidon: This man will not get home so soon.
42. He sent a great storm down on Odysseus.
43. Odysseus was many days in the sea.
44. At last he came to a land.
45. This was the land of the Phaeacians.
46. A woman came to Odysseus by the sea.
47. Woman: Come and see my father the king.
48. The king of the Phaeacians was good to him.
49. King: Come and eat with us, Odysseus.
50. King: Tell us your name and your land.
51. Odysseus: I am Odysseus, who was at Troy.
52. Odysseus told the king of the Cyclops and Circe.
53. Odysseus: I have lost my ships and all my men.
54. King: We will give you a ship to take you home.
55. The Phaeacians put gold on the ship for him.
56. Odysseus slept as the ship went over the sea.
57. At day the ship came to Ithaca.
58. The men put Odysseus down on his land.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The ship reaches the island of the Sun, and Odysseus, remembering Circe's warning, tells his men not to touch the sun god's cattle.
6-9: A storm keeps them on the land until the food is gone, and the hungry, frightened men decide it is better to eat the cattle than to die.
10-14: While Odysseus sleeps the men kill and cook the cattle, and when he wakes he is angry and says the sun god will make them pay.
15-21: The sun god calls on Zeus, who breaks the ship with fire, so all the men die but Odysseus, who holds to the mast for nine days.
22-25: The sea carries Odysseus to an island, where the goddess Calypso finds him on her shore, takes him in, and is good to him.
26-30: Calypso offers to make him a god if he will stay forever, but his home is Ithaca and his wife, and for years he longs for the sea road home.
31-35: Athena begs Zeus to help, so Zeus sends Hermes to tell Calypso she must let the man go home.
36-39: Calypso cannot say no to Zeus, so she helps Odysseus make a boat, and he sails away over the sea toward home.
40-45: Poseidon sends a storm down on Odysseus, but he comes at last to the land of the kind Phaeacians.
46-51: A woman brings Odysseus to her father the king, who welcomes him to eat, and Odysseus names himself and tells how the sea has kept him from home.
52-54: Odysseus tells the king of the Cyclops and Circe and of his lost men, and the king, moved by so hard a tale, gives him a ship home.
55-58: The Phaeacians load the ship with gold and carry the sleeping king to Ithaca, setting him down on his own land.

Home in Disguise / The Great Bow / The Fall of the Suitors / The Secret of the Bed
1. Odysseus woke on a land he did not know.
2. Athena came to him as a man.
3. Athena: You are home now, in Ithaca.
4. Odysseus: Then I must take care, for I have no men.
5. Athena: The bad men are in your hall and want your wife.
6. Athena: I will make you an old man.
7. Odysseus became an old man.
8. Odysseus went to the home of his pig man.
9. The pig man did not know his own king.
10. Pig Man: Come in, old man, and eat with me.
11. Pig Man: My king Odysseus is lost on the sea.
12. Odysseus: Your king is near, I tell you.
13. That day Telemachus came home from the sea.
14. Athena made Odysseus the king again.
15. Telemachus: Are you a god come to me?
16. Odysseus: No, I am your father, home again.
17. Telemachus: Father, I have waited so long for you!
18. They held each other and cried.
19. Odysseus: Tell no one that the king is home.
20. Odysseus: We will send the bad men from my hall.
21. Penelope came to the hall with the bad men.
22. Penelope: The man who wins my game will be king.
23. Penelope: Here is the great bow of Odysseus.
24. Penelope: Bend it and shoot an arrow through twelve rings.
25. She set twelve rings up in a row.
26. One by one the bad men came to bend the bow.
27. Not one of them was strong to bend it.
28. The old man came up to the bow.
29. Odysseus: Let me try the great bow.
30. Suitor: Sit down, old man, this bow is not for you.
31. Telemachus: Give the man the bow, and let him try.
32. Odysseus took the bow and looked at it.
33. He bent the bow and put the string on.
34. The bad men grew still and looked on.
35. Odysseus put an arrow to the bow.
36. The arrow went through all twelve rings.
37. Telemachus took his spear and stood by Odysseus.
38. Odysseus put down his old-man look and stood up.
39. Odysseus: You said the king would never come home.
40. Odysseus: I am Odysseus, and I am here.
41. The bad men got up and looked for the door.
42. Suitor: Put down the bow, Odysseus!
43. Suitor: We will pay for all we ate!
44. Odysseus: You ate my food and wanted my wife.
45. Odysseus: Now no one of you will get out.
46. Telemachus shut the doors of the hall.
47. Odysseus put his arrows into the bad men.
48. Telemachus and the pig man stood by the king.
49. Suitor: The gods are with this man!
50. One by one the bad men fell.
51. Soon all the bad men were dead.
52. Old Nurse: My king, are you home at last?
53. Odysseus: I am home again, good nurse.
54. Athena stood by Odysseus.
55. Athena: The bad men are dead, and your home is your own.
56. Odysseus: One thing is left for me now.
57. Odysseus: I must go up to my wife Penelope.
58. Penelope came to the hall.
59. She looked at the man who said he was her king.
60. Penelope: They tell me the king has come home.
61. Penelope: But it has been so long, and I do not know you.
62. Odysseus: Look at me, Penelope, I am your king.
63. Penelope: I will not take a man I do not know.
64. Penelope: Move our bed out of the room for this man.
65. Odysseus: No man can move that bed, and you know why.
66. Odysseus: I made the bed from a tree in the ground.
67. Then Penelope knew this man was her own king.
68. She came to Odysseus and held him.
69. Penelope: Now I know you are my king.
70. Odysseus: And I know you are my own wife.
71. The king and his wife told of their long years.
72. Athena kept back the day to give them more night.
73. Odysseus was home in Ithaca, and his long way was over.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Odysseus wakes on his own land, where Athena tells him he is home in Ithaca and makes him look like an old man.
8-12: Odysseus goes to the home of his pig man, who does not know his king and speaks sadly of his lost master, while Odysseus hints the king is near.
13-17: Telemachus comes home, and Athena makes Odysseus the king again, so father and son hold each other and cry.
18-20: Odysseus tells his son to keep the secret, so together they can send the bad men from the hall.
21-27: Penelope sets a game to find a husband: whoever can bend the king's great bow and shoot an arrow through twelve rings wins her, but no bad man is strong enough.
28-33: The old man asks to try, and though a suitor mocks him, Telemachus gives him the bow, which Odysseus bends and strings with ease.
34-36: The bad men grow still as Odysseus shoots one arrow through all twelve rings, a thing no other man could do.
37-40: Telemachus takes up his spear, and Odysseus puts down his old-man look and names himself to the men who said the king would never return.
41-45: The bad men panic and offer to pay, but Odysseus refuses, for they ate his food and wanted his wife, and now none of them will get out.
46-51: Telemachus shuts the doors, and with the pig man beside him Odysseus shoots the bad men down until every one is dead and their long years are over.
52-57: The old nurse knows her king, and Athena praises him, but Odysseus says one thing is left: to go up to his wife.
58-63: Penelope comes to the hall but is careful, unsure after so long, and Odysseus tells her he is the king she has waited for.
64-67: To test him, Penelope asks to move their bed, but Odysseus says no man can move it, for he made it from a living tree rooted in the ground, and by this she knows her king.
68-71: Penelope comes to Odysseus and holds him, and each knows the other at last.
72-73: The king and his wife share their long tales while Athena holds back the day, and Odysseus is home in Ithaca at last.
