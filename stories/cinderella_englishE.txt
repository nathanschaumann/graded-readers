Cinderella

A New Stepmother
1. A kind man lived in a big house.
2. The king and his young prince lived in the kingdom's palace.
3. The man's wife died when their daughter was young.
4. The girl was kind too.
5. The man loved his daughter.
6. But he missed his wife.
7. He married a proud woman.
8. The woman had two daughters of her own.
9. Her daughters were proud too.
10. The girl now had two stepsisters.
11. The family moved to the big house.
12. The man hoped his daughter had sisters.
13. But the stepmother did not love the girl.
14. She loved only her own daughters.
15. The stepmother gave her daughters the best rooms.

Paragraphs:
1-7: A kind man lives in a big house with his young daughter, near the palace where the king and his young prince live, and after his wife dies, he marries a proud woman who has two daughters of her own.
8-15: The girl now has two stepsisters, and the proud family moves into the big house, but though the man hopes his daughter has sisters, the stepmother loves only her own daughters and gives them the best rooms in the house.

A Hard New Life
1. The kind man died.
2. The girl lived on, alone in the big house.
3. She had no one to love her.
4. The girl now lived in the cold attic.
5. The stepsisters had the two best rooms.
6. She worked in the cold kitchen every day.
7. She slept by the fireplace.
8. She had cinders on her clothes.
9. The family did not call her a daughter.
10. The stepsisters called her Cinderella.
11. But Cinderella stayed as kind as before.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The kind man dies, and she lives on, alone in the big house with no one to love her, so she now lives in the cold attic while the stepsisters have the best rooms.
6-11: The girl works in the cold kitchen every day and sleeps by the fireplace with cinders on her clothes; the family does not call her a daughter, so the stepsisters call her Cinderella, and she stays as kind as before.

Friends in the Kitchen
1. Cinderella worked every day in the kitchen.
2. Mice lived in the kitchen.
3. Birds came to the kitchen every day.
4. Cinderella was kind to the mice and birds.
5. The mice and birds loved Cinderella.
6. They sang and stayed near her.
7. The stepsisters sat before a mirror.
8. They looked at their own faces.
9. The stepsisters called Cinderella very ugly.
10. But Cinderella did not answer them.
11. She worked on and stayed kind.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Cinderella works every day in the kitchen, where mice and birds live and visit, and she is kind to them, so they love her and sing and stay near her.
7-11: The stepsisters sit before a mirror and look at their own faces, and though they call Cinderella ugly, she does not answer and simply works on, staying kind.

News from the Palace
1. The kind king wanted the prince to marry one day.
2. The prince would find a wife at a royal ball.
3. The king's news went to every house in the kingdom.
4. The king's news came to Cinderella's big house too.
5. Every young woman would go to the ball.
6. The stepsisters and their family were very proud.
7. They wanted new gowns for the ball.
8. The girl wanted to go to the ball too.
9. Cinderella hoped for the stepmother's answer.
10. "May I go to the ball?" she asked.
11. The stepmother did not answer her.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The king wants the prince to marry and find a wife at a ball, so news of it goes to every house in the kingdom, and an invitation comes to Cinderella's house too, for every young woman may attend.
6-11: The stepsisters are very proud and want new gowns for the ball, and the girl wants to go too, hoping for the stepmother's answer, but when she asks if she may go, the stepmother does not answer her.

Getting Ready for the Ball
1. The stepsisters wanted the best gowns.
2. Cinderella had to sew their gowns.
3. She had to comb their hair too.
4. No one gave Cinderella a new gown.
5. Cinderella's room was in the cold attic.
6. The stepsisters looked in the mirror.
7. They wanted to look pretty for the ball.
8. Cinderella wanted to look pretty too.
9. "May I go to the ball too?" she asked again.
10. The stepmother looked at Cinderella.
11. "You have too much to do," said the stepmother.
12. Cinderella stopped asking.
13. She said nothing and worked on.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The stepsisters want the best gowns, so Cinderella must sew them and comb their hair too, though no one gives her a new gown of her own and her room is in the cold attic, while the stepsisters look in the mirror and want to look pretty for the ball.
8-13: Cinderella wants to look pretty too, and asks again if she may go to the ball, but the stepmother looks at her and says she has too much to do, so Cinderella stops asking and says nothing, working on.

Left Behind
1. The day of the ball came.
2. The stepsisters wore their new gowns.
3. "May I go to the ball now?" Cinderella asked.
4. "You have no gown to wear," said the stepmother.
5. Cinderella cried.
6. She missed her father very much.
7. The stepmother and stepsisters left in a coach.
8. Cinderella saw them go.
9. She was alone in the big house.
10. She sat by the fireplace and cried again.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The day of the ball comes, and the stepsisters wear their new gowns, but when Cinderella asks to go too, the stepmother says she has no gown to wear, so Cinderella cries and misses her father very much.
7-10: The stepmother and stepsisters leave in a coach, and Cinderella sees them go, alone in the big house, where she sits by the fireplace and cries again.

The Fairy Godmother
1. Cinderella sat by the fireplace again.
2. The stepmother had left her alone.
3. Then a fairy godmother came before her.
4. "Why do you cry?" the godmother asked.
5. Cinderella told her of the ball.
6. "I will help you go to the ball," said the godmother.
7. The godmother waved her wand.
8. Cinderella stopped and smiled.
9. She thanked the kind fairy godmother.
10. The godmother smiled at Cinderella too.
11. "The magic will begin now," said the godmother.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Cinderella sits by the fireplace again, left alone, when a fairy godmother appears before her and asks why she cries; Cinderella tells her of the ball, and the godmother promises to help her go.
7-11: The godmother waves her wand, and Cinderella stops crying to smile and thank her; the godmother smiles back and says the magic will begin now.

Magic in the Garden
1. Cinderella found a big pumpkin in the garden.
2. The kind girl thanked her again.
3. The godmother waved her wand at the pumpkin.
4. The pumpkin became a coach.
5. Cinderella found mice near the house.
6. The godmother waved her wand at the mice.
7. The mice became horses.
8. Then the godmother waved her wand at Cinderella.
9. Cinderella's old clothes became a gown.
10. She had glass slippers on her feet.
11. "This magic will end at midnight," said the godmother.
12. "You must come home before midnight," she said.
13. Cinderella promised to remember.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Cinderella finds a big pumpkin in the garden and thanks the godmother again; the godmother waves her wand at the pumpkin so it becomes a coach, then waves her wand at mice found near the house so they become horses.
8-13: The godmother waves her wand at Cinderella, whose old clothes become a gown, with glass slippers on her feet, and the godmother warns that the magic will end at midnight and she must come home before then, which Cinderella promises to remember.

The Ball
1. Cinderella rode the coach to the palace.
2. She arrived at the palace.
3. Everyone looked at the girl in her gown.
4. No one knew who she was.
5. The prince saw Cinderella and smiled.
6. "Will you dance with me?" the prince asked.
7. Cinderella danced with the prince at night.
8. The stepsisters and stepmother did not know her.
9. Everyone at the big ball loved Cinderella.
10. The prince wanted to know her name.
11. But Cinderella did not tell him.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Cinderella rides the coach to the palace and arrives, where everyone looks at her and no one knows who she is; the prince sees her, smiles, and asks her to dance.
7-11: Cinderella dances with the prince at night, and though the stepsisters and stepmother do not know her, everyone at the ball loves her; the prince wants to know her name, but Cinderella does not tell him.

Midnight
1. The prince loved her kind smile.
2. Everyone at the ball saw them dance.
3. Midnight came.
4. The clock began to strike.
5. Cinderella remembered the godmother.
6. She ran from the palace.
7. She ran down the stairs.
8. One glass slipper fell on the stairs.
9. Cinderella did not stop to find it.
10. She ran home.
11. The prince ran too.
12. But he could not find her.
13. The prince found the glass slipper on the stairs.
14. He picked it up.

Paragraphs:
1-8: The prince loves her kind smile, and everyone at the ball sees them dance, but midnight comes, and the clock begins to strike; Cinderella remembers the godmother's warning and runs from the palace, down the stairs, though one glass slipper falls on the stairs.
9-14: Cinderella does not stop to find it and runs home; the prince runs too but cannot find her, though he finds the glass slipper on the stairs and picks it up.

Home Again
1. Cinderella ran home before midnight.
2. Her gown became old clothes again.
3. The coach became a pumpkin again.
4. The horses became mice again.
5. But the glass slippers did not change.
6. Cinderella still wore one slipper.
7. The stepsisters came home too.
8. They knew only of the ball.
9. "A girl danced with the prince all night," said one stepsister.
10. "No one knew her name," said the other.
11. Cinderella said nothing.
12. She hid her one glass slipper.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Cinderella runs home before midnight, and her gown becomes old clothes again, the coach becomes a pumpkin, and the horses become mice, but the glass slippers do not change, so Cinderella still wears one.
7-12: The stepsisters come home too, knowing only of the ball, saying a girl danced with the prince and no one knew her name; Cinderella says nothing, then hides her one glass slipper.

The Search Begins
1. The prince had the glass slipper.
2. "I will marry the girl who owns this slipper," said the prince.
3. The king sent the prince to find her.
4. Every young woman had to try the slipper.
5. He went from house to house in the kingdom.
6. For many days, the prince tried to find her.
7. No woman's foot fit the slipper.
8. The prince did not find her.
9. The stepsisters heard of the search.
10. They wanted to try the slipper too.
11. Soon the prince would come to every house.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The prince has the glass slipper and says he will marry the girl who owns it, so the king sends him to find her, and every young woman has to try the slipper as he goes from house to house in the kingdom for many days.
7-11: No woman's foot fits the slipper, and the prince does not find her, but the stepsisters hear of the search and want to try the slipper too, for soon the prince would come to every house.

The Slipper Comes to the House
1. One day, the prince came to Cinderella's big house.
2. He knocked on the door.
3. The proud stepmother opened the door.
4. "I look for a kind girl," said the prince.
5. The stepsisters ran to try the slipper.
6. One stepsister tried the slipper.
7. It did not fit her foot.
8. The other stepsister tried next.
9. It did not fit her either.
10. The prince looked sad.
11. "Is anyone else in the house?" the prince asked.

Paragraphs:
1-6: One day the prince comes to Cinderella's house and knocks on the door; the stepmother opens it, and the prince says he looks for a girl, so the stepsisters run to try the slipper, and one stepsister tries first.
7-11: It does not fit her foot, so the other stepsister tries next, and it does not fit her either; the prince looks sad and asks if anyone else is in the house.

Cinderella's Turn
1. "There is no one else here," said the stepmother.
2. But the prince heard Cinderella in the attic.
3. Cinderella came down from the attic.
4. The stepmother did not want this.
5. "May I try the slipper too?" Cinderella asked.
6. The prince let her try it.
7. The glass slipper fit her foot.
8. Everyone was very surprised.
9. Cinderella showed her other glass slipper.
10. It was the match.
11. The prince knew he had found her.
12. "You are the girl from the ball," he said.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The stepmother says there is no one else, but the prince hears Cinderella in the attic, and she comes down; the stepmother does not want this, but Cinderella asks to try the slipper too, and the prince lets her.
7-12: The glass slipper fits her foot, and everyone is very surprised when Cinderella shows her other glass slipper, which is the match; the prince knows he has found her and says she is the girl from the ball.

A Royal Wedding
1. Soon Cinderella married the prince.
2. The wedding was at the king's palace.
3. Cinderella wore her glass slippers again.
4. The kind fairy godmother came to see her too.
5. Everyone in the kingdom came.
6. The stepsisters and stepmother came too.
7. They were surprised to see Cinderella so happy.
8. Cinderella wanted to forgive her stepsisters.
9. She was still kind to them.
10. The kind girl was now a princess.
11. The prince and Cinderella danced all night.
12. Everyone sang and danced too.
13. The king was very happy for the prince.
14. Cinderella and the prince lived in the palace.
15. Cinderella and the prince were happy forever.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Soon Cinderella marries the prince at a wedding in the king's palace, wearing her glass slippers again while the kind fairy godmother comes to see her too; everyone in the kingdom comes, along with the stepsisters and stepmother, who are surprised to see Cinderella so happy.
8-15: She wants to forgive her stepsisters and is still kind to them, for the kind girl is now a princess; the prince and Cinderella dance all night, and everyone sings and dances too, while the king is very happy for the prince, and Cinderella and the prince live in the palace, happy forever.
