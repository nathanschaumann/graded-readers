Rumpelstiltskin

The Miller's Boast
1. A miller lived by a mill with his daughter.
2. The girl helped him every day.
3. One day, the king came to the mill.
4. The miller wanted to please the king.
5. "My daughter can spin straw into gold," he said.
6. This was not true, but he said it anyway.
7. The king wanted gold more than anything.
8. "Bring your daughter to my castle," said the king.
9. The miller brought the girl to the castle.
10. "Tonight you will spin straw into gold," said the king.

Paragraphs:
1-5: A miller and his daughter live by a mill, and when the king comes by, the miller wants to please him, so he claims his daughter can spin straw into gold.
6-10: The boast is not true, but the miller says it, and the king, who wants gold more than anything, brings the girl to his castle and orders her to prove it that night.

The First Night's Bargain
1. The king took the girl to a small room.
2. The room was full of straw.
3. "Spin this straw into gold by morning," said the king.
4. "If you cannot, you will die," he said.
5. The king locked the door and left.
6. The girl sat alone and cried.
7. Suddenly, a little man appeared in the room.
8. "What is wrong?" said the little man.
9. "I cannot spin straw into gold," said the girl.
10. "I can spin it for you," said the little man.
11. "But what will you give me?" he asked.
12. The girl gave him her necklace.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The king locks the girl in a small room full of straw, warns she will die if it is not gold by morning, and leaves her there alone to cry.
7-12: A little man appears and asks what is wrong; when the girl explains her problem, he offers to spin the straw himself if she pays him, and she gives him her necklace.

Gold by Morning
1. The little man sat at a wheel.
2. He spun the straw all through the night.
3. By morning, the straw was gold.
4. The king opened the door and looked inside.
5. He looked at all the gold.
6. But the king wanted even more gold.
7. He took the girl to a bigger room.
8. This room had even more straw.
9. "Spin this again, or you will die," said the king.
10. The king locked the door once more.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The little man spins the straw at his wheel all night, and by morning the king opens the door to find the room full of gold.
6-10: Wanting even more gold, the king moves the girl to a bigger room with even more straw, repeats his threat, and locks the door again.

The Second Night's Bargain
1. That night, the girl sat alone again.
2. The little man appeared once more.
3. "Have you come to help me again?" she asked.
4. "What will you give me this time?" he asked.
5. "I have no more gold or jewels," she said.
6. "But you can have my ring," she said.
7. The girl gave him her ring.
8. "That will do," said the little man.
9. He sat down at the wheel.
10. He spun the straw into gold.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Alone again that night, the girl is glad when the little man appears, but this time she has no more gold or jewels to offer him.
6-10: She gives him her ring instead, and he accepts it, sitting down at the wheel to begin spinning the straw into gold.

The King's Promise
1. In the morning, the king found more gold.
2. He looked at the gold.
3. This was more gold than ever before.
4. But the king still wanted more.
5. He took her to the biggest room.
6. This room was full of even more straw.
7. "Spin this into gold one more time," said the king.
8. "If you do, I will marry you," he said.
9. "If you cannot, you will still die," said the king.
10. The girl did not know what to say.
11. Then the king walked out and locked the door.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The king finds even more gold in the morning, and still wanting more, he takes the girl to the biggest room yet, full of even more straw.
7-11: He promises to marry her if she spins this straw too, but warns she will still die if she cannot, and he leaves her alone with no answer.

The Last Promise
1. That night, the girl sat alone once more.
2. The little man came again.
3. "Do you have anything left to give me?" he asked.
4. "I have nothing left to give," said the girl.
5. "Then promise me your first child," he said.
6. The girl was afraid to promise this.
7. But if she said no, she would die.
8. "I promise you my first child," she said.
9. The little man smiled and sat at the wheel.
10. He spun the last of the straw.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Alone once more, the girl tells the little man she has nothing left to pay him.
5-10: He asks for her first child instead, and though she is afraid, she knows she will die if she refuses, so she promises him her child, and he begins to spin the last of the straw.

The Queen
1. The next morning, the king found the gold.
2. "You will be my queen," said the king.
3. The king married the girl.
4. She was now the queen.
5. The queen lived in the castle with the king.
6. The king never knew about the little man.
7. Only the queen knew that secret.
8. A peaceful year passed in the kingdom.
9. The queen had a baby.
10. The king was glad to have a healthy heir.
11. The queen thought only about the baby.
12. She forgot all about her promise.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The king finds the gold, keeps his promise to marry the girl, and she becomes the queen, living in the castle with him, though only she knows the secret of the little man.
8-12: A peaceful year passes in the kingdom, the queen has a healthy baby, and thinking only about the baby, she forgets all about her promise.

The Little Man's Return
1. One night, the little man walked into the castle.
2. "You promised me your first child," he said.
3. "I have come for the baby," said the little man.
4. The queen cried and held her baby close.
5. "Please do not take my baby," she said.
6. "I will give you all my gold," said the queen.
7. "I do not want your gold," said the little man.
8. "A living child means more to me than gold."
9. The little man saw the queen's tears.
10. "I will give you a chance," said the little man.
11. "Guess my name in three days."
12. "Guess right, and keep the baby."

Paragraphs:
1-5: One night the little man walks into the castle to collect on the queen's promise, and though she cries and begs him not to take her baby, he has come for it.
6-12: She offers him all her gold, but he refuses, saying a living child means more to him, and moved by her tears, he offers her a chance: guess his name in three days and keep the baby.

Two Days of Guessing
1. That night, the little man came back.
2. "Have you guessed my name?" he asked.
3. The queen guessed the names she knew.
4. Every name she said was wrong.
5. "You have two days left," he said.
6. The next night, he came again.
7. The queen guessed many new names.
8. None of the names were right.
9. The little man laughed, sure he was safe.
10. "Tomorrow is your last chance," he said.
11. The queen sent a servant into the kingdom.
12. "Find me a strange name," she said.

Paragraphs:
1-5: That night the little man comes back and asks if the queen has guessed his name, but every name she knows is wrong, and he warns she has two days left.
6-12: The next night he returns and she guesses many new names, but none are right, so with only one day left, she sends a servant into the kingdom to find her a strange name.

Rumpelstiltskin
1. The servant walked into the woods and saw a fire.
2. The little man danced around the fire.
3. "My name is Rumpelstiltskin!" he sang.
4. The servant ran back to tell the queen.
5. He told her the strange name.
6. The little man came back for the baby.
7. "Is your name Rumpelstiltskin?" asked the queen.
8. The little man's smile was gone.
9. He stamped his foot.
10. Then he fell through the floor.
11. No one ever saw him.
12. The queen held her baby and smiled.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The servant walks into the woods and finds the little man dancing around a fire and singing his own name, so he runs back to tell the queen his strange name.
6-12: That night the little man comes for the baby, but when the queen names him Rumpelstiltskin, his smile vanishes, he stamps his foot, and falls through the floor, never to be seen again, leaving the queen to hold her baby happily.
