A Christmas Carol

Marley Is Dead
1. Marley was dead, and he had been dead for 7 years.
2. Scrooge was his only friend and his only partner.
3. Scrooge kept the name Marley on the door of the office, and he did his work there every day.
4. He gave nothing to the poor.
5. It was Christmas Eve, and the day was cold and dark.
6. Scrooge sat in his office with the door open, so that he could look at his clerk in the next room.
7. The clerk was Bob Cratchit, and his fire was small, and the fire in Scrooge's office was as small.
8. The coal was in Scrooge's office, and Bob could not take any.
9. "A merry Christmas, Uncle!" said Fred, who was Scrooge's nephew and had come in from the street.
10. "Bah, humbug!" said Scrooge.
11. "Christmas a humbug, Uncle?" said Fred.
12. "You do not mean that," Fred said.
13. "I do mean it," said Scrooge, "and what right have you to be merry, when you are so poor?"
14. "What right have you to be angry, when you are so rich?" Fred said.
15. "Bah, humbug!" said Scrooge again.
16. "Christmas is a time when you are a year older and no richer," said Scrooge.
17. "It is a time when men give to the poor, Uncle," Fred said.
18. "It has never given me any money, but it has done me good."
19. Bob made a sound in the next room, and Scrooge went to the door.
20. "Make one more sound, and you will have Christmas with no work," said Scrooge.
21. "Keep Christmas if you want, and I will keep my money," Scrooge said to Fred.
22. "But you do not keep Christmas at all, Uncle," Fred said.
23. "Come and eat dinner with us tomorrow," said Fred.
24. "Good afternoon," said Scrooge.
25. "I want nothing from you, so why can we not be friends?" Fred said.
26. "Good afternoon," said Scrooge again.
27. "But I will say a merry Christmas to you," said Fred.
28. Fred said a merry Christmas to Bob, and Bob said it back to him.
29. "There is a fool," said Scrooge, "for my clerk is poor, and he says a merry Christmas."
30. Scrooge went back to his work, and Fred went out into the street.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Marley has been dead 7 years, and his partner Scrooge still works under both names and gives nothing to anyone.
5-10: On a cold Christmas Eve, Scrooge watches Bob work until his nephew Fred comes in and says merry Christmas.
11-14: Fred and Scrooge argue over who has the right to be merry, and each says the other is wrong.
15-18: Scrooge calls Christmas a time with no gain, but Fred says it is a kind time that has done him good.
19-24: Bob makes a sound, Scrooge warns him, and Fred asks his uncle to dinner but is told only good afternoon.
25-30: Fred asks for friendship and is refused, then he and Bob wish each other a merry Christmas.

Two Men Ask for Money
1. 2 men came into the office with a paper.
2. "Is this the office of Scrooge and Marley?" said one of the men.
3. "Marley has been dead for 7 years," said Scrooge, "and he died on this night."
4. "At Christmas we must give to the poor," the man said.
5. "Many of the poor are cold, sir, and they have no fire," said the other man.
6. "Are there no prisons?" said Scrooge.
7. "There are prisons, and there are workhouses," the man said, "but the poor will not go there, and many would sooner die."
8. "Are there no workhouses?" said Scrooge again.
9. "Then let them die," said Scrooge, "and there will be fewer people in the world."
10. "What can I put down for you, sir?" the man said.
11. "Nothing," said Scrooge, "for I pay for the prisons and the workhouses, and they are enough."
12. "I wish to be alone," Scrooge said, "and I do not make merry at Christmas."
13. "But the poor are many, sir," said the man.
14. "It is not my work to give to them," said Scrooge.
15. "Good afternoon, sir," said the men, and they went out into the street.
16. A boy came to the door of the office and sang, and Scrooge took a ruler, and the boy ran away.
17. Scrooge went back to his work, and the day grew darker, and it was time to shut the office.
18. "You will want all of tomorrow," said Scrooge to Bob.
19. "If it is not a bad time for you, sir," Bob said.
20. "It is a bad time," said Scrooge, "for I pay you for a day, and you do no work."
21. "It is only once in a year, sir," said Bob.
22. "Then take it," said Scrooge, "but be here all the earlier the next morning."
23. Bob said he would, and he shut the door of the office.
24. Bob ran home in the cold, and he ran fast.
25. Scrooge shut his books and went out to eat dinner alone.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Two men come into the office and ask Scrooge to give something to the poor at Christmas.
5-9: Scrooge asks if there are no prisons or workhouses and says the poor should die if they would sooner die.
10-16: Scrooge tells the men to put him down for nothing and says it is not his work to give, and they leave.
17-20: As the day grows dark, Bob asks for Christmas Day, and Scrooge says it is not fair to pay him for no work.
21-25: Scrooge lets Bob take the day if he comes early after, and Bob runs home while Scrooge goes to eat dinner alone.

The Face on the Door
1. Scrooge ate his dinner alone in a dark room, and he looked at the papers.
2. Scrooge went home to the old house that had been Marley's.
3. The house was in a dark yard, and Scrooge put his key in the door.
4. Scrooge looked at the knocker.
5. There was no knocker on the door, and there was Marley's face.
6. The face looked at Scrooge, and the eyes were open, but they did not move.
7. Scrooge looked again, and it was a knocker.
8. "Bah!" said Scrooge, and he shut the door with a loud sound.
9. The sound went up the stairs of the house, but no one came.
10. Scrooge lit a candle and went up to his rooms.
11. Scrooge looked in every room, under the bed and behind the door, and no one was there.
12. His fire was small.
13. Scrooge shut the door, took off his coat, and sat down by the fire.
14. Scrooge looked at a bell in the room.
15. The bell moved, and it rang, and every bell in the house rang with it.
16. The bells rang, and then they stopped.
17. There was a noise in the house, and it was the sound of a chain on the floor.
18. A door opened with a loud sound, and the noise came up the stairs.
19. It came on to the door of Scrooge's room.
20. "It is a humbug," said Scrooge, "and I will not believe it."
21. The noise came through the door, and a ghost came into the room.
22. It was Marley, and he had on his old coat.
23. Around his body was a long chain, and the chain was made of money, and keys, and books, and papers.
24. Scrooge looked at the ghost, and he could see through the body to the door behind it.
25. Marley was dead, and Scrooge knew it, but he did not believe his eyes.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Scrooge eats dinner alone and goes home, and on his door he sees Marley's face where the knocker should be.
6-10: The face turns into a knocker again, and Scrooge shuts the door, lights a candle, and goes up.
11-15: Scrooge finds no one in his rooms, locks the door, and sits down to eat, and then every bell in the house begins to ring.
16-21: A chain is heard below and comes up the stairs and through the locked door, and a ghost comes into the room.
22-25: The ghost is Marley, in a chain of money boxes, keys, books, and papers, and Scrooge can see through him.

Marley Speaks
1. "What do you want with me?" said Scrooge.
2. "I want to speak with you," said the ghost.
3. "Who are you?" said Scrooge.
4. "In life I was your partner, Jacob Marley," said the ghost.
5. "You do not believe in me," said Marley.
6. "I do not," said Scrooge.
7. "Why do you not believe your eyes?" said Marley.
8. "A bad dinner can trick my eyes," said Scrooge, "and you are nothing but my dinner."
9. Marley made a loud sound and shook his chain, and Scrooge fell down.
10. "Jacob, why do you come to me?" said Scrooge.
11. "Do you believe in me or not?" said Marley.
12. "I do," said Scrooge, "I must."
13. "Why do you walk in the world?" said Scrooge.
14. "Every man must walk with other men," said Marley, "and if he did not in life, he must when he is dead."
15. "I must walk, and I cannot stop."
16. "Look at my chain," said Marley, "for I made it in my life."
17. "You have a chain too," said Marley.
18. "It was as long as this when I died, and you make it long every day."
19. Scrooge looked at the floor around him, and he saw no chain.
20. "Tell me more, Jacob," said Scrooge.
21. "I have nothing to tell you," said Marley, "for in life I never went out of the office to look at the poor."
22. "You were a good man of business, Jacob," said Scrooge.
23. "Business!" said Marley.
24. "Men were my business, and the poor were my business, and I gave them nothing."
25. "I see the poor at Christmas, and I cannot help them," said Marley.
26. "I am here to speak with you, for you have a chance."
27. "3 spirits will come to you."
28. "Is that the chance you spoke of?" said Scrooge.
29. "It is," said Marley.
30. "I would sooner not," said Scrooge.
31. "If they do not come, you will have a chain like this," said Marley.
32. "One will come tomorrow at 1, when the bell rings."
33. "One will come on the next night at 1, and one on the night after that at 12."
34. "Could they not all come at once, Jacob?" said Scrooge.
35. "You will see no more of me," said Marley.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The ghost says he is Marley, and that he has come to speak with Scrooge, who does not believe in him.
6-12: Scrooge says a bad dinner tricks his eyes, but Marley shakes his chain until Scrooge believes.
13-18: Marley explains that a dead man must walk among men, and that Scrooge has a chain as long as his own.
19-24: Scrooge sees no chain on himself and asks for more, and Marley says the poor were his business but he gave them nothing.
25-31: Marley says Scrooge has a chance to change, and that 3 spirits will come to him.
32-35: Marley says when each of the 3 spirits will come, and that Scrooge will see no more of him.

The Ghost of Christmas Past
1. Marley walked back from Scrooge to the window, and the window opened.
2. The air outside was full of ghosts, and every ghost had a chain, and every ghost walked and could not stop.
3. One old ghost saw a poor woman with a child, and he wanted to help her, but he could not.
4. Then the ghosts went away into the dark, and Marley went with them, and the window shut.
5. Scrooge wanted to say "Bah, humbug!" but he could not say it.
6. Scrooge was tired, so he went to bed, and he slept at once.
7. When he woke, the room was dark.
8. The bell rang 12, and Scrooge waited for it to ring 1.
9. When the bell rang 1, a light came into the room, and there was a spirit by the bed.
10. The spirit was small, and its hair was white, but its face was young.
11. It had a white robe, and a bright light came from its head, and it had a cap in its hand.
12. "Are you the spirit that Marley spoke of?" said Scrooge.
13. "I am," said the spirit.
14. "Who are you?" said Scrooge.
15. "I am the Ghost of Christmas Past," said the spirit.
16. "Long past?" said Scrooge.
17. "No, your past," said the ghost.
18. "Why have you come?" said Scrooge.
19. "I have come for your good," said the ghost.
20. "Get up and walk with me."
21. "It is cold, and I have no coat," said Scrooge.
22. "I am a man," said Scrooge, "and I will fall."
23. "Take my hand, and you will not fall," said the ghost.
24. Scrooge took its hand, and they went out through the window.
25. The room was gone, and Scrooge was in a road, and it was day, and snow was on the road.
26. "Good heaven, I was a boy in this place!" said Scrooge.
27. "Do you know the way?" said the ghost.
28. "I know it," said Scrooge, "for I walked it every day."
29. Boys came along the road, and they said a merry Christmas to each other.
30. "These are only shadows of the past," said the ghost, "and they do not see us."

Paragraphs:
1-5: Marley goes to the window, where ghosts with chains walk in the air, and then he goes away with them.
6-11: Scrooge wakes at 12 and waits, and at 1 a small spirit with white hair and a young face comes to his bed.
12-17: The spirit says it is the Ghost of Christmas Past, and that it is Scrooge's own past.
18-24: The ghost tells Scrooge to walk with it, and Scrooge takes its hand and goes out through the window.
25-30: Scrooge stands on a road in the snow and knows it as the place where he was a boy, and boys come by who do not see him.

Fan and Fezziwig
1. The ghost and Scrooge came to a school by the road.
2. The school was an old house, and it was cold and dark.
3. The boys had gone home for Christmas, but one boy sat alone in a room.
4. The boy sat by a poor fire and looked at a book.
5. "Do you know this boy?" said the ghost.
6. "I was that boy," said Scrooge, and Scrooge cried.
7. "Poor boy!" said Scrooge.
8. "What is wrong?" said the ghost.
9. "A boy sang at my door," said Scrooge, "and I gave him nothing."
10. "I wish I had given him money," Scrooge said.
11. "Let us see another Christmas," said the ghost.
12. The boy was older now, and again he sat alone.
13. The door opened, and a small girl ran in.
14. "Brother, I have come to bring you home!" said the girl.
15. "I asked Father if you could come home, and he said yes."
16. "Father is kind now, and I came in a coach to bring you."
17. "You will come home, and we will have a merry Christmas."
18. "You will never come back to this school," said Fan.
19. The girl was Fan, and she was Scrooge's sister.
20. She took the boy by the hand, and he went with her to the door.
21. A coach was in the road, and Fan and the boy went in, and the coach went away.
22. "She was always kind to me," said Scrooge.
23. "Your sister was small, and she was kind," said the ghost, "but she died when she was a woman."
24. "She had a child."
25. "One child," said Scrooge.
26. "Yes, your nephew," said the ghost.
27. Scrooge looked at the door and said nothing.
28. The ghost and Scrooge came to a room where an old man sat at a desk.
29. "That is old Fezziwig, and he was good to me," said Scrooge.
30. "Ebenezer and Dick!" said Fezziwig.
31. A young man came in, and he was Scrooge, and a boy named Dick came in with him.
32. "It is Christmas Eve, boys," said Fezziwig.
33. "Shut the office, and let us have a Christmas party."
34. The young men shut the office.
35. A man came in with a fiddle, and many came in from every house, and the room was full.
36. There was food and drink for every one.
37. Fezziwig and his wife danced, and the rest danced with them, and they danced until 11.
38. When the party was done, Fezziwig shook the hand of every man and woman, and he said a merry Christmas to every one.
39. "He has spent money," said the ghost, "and they are glad."
40. "It is not small," said Scrooge.
41. "Fezziwig could make us glad, and he did."
42. "What he gave us was worth more than money."
43. "What is it?" said the ghost.
44. "Nothing," said Scrooge, "but I wish I could say a word to my clerk now."

Paragraphs:
1-4: Scrooge and the ghost come to an old school, where one boy sits alone by a poor fire at Christmas.
5-10: Scrooge says he was the boy, and that he gave nothing to a boy who sang at his door.
11-14: The ghost shows another Christmas, and the boy is older when a small girl runs in to bring him home.
15-18: The girl says her father is kind now and sent her in a coach, and the boy will never come back to the school.
19-22: Fan takes the boy home by coach, and Scrooge says she was always kind to him.
23-27: The ghost says that Fan died a woman and left one child, Scrooge's nephew, and Scrooge says nothing.
28-33: Scrooge sees his old master Fezziwig call in the young Scrooge and Dick and tell them to shut the office for a Christmas party.
34-38: The room is made ready, guests and a fiddler come, and there is food, drink, and dancing until 11.
39-44: Scrooge says Fezziwig's gift was worth more than money and wishes he could speak to his own clerk.

Belle and the Last Shadow
1. The ghost and Scrooge came to another room.
2. Scrooge was a man now, and a girl sat by him, and she cried.
3. The girl was Belle.
4. "You love money now," said Belle, "and you love it more than you loved me."
5. "I am not changed to you," said Scrooge.
6. "You are changed," said Belle.
7. "When we made our promise, we were poor."
8. "Now you are not the same man."
9. "I was a boy," said Scrooge.
10. "You were not what you are now," said Belle, "but I am the same."
11. "Have I ever asked to be free?" said Scrooge.
12. "Not in words," said Belle.
13. "In what, then?" said Scrooge.
14. "In everything you do," said Belle.
15. "If we had never made our promise, would you come to me now and ask me to marry you?"
16. Scrooge looked at the floor, and he said nothing.
17. "I set you free," said Belle, "for I loved the man you were."
18. "I wish you a good life."
19. She went out, and Scrooge sat alone.
20. The ghost and Scrooge came to a room full of noise.
21. Many children were in the room.
22. Belle was a woman now, and she sat by the fire with a girl who was like her.
23. Her husband came in with toys in his hands, and the children ran to him.
24. The children took the toys and made a loud noise, and the room was full of it.
25. "That girl could have been your child," said the ghost.
26. "I saw an old friend today," said the man to Belle.
27. "Who was it?" said Belle.
28. "It was Scrooge," said the man.
29. "His office door was open, and I saw him at his desk."
30. "Marley is dying," said the man, "and Scrooge sat there alone."
31. "Spirit, take me away from this place!" said Scrooge.
32. "I told you these were shadows of what has been," said the ghost.
33. "Do not blame me."
34. "Take me away, for I cannot look at them!" said Scrooge.
35. "Take me home, and do not come to me again!"
36. Scrooge turned on the ghost and took the cap from its hand, and he pushed the cap down on its head.
37. The ghost went down under the cap, but the light came out from under it and was on the floor.
38. Scrooge pushed the cap down again, but the light was still there.
39. The ghost did not speak.
40. Scrooge was in his room again, and he fell on the bed and slept at once.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Scrooge is a man now, and Belle tells him that he loves money more than he loves her.
7-13: Belle says he is not the man he was when they were poor, and he asks how he has shown it.
14-19: Belle says he would not ask her now, sets him free, wishes him a good life, and leaves.
20-24: Scrooge sees Belle as a woman by the fire with many children, and her husband comes home with toys.
25-30: The ghost says the girl could have been Scrooge's child, and Belle's husband says he saw Scrooge alone at his desk.
31-35: Scrooge begs the ghost to take him away, and the ghost says the shadows are not its fault.
36-40: Scrooge pushes the cap down on the ghost, but the light is still there, and he is back in his room and falls asleep.

The Ghost of Christmas Present
1. Scrooge woke in the dark, and the bell rang 1.
2. No spirit came, and Scrooge sat in his bed and looked at every door.
3. A light came from the next room, and a voice said, "Come in, Scrooge, and know me!"
4. Scrooge went to the door and opened it.
5. The room was his room, but it was full of green leaves.
6. There were turkeys and cakes and fruit on the floor, and a fire was in the room.
7. A big man sat by the food in a green robe, with a light in his hand.
8. "Come in, Scrooge," said the man, "and look at me!"
9. "I am the Ghost of Christmas Present."
10. Scrooge came in, and he looked at the floor.
11. "Have you seen a spirit like me?" said the ghost.
12. "No," said Scrooge.
13. "Have you many brothers?"
14. "I have more than 1800," said the ghost.
15. "That is a big family," said Scrooge.
16. "Spirit, take me where you will," said Scrooge.
17. "I went with a spirit last night, and I will go with you and learn."
18. "Touch my robe," said the ghost.
19. Scrooge touched the robe, and the room was gone, and they were in a street on Christmas morning.
20. It was cold, and snow was on the road.
21. Men and boys took the snow off the road, and every house sent out its dinner to the baker.
22. 2 men were angry with each other in the street.
23. The ghost held his light over them, and they said a merry Christmas to each other.
24. "What is in your light?" said Scrooge.
25. "It is my own," said the ghost.
26. "Is it for any dinner?" said Scrooge.
27. "It is for any dinner that is given in a kind way," said the ghost, "and most for a poor dinner."
28. "Why for a poor dinner most?" said Scrooge.
29. "Because it needs it most," said the ghost.
30. The ghost took Scrooge to a small house, and it was the house of Bob Cratchit.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Scrooge wakes at 1 and waits, and then a light and a voice call him from the next room.
5-10: Scrooge finds his room full of green leaves and food, and a big man in a green robe calls himself the Ghost of Christmas Present.
11-14: The ghost says he has more than 1800 brothers.
15-18: Scrooge asks to be taken where the ghost will so that he can learn, and he touches the robe.
19-25: On Christmas morning in the street the ghost holds his light over two angry men, and they wish each other a merry Christmas.
26-30: The ghost says his light is for any dinner given kindly, most of all a poor one, and takes Scrooge to Bob's house.

The Cratchit Dinner
1. The house was small, and the Cratchit family was poor.
2. Bob's wife put the dinner on the table, and her son Peter was by the fire with the potatoes.
3. Her daughter Belinda helped her.
4. 2 small Cratchits ran in and said, "There is a goose!"
5. "Where is Bob, and where is Tim, and where is Martha?" said his wife.
6. The door opened, and Martha came in.
7. "Here is Martha, Mother!" said the small Cratchits.
8. Bob's wife kissed Martha, and she took Martha's coat.
9. "Here is Bob!" said the small Cratchits, and Bob came in with Tim.
10. Tim was small, and he walked with a crutch, and Bob had him on his back.
11. Bob put Tim in a chair by the fire.
12. "How was Tim in church?" said Bob's wife.
13. "He was very good," said Bob, "and he was better than good."
14. "Tim hoped the people saw him," said Bob, "and thought of the man who made the lame walk and the blind see."
15. Bob's voice was not strong when he said it, and he said no more of it.
16. Tim was by the fire, and he did not say a word.
17. Peter and the small Cratchits went to get the goose.
18. The goose came in, and every one said it was a good goose.
19. It was a small goose, and it was not big for the family, but every one had some, and some was left.
20. Bob's wife went out and came in with the pudding, and there was a piece of green on it.
21. "It is the best pudding since we were married," said Bob.
22. The pudding was small, but every one said it was good.
23. The dinner was done, and the family sat by the fire.
24. There was fruit on the table, and there were apples and oranges.
25. Bob held up a glass.
26. "A merry Christmas to us all, my dears," said Bob.
27. "God bless us!"
28. "God bless us every one!" said Tim, and he was by Bob's side, and Bob held his hand.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The Cratchit family is poor, and while they make ready for dinner, Bob's wife asks where Bob, Tim, and Martha are.
6-11: Martha comes home and is kissed by her mother, and then Bob comes in carrying Tim, who cannot walk, and puts him by the fire.
12-16: Bob says Tim hoped the people in church saw him so they would think of the man who made the lame walk.
17-22: The Cratchits eat a small goose and a small pudding, and every one says they are good.
23-28: After dinner the family sits by the fire, and Bob wishes them a merry Christmas, and Tim says God bless us every one.

The Empty Chair
1. Scrooge looked at Tim for a long time.
2. "Spirit," said Scrooge, "will Tim live?"
3. "I see a chair by the fire with no one in it," said the ghost, "and a crutch with no one to hold it."
4. "If the shadows do not change, the child will die."
5. "No, no," said Scrooge, "say that he will not die!"
6. "Then no one of my kind will find him here," said the ghost.
7. "What then?" said the ghost.
8. "If he will die, he had better do it, and there will be fewer people in the world."
9. Scrooge heard his own words, and he looked at the floor and said nothing.
10. "Man," said the ghost, "if you are a man, do not say those words until you know who the poor are."
11. "Will you say which man shall live and which man shall die?"
12. "It may be that you are less fit to live than this poor man's child."
13. Scrooge heard his name, and he looked up.
14. "Mr Scrooge!" said Bob, and he held up his glass.
15. "I will give you the man who pays for our dinner!"
16. "I wish he was here," said Bob's wife, "and I would tell him what I think of him."
17. "My dear, it is Christmas Day," said Bob.
18. "It is not a day to drink to a man like Scrooge," she said.
19. "He gives nothing to any one, and he loves no one."
20. "You know it, Bob!"
21. "My dear," said Bob, "it is Christmas Day."
22. "I will drink to him for Christmas Day, and for you," said Bob's wife.
23. The children drank to Mr Scrooge, but they did not say a merry Christmas.
24. For 5 minutes no one said a word, and the name of Scrooge was in the room.
25. The family was merry again, for Tim was in his chair by the fire.
26. Scrooge looked at the Cratchits and at Tim until they were gone.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Scrooge asks if Tim will live, and the ghost says that if nothing changes he will die and leave an empty chair and a crutch.
6-10: The ghost gives Scrooge's own words about the poor back to him, and Scrooge looks at the floor and says nothing.
11-15: The ghost asks if Scrooge will say who lives and who dies, and Bob toasts Mr Scrooge as the man who pays for their dinner.
16-21: Bob's wife says Scrooge gives nothing and loves no one, and Bob reminds her that it is Christmas Day.
22-26: Bob's wife drinks to Scrooge for Bob's sake, and after 5 quiet minutes the family is merry again while Scrooge watches Tim until they are gone.

Fred's Party
1. The ghost took Scrooge to many places that night, and in every place he held his light.
2. They went to a hut where poor men sat by a fire, and the men said a merry Christmas.
3. They went to a ship at sea, and every man on the ship said a merry Christmas to the man by him.
4. Scrooge heard a laugh, and it was the laugh of his nephew.
5. Fred was in his house, and his wife and her sisters and some friends were with him.
6. "He said that Christmas was a humbug!" said Fred, and he laughed.
7. "More shame for him, Fred!" said Fred's wife.
8. "I am sure he is rich," said one of her sisters.
9. "What of that?" said Fred.
10. "His money does him no good, and he does not make any one glad with it."
11. "I am sorry for him," said Fred, "and I could not be angry with him if I tried."
12. "His ways hurt him and no one else."
13. "He will not come to dinner with us, and he loses a good dinner."
14. "I think he loses a very good dinner," said Fred's wife, and every one said the same.
15. After dinner they made music, and then they played a game.
16. Fred thought of a thing, and the rest asked questions, and Fred said yes or no.
17. "Is it an animal?" said one of the sisters.
18. "Yes," said Fred, "and it is a bad animal, and it lives in London, and it walks in the street."
19. "Is it a dog?" said the sister.
20. "No," said Fred.
21. "Is it a bear?" said the sister.
22. "No," said Fred, and he laughed and laughed.
23. "I know what it is, Fred!" said the sister.
24. "It is your Uncle Scrooge!"
25. "Yes," said Fred, "and it was."
26. Scrooge laughed, but no one heard him.
27. "He has given us a good time," said Fred, "and I will drink to him."
28. "A merry Christmas to Uncle Scrooge!"
29. "He would not take it from me, but let him have it."
30. Every one said, "A merry Christmas to Uncle Scrooge!" and Scrooge wished he could answer them.
31. But the ghost took Scrooge away, and the house was gone.

Ignorance and Want
1. The ghost was old now, and his hair was white, and the light in his hand was small.
2. "Is your life short?" said Scrooge.
3. "My life in this world is short," said the ghost, "and it ends tonight at 12."
4. "Spirit, something is under your robe," said Scrooge.
5. "What is it?"
6. The ghost opened the robe, and a boy and a girl were under it.
7. The boy and the girl were thin and poor, and they looked up at Scrooge.
8. They did not speak, and they did not move.
9. "Spirit, are they yours?" said Scrooge.
10. "They are the children of men," said the ghost.
11. "This boy is Ignorance, and this girl is Want."
12. "Be afraid of them both, and most of all of the boy."
13. "Spirit, I cannot look at them," said Scrooge.
14. "Do not turn from them," said the ghost.
15. "Have they no help?" said Scrooge.
16. "Are there no prisons?" said the ghost.
17. "Are there no workhouses?"
18. Scrooge said nothing, and he looked at the floor.
19. The bell rang 12, and the ghost was gone.
20. Scrooge remembered what Marley said, and he looked up.
21. A tall figure in a black robe came toward him in the dark.
22. The robe was over its head, and Scrooge could not see its face, and it did not speak.
23. "Are you the Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come?" said Scrooge.
24. The figure held out its hand, and the hand was long and thin.
25. Scrooge looked at the hand, and he could see no more of the figure.
26. Scrooge shook when it came near, and he fell down.
27. "I am more afraid of you than of the others," said Scrooge.
28. "But you come for my good, and I hope to be a new man, and I will go with you."
29. The figure pointed on, and Scrooge went after it.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The ghost says his life ends tonight at 12, and under his robe Scrooge sees a boy and a girl.
7-12: The ghost says the children are Ignorance and Want and tells Scrooge to be afraid of them, most of all the boy.
13-18: Scrooge cannot look at the children, and the ghost gives him back his own questions about prisons and workhouses.
19-25: At 12 the ghost is gone, and Scrooge remembers Marley and sees a tall figure in a black robe with a long thin hand.
26-29: Scrooge falls down, says he is afraid but will go with the figure, and follows where it points.

The Man Who Died
1. The figure took Scrooge to a place in the city, where men stood in the street.
2. Scrooge knew the men, and the figure stopped by 3 of them.
3. "I do not know much about it," said the first man, "but I know he is dead."
4. "When did he die?" said the second man.
5. "Last night, I think," said the first man.
6. "What was wrong with him?" said the third man.
7. "I thought he would never die."
8. "I do not know," said the first man.
9. "What has he done with his money?" said the second man.
10. "I have not heard," said the first man, "but he has not left it to me."
11. "It will be a poor funeral," said the third man, "for I do not know any one who will go."
12. "I will go if there is dinner," said the first man, and they laughed.
13. Scrooge did not know who the dead man was, and he looked for himself, but he was not there.
14. The figure took Scrooge to a small shop in a dark street.
15. An old man named Joe sat in the shop, and it was full of old things.
16. 2 women and a man came in, and each had a bundle.
17. They saw each other and laughed, for each had come to sell.
18. "You are first," said Joe to the first woman.
19. "The dead man will not miss these things, Joe," said the first woman.
20. "He loved no one, and no one loved him."
21. "That is so," said Joe.
22. The first woman put her bundle on the floor, and Joe looked at it.
23. "It is a coat and some small things," said Joe, "and I will give you 5 for them."
24. The man had a shirt, and Joe gave him some money for it.
25. The second woman had sheets from the dead man's bed.
26. "Did you take the sheets off his bed?" said Joe.
27. "I did," said the second woman, "and he was on the bed."
28. "Did he die on it?" said Joe.
29. "He did," said the second woman, "and I took the shirt he had on, and it was a good shirt."
30. "If he had been kind in life, some one would have been by him when he died," said the second woman.
31. "He died alone, so why should I not have his things?"
32. The men and women laughed, and Scrooge looked at them.
33. "I see it," said Scrooge to the figure, "and the case of this man could be mine."

Paragraphs:
1-5: The figure takes Scrooge to 3 men in the city who talk about a man who has died.
6-12: The men wonder what the dead man did with his money, say his funeral will be poor, and one will go only for the dinner.
13-17: Scrooge does not see himself, and the figure takes him to Old Joe's shop, where 2 women and a man come in with bundles.
18-23: The women and the man sell what they took from the dead man, and Joe pays the first woman 5 for a coat and small things.
24-29: The man sells a shirt, and the second woman sells the sheets from the bed and the shirt the dead man wore.
30-33: The second woman says the man died alone because he was not kind in life, and Scrooge sees that he could be that man.

The Man Under the Sheet
1. The figure took Scrooge to a room in a dark house.
2. "Spirit, this is a bad place," said Scrooge, "and I will not forget it."
3. "Let us go."
4. The figure pointed at the bed.
5. A man was on the bed with a sheet over him, and there was no one else in the room.
6. Scrooge wanted to see the face of the man, but he did not touch the sheet.
7. "If any one in this city is sad for this man, show me that one, Spirit," said Scrooge.
8. The figure held out its hand, and there was a room in the day.
9. A woman and her children were in the room, and they waited for her husband.
10. They owed money to a man who was not kind, and the woman looked at the door many times.
11. At last the husband came in, and he looked tired.
12. "Is it good or bad?" said the woman.
13. "It is bad," said the husband.
14. "Do we have nothing now?" said the woman.
15. "No," said the husband, "there is hope, if he is kind to us."
16. "If he is kind, there is hope," said the woman, "but he is not kind to any one."
17. "He is dead," said the husband.
18. The woman was glad, and she said she was sorry for it, but she was glad.
19. "Who will have what we owe him?" said the woman.
20. "I do not know," said the husband, "but we will have the money, and the next man cannot be worse."
21. "We can sleep tonight, and we will not be afraid."
22. The children came near to hear, and their faces were bright, for the man they owed was dead.
23. "This is the only thing that his death has done," said Scrooge.
24. Scrooge looked at the family and said nothing.
25. "Spirit, show me a house where a death is sad," said Scrooge.
26. "Show me, or I will always see that dark room."
27. The figure took him away.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The figure shows Scrooge a man on a bed under a sheet in a dark room, and Scrooge does not touch the sheet.
7-11: Scrooge asks to see some one who is sad for the dead man, and the figure shows him a woman and children waiting for the husband, who owes money to an unkind man.
12-16: The woman asks if the news is good or bad, and the husband says there is hope if the man they owe is kind, but she says he is not.
17-22: The husband says the man they owe is dead, the woman is glad, and they say they will have the money and can sleep without fear.
23-27: Scrooge says the death has made only one family glad, asks to see a house where a death is sad, and the figure takes him away.

Bob and Tim
1. The figure took Scrooge to the house of Bob Cratchit, and the house was quiet.
2. Bob's wife and the children sat by the fire, and no one spoke.
3. Peter had a book, and he looked at it, but he did not read.
4. "It is late," said Bob's wife, "and Bob will be here soon."
5. "He walks slower these days, Mother," said Peter.
6. "I have seen him walk fast with Tim on his back," said Bob's wife.
7. "So have I," said Peter, "and so have we all."
8. Bob came in, and his tea was ready for him.
9. "I went to the place where Tim is," said Bob.
10. "It is a green place, and you will see it, and I said I would go there on Sunday."
11. "My little child!" said Bob, and he cried.
12. Bob went up to the room above, where Tim was, and he sat by the child and kissed his face.
13. Bob went down, and he was not sad.
14. "I saw Fred today," said Bob, "the nephew of Mr Scrooge."
15. "He was kind to me, and he said he was sorry for Tim, and he was sorry for you, my dear."
16. "He gave me his card and said, 'Come to me if I can help you.'"
17. "It is not what he can do for us, my dear, but the kind way he spoke to me."
18. "I think he is a good man," said Bob's wife.
19. "I think he will find a place for Peter," said Bob.
20. Peter laughed, and the family laughed with him.
21. "We will not forget Tim," said Bob, "and we will not forget that we lost him."
22. "We will remember how good he was, and we will not quarrel with each other."
23. "Never, Father!" said the children.
24. "No, never, Father!" said the children again.
25. "I am glad," said Bob.
26. Scrooge looked at Tim's chair by the fire, and it was empty.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The Cratchit house is quiet, and the family says Bob walks slower now than when he carried Tim.
8-13: Bob comes home, says he has been to the green place where Tim is, cries for his little child, and kisses him in the room above.
14-20: Bob says Scrooge's nephew was kind to him, offered help, and may find a place for Peter.
21-26: Bob asks his family never to forget Tim or quarrel, and Scrooge looks at Tim's empty chair.

The Grave
1. "Spirit, our time is short," said Scrooge, "for I know it."
2. "Tell me who the man was that we saw on the bed."
3. The figure took Scrooge to the door of his own office.
4. "This is my office," said Scrooge, "and I will see what I will be."
5. The figure pointed to the window, and Scrooge looked in.
6. It was an office still, but it was not his, and the man in the chair was not Scrooge.
7. The figure took Scrooge to a place with graves, and no one was in it.
8. The figure stood by a grave and pointed down.
9. "Before I go to the stone," said Scrooge, "tell me one thing."
10. "Are these shadows of what will be, or of what may be only?"
11. The figure pointed at the grave and did not move.
12. "A man's ways show where they will end," said Scrooge, "but if he changes his ways, the end changes."
13. "Say that it is so with what you show me!"
14. The figure did not move.
15. Scrooge came near, and he shook, and he read the name on the stone.
16. The name was Ebenezer Scrooge.
17. "Am I the man who was on the bed?" said Scrooge, and he fell down.
18. The hand pointed from the grave to Scrooge and back to the grave.
19. "No, Spirit!" said Scrooge.
20. "Oh no, no!"
21. The hand was still there.
22. "Spirit, hear me!" said Scrooge, and he took hold of its robe.
23. "I am not the man I was, and I will not be the man I would have been."
24. "Why do you show me this, if I have no hope?"
25. The hand shook.
26. "I will keep Christmas all the year," said Scrooge.
27. "I will not forget what the 3 spirits taught me, so tell me I can take the name off this stone!"
28. Scrooge took the hand and held it, but the figure was strong, and it pushed him back.
29. Scrooge held up his hands in a last prayer.
30. Then the robe of the figure grew small, and it fell down, and it was a bedpost.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Scrooge asks who the dead man was, and the figure takes him to his office, where a man who is not Scrooge sits in the chair.
7-11: The figure takes Scrooge to a grave and points down, and Scrooge asks if the shadows are of what will be or what may be.
12-16: Scrooge says that a man who changes his ways changes his end, but the figure does not move, and he reads his own name on the stone.
17-21: Scrooge asks if he is the man on the bed, and the hand points from the grave to him and back.
22-25: Scrooge begs the figure to hear him, says he is not the man he was, and sees the hand shake.
26-30: Scrooge promises to keep Christmas all year and asks that the name be taken off the stone, and the robe shrinks into a bedpost.

The Bells on Christmas Day
1. Scrooge held a bedpost in his hand, and the bed was his own.
2. Scrooge was in his own room, and the night was over.
3. "I can change what will be!" said Scrooge.
4. "I will not forget the lessons of the 3 spirits."
5. "Here is the door where Marley came in!"
6. Scrooge got out of bed, and he laughed, and he cried.
7. "I do not know what day it is," said Scrooge, "and I do not know how long I was with the spirits."
8. "A merry Christmas to every one!"
9. Scrooge opened the window and looked out.
10. There was no fog, and the day was bright and cold, and the church bells rang.
11. A boy was in the street below.
12. "What day is it?" said Scrooge to the boy.
13. "Why, it is Christmas Day," said the boy.
14. "I have not missed it," said Scrooge, "for the spirits did it all in one night."
15. "Do you know the shop at the end of the next street, where they sell turkeys?"
16. "I do," said the boy.
17. "Do you know if the big turkey is still in the window?" said Scrooge.
18. "It is there now," said the boy.
19. "Go and buy it, and tell the man to bring it here," said Scrooge.
20. "Come back with him in 5 minutes, and I will give you a shilling."
21. The boy ran, and Scrooge laughed.
22. "I will send the turkey to Bob Cratchit," said Scrooge.
23. "He will not know who sent it, and it is twice as big as Tim."
24. Scrooge wrote the name on a paper, and he went down to wait for the man.
25. The man came with the turkey, and it was so big that he could not carry it far.
26. "You cannot take it to Bob's house on your back," said Scrooge, "so you must go in a coach."
27. Scrooge paid for the turkey and for the coach, and he paid the boy.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Scrooge wakes in his own bed and knows he can change what will be.
7-11: Scrooge does not know the day, and he opens the window to a bright day with church bells and a boy in the street.
12-18: The boy tells Scrooge it is Christmas Day and that the big turkey is still in the shop window.
19-23: Scrooge sends the boy to buy the turkey and plans to send it to Bob without saying who sent it.
24-27: The man comes with the turkey, and Scrooge pays for it, for a coach, and for the boy.

Scrooge Gives and Bob Is Late
1. Scrooge put on his best coat and went out into the street.
2. Every man he saw said good morning, and Scrooge said a merry Christmas to each.
3. Scrooge saw one of the 2 men who had come to his office.
4. "Sir," said Scrooge, "I hope you did well yesterday."
5. "Are you Mr Scrooge?" said the man.
6. "That is my name," said Scrooge, "and I am sorry for what I said to you."
7. Scrooge told the man how much money he would give.
8. "Are you sure, Mr Scrooge?" said the man.
9. "I am sure," said Scrooge, "and it is not a little, for it is all that I did not give before."
10. "I do not know what to say to you, sir," said the man.
11. "Say nothing," said Scrooge, "but come and see me."
12. "I will," said the man.
13. Scrooge went to church, and he walked in the streets and said good day to the people.
14. Scrooge gave to the poor, and he looked at the houses and the people in them.
15. He saw the children in the street, and he put his hand on the head of each child.
16. He had never thought a walk could make him so glad.
17. In the afternoon Scrooge came to the door of Fred's house.
18. Scrooge stood at the door for some time, and he was afraid to knock, but he knocked.
19. "Is Fred at home?" said Scrooge to the girl.
20. "Yes, sir," said the girl.
21. "Fred, it is your Uncle Scrooge," said Scrooge.
22. "I have come to dinner, and will you let me in, Fred?"
23. Fred shook his hand for a long time, and Scrooge came in.
24. Fred's wife came, and the sisters came, and every one shook the hand of Scrooge.
25. They had dinner, and they played the game, and Scrooge laughed with them.
26. Every one said a merry Christmas to Scrooge, and Scrooge said it to them.
27. The next morning Scrooge was in his office early, and he wanted to see Bob come in late.
28. The bell rang 9, and Bob was not there.
29. Bob came in 18 minutes late.
30. "What do you mean by coming in at this time?" said Scrooge in his old voice.
31. "I am sorry, sir," said Bob, "for I am late."
32. "You are," said Scrooge, "and I think you are."
33. "It is only once in a year, sir," said Bob.
34. "It will not be so again, for I was merry yesterday."
35. "I will tell you what, my friend," said Scrooge, "I will not have this any more."
36. Bob was afraid, and he looked at the door.
37. "And so," said Scrooge, "I will give you more money."
38. "A merry Christmas, Bob!" said Scrooge.
39. "It is a better Christmas than I have given you in many years."
40. "I will help your family, and we will speak of it today by the fire."
41. "Put more coal on the fire, Bob, before you write another word!"
42. Scrooge did all that he said, and more.
43. Tim did not die, and Scrooge helped him and loved him.
44. Scrooge was a good friend and a good man, and every one knew it.
45. Some people laughed at him, but Scrooge let them laugh.
46. Scrooge did not see the spirits again, and it was said of him that he knew how to keep Christmas.
47. And Tim said, "God bless us every one!"
