The Steadfast Tin Soldier

Twenty-Five Brothers / The Nursery Table / The Paper Dancer / Night in the Nursery / The Toy Goblin's Warning
1. A toy maker had one tin spoon.
2. He melted the tin spoon over a fire.
3. He poured the liquid tin into a mold.
4. The mold made one soldier.
5. 25 tin soldiers came from the mold.
6. Each soldier held a small gun.
7. The soldiers all looked the same.
8. But there was no tin for the last one.
9. That soldier got only one leg.
10. He stood on his one leg.
11. He stood as firm as his brothers.
12. The toy maker put the soldiers in a box.
13. A boy took the tin soldiers out of their box.
14. He set them in a row on a table.
15. Many other toys filled the table too.
16. A paper castle stood at one end.
17. The castle had a door and small windows.
18. The other soldiers stood in pairs.
19. The last soldier had no partner.
20. He stood alone at the end of the row.
21. From there, he could see the castle door.
22. A toy goblin sat on the table.
23. The toy goblin sat inside a little box.
24. The little box had a lid.
25. A paper dancer stood at the castle door.
26. She had a dress.
27. A shiny spangle was on her dress.
28. She held her arms out.
29. She lifted one leg.
30. The tin soldier could see only her other leg.
31. He believed she had only one leg, like him.
32. He loved her for this.
33. She had a castle.
34. He had only a box.
35. The boy went to bed.
36. The house was still.
37. The boy put the other soldiers in their box.
38. But he forgot the last soldier on the table.
39. At night, the toys moved by themselves.
40. The boxed soldiers moved to come out.
41. But they could not open the lid.
42. The tin soldier did not move.
43. She stood on one leg all night.
44. The tin soldier stood on his one leg too.
45. He held his gun.
46. He watched her all night.
47. Midnight came.
48. The lid on the toy goblin's little box flew open.
49. He jumped.
50. He sat near the castle.
51. The last soldier stood by the box.
52. The goblin looked at the last soldier.
53. The last soldier watched the paper dancer.
54. "Stop looking at her," said the goblin.
55. But the tin soldier said nothing.
56. "You will be sorry tomorrow," said the goblin.
57. The tin soldier was not afraid.
58. He watched the dancer.

Paragraphs:
1-7: A toy maker melts a tin spoon, pours it into a mold, and makes 25 tin soldiers, each holding a small gun and looking just alike.
8-12: The last soldier gets only one leg for lack of tin, but stands as firm as his brothers, and the maker boxes them all.
13-17: A boy takes the soldiers out and lines them up on a table full of toys, including a paper castle with a door and windows.
18-21: The last soldier, with no partner, stands alone at the row's end, able to see the castle door.
22-24: A toy goblin sits on the table, inside a lidded little box.
25-29: A paper dancer stands at the castle door, her dress spangled, arms out and one leg lifted.
30-34: Believing she too has one leg, the soldier loves her, though she has a castle and he only a box.
35-38: The boy goes to bed, boxing the other soldiers but forgetting the last one on the table.
39-42: At night the toys move, but the boxed soldiers can't escape, and the tin soldier stays still.
43-46: The dancer stands on one leg all night, as does the soldier, watching her.
47-53: At midnight, the goblin's box lid flies open and he jumps out to sit near the castle, looking at the last soldier, who watches the dancer.
54-58: The goblin warns him to stop looking or be sorry tomorrow, but the soldier, unafraid, says nothing and keeps watching.

The Fall / The Paper Boat / The Rat's Toll / Into the Canal / Swallowed by a Fish
1. Morning came.
2. The boy found the last soldier on the table.
3. He set the tin soldier in the window.
4. The window of the house was high over the street.
5. The wind blew hard.
6. The window flew open.
7. The tin soldier fell from the window.
8. He fell all the way to the street.
9. He landed between two stones.
10. His gun was stuck between the stones.
11. The boy looked for the tin soldier.
12. A maid looked too.
13. But they did not find him.
14. Rain fell hard on the street.
15. The tin soldier lay between the stones.
16. Two boys came down the street.
17. They saw the tin soldier in the street.
18. "Look, a tin soldier," said one boy.
19. "Let's make him a boat," said the other boy.
20. The boys made a boat from paper.
21. They put the tin soldier inside the boat.
22. The boys ran with the boat to the gutter.
23. They set it in the gutter.
24. It sailed down the gutter fast.
25. But the tin soldier did not move.
26. He stood on his one leg inside the boat.
27. The boat sailed into a dark tunnel.
28. It was as dark as his box.
29. The tin soldier believed the goblin did this.
30. A big rat came into the tunnel.
31. The rat looked up at him.
32. "You must pay a toll," said the rat.
33. But the tin soldier said nothing.
34. He held his gun hard.
35. The rat could not stop him.
36. The boat sailed on fast.
37. Ahead, the tin soldier saw the way out.
38. The boat came out of the tunnel.
39. It sailed into a big canal.
40. The canal water was fast.
41. The boat spun around and around.
42. Water filled the boat.
43. The paper broke a little.
44. The tin soldier stood in the water.
45. The water was up to his neck.
46. He believed he would not see her again.
47. The boat broke.
48. He sank into the water.
49. A big fish came by.
50. It swallowed the tin soldier.
51. Inside the fish, it was dark.
52. It was as dark as the tunnel and his box.
53. The last soldier lay down inside the fish.
54. The toy soldier thought of the paper dancer.
55. The fish swam in the sea.
56. Days passed.
57. At last, the fish was still.
58. One day, a man caught the fish.
59. "What a big fish!" said the man.
60. He sold it at a market.

Paragraphs:
1-6: In the morning, the boy finds the last soldier and sets him in the high window, and a hard wind blows it open.
7-10: The soldier falls from the window, landing between two stones, his gun stuck fast.
11-13: The boy and a maid search but never find him.
14-19: Rain falls hard on the street where the soldier lies between two stones, and two passing boys spot him and decide to make him a boat.
20-24: The boys make a paper boat, put the soldier inside, and run it to the gutter, where it sails off fast.
25-26: The soldier stands still on his one leg.
27-29: The boat enters a dark tunnel, and the soldier blames the goblin.
30-35: A big rat demands a toll in the tunnel, but the soldier stays silent, holds his gun firm, and the rat cannot stop him.
36-37: The fast boat nears the way out.
38-42: The boat leaves the tunnel into a fast canal, spinning around and around as water fills it.
43-46: The paper breaks, and as water rises to his neck, he believes he'll never see her again.
47-48: The boat breaks; he sinks.
49-52: A big fish swallows him; inside it is as dark as his box.
53-54: He lies inside the fish, thinking of the dancer.
55-57: The fish swims on for days, then stills.
58-60: A man catches the fish one day and sells it at market.

Back to the Same House / The Same Room / The Little Tin Heart
1. A cook came to the market.
2. She bought the fish.
3. The cook was from the same house.
4. She went to the kitchen.
5. She cut the fish open.
6. Inside, she found the last soldier.
7. He still held his gun.
8. But he did not move.
9. The cook was very surprised.
10. She took him to the boy's room.
11. She put him near the castle.
12. The tin soldier was on the table again.
13. The same paper castle stood there.
14. The same dancer stood at the door.
15. She still stood on one leg.
16. The tin soldier still held his gun.
17. He watched her.
18. She looked at him.
19. The tin soldier was glad to see her.
20. He thought of his box.
21. But they said nothing.
22. The boy came to see the tin soldier.
23. He did not know of the fish.
24. One day, a boy took the tin soldier.
25. He threw the tin soldier into the stove.
26. No one knew why.
27. Perhaps the toy goblin was behind it.
28. The tin soldier stood in the fire.
29. He did not let go of his gun.
30. The tin soldier melted.
31. Perhaps it was the fire, or his love.
32. A door opened.
33. A draft blew the paper dancer into the stove.
34. She was gone.
35. In the morning, the maid came to the stove.
36. She found a little tin heart in the ashes.
37. Of the dancer, only the spangle was left.
38. It was burnt black.

Paragraphs:
1-3: A cook from the same house buys the fish at market.
4-8: In the kitchen she cuts the fish open, finding the soldier still holding his gun.
9-11: Surprised, the cook takes him to the boy's room.
12-16: The soldier is back on the same table, with the same castle and dancer, still on one leg and holding his gun.
17-23: Watching each other and glad to be reunited, they say nothing, and the boy, not knowing of the fish, comes to see the soldier.
24-27: One day a boy throws him into the stove for no known reason, perhaps the goblin's doing.
28-31: The soldier stands in the fire, still gripping his gun, and melts, whether from heat or love.
32-34: The dancer is blown into the stove and gone.
35-38: The maid finds a tin heart in the ashes; only a burnt spangle remains of the dancer.
