The Ugly Duckling

A Nest by the Water
1. A duck built a nest by the water.
2. She laid many eggs in the nest.
3. Every day, she sat on her eggs.
4. One egg was bigger than the other eggs.
5. "Whose big egg is this?" an old duck asked.
6. The mother duck did not know whose egg it was.
7. She said nothing more.
8. Soon the smaller eggs began to crack open.
9. Small yellow ducklings came out.
10. The small ducklings sat by the nest.
11. The mother duck came to the nest every day.
12. But the big egg did not crack yet.
13. The mother duck sat on the big egg again.
14. She waited for the big one to open.

Paragraphs:
1-6: A duck builds a nest by the water and lays many eggs, sitting on them every day; one egg is bigger than the other eggs, and an old duck asks whose big egg it is.
7-10: The mother duck says nothing more, and soon the smaller eggs crack open into small yellow ducklings, who sit by the nest.
11-14: The mother duck comes to the nest every day, but the big egg does not crack yet, so she sits on it again and waits for it to open.

The Last Egg Hatches
1. At last, the big egg began to crack.
2. A gray duckling came out of the egg.
3. The other ducklings were small and yellow.
4. This duckling was big and gray.
5. "This duckling is not pretty," an old duck said.
6. "But he is still my child," the mother duck said.
7. The next day, the mother duck took her ducklings to the water.
8. One by one, the ducklings went into the water.
9. The gray duckling went into the water too.
10. He swam better than the other ducklings.
11. Even the old duck watched him swim.
12. "This duckling swims very well," the mother duck said.
13. She was proud of the gray duckling.
14. Soon she would take her ducklings to the farmyard.

Paragraphs:
1-6: At last the big egg cracks open, and a gray duckling comes out, big and gray next to the other small, yellow ducklings; an old duck says he is not pretty, but the mother duck says he is still her child.
7-10: The next day the mother duck takes her ducklings to the water; one by one they go in, and the gray duckling goes in too, swimming better than the other ducklings.
11-14: Even the old duck watches him swim, and the mother duck says he swims very well; proud of him, she plans to take her ducklings to the farmyard soon.

Into the Farmyard
1. The mother duck took her ducklings to the farmyard.
2. Many other ducks lived in the farmyard.
3. The other ducks saw the gray duckling.
4. "Look at that ugly duckling!" one duck said.
5. "He is too big for a duckling," said another duck.
6. The ducks did not want him near them.
7. One duck bit his neck.
8. "Go away, ugly duckling!" the duck said.
9. The other ducklings went to play in the farmyard.
10. But no duck would play with the gray duckling.
11. He walked alone.
12. He put his head down and said nothing.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The mother duck takes her ducklings to the farmyard, where many other ducks live and see the gray duckling; one duck calls him ugly, and another says he is too big, so the ducks do not want him near them.
7-12: One duck bites his neck and tells him to go away, and while the other ducklings go to play in the farmyard, no duck will play with the gray duckling, so he walks alone with his head down, saying nothing.

The Hens and the Girl
1. Hens lived in the farmyard too.
2. The hens looked at the gray duckling.
3. "That duckling is strange," one hen said.
4. "He is too big and too gray," said another hen.
5. The hens went away from him.
6. A girl fed the ducks and hens every day.
7. The girl saw the gray duckling.
8. She kicked him away from the food.
9. "Go away, you ugly duckling," she said.
10. The gray duckling went hungry that day.
11. His mother grew tired of him too.
12. "I wish you were far from here," she said.
13. The duckling heard what she said.
14. He walked away with his head down.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Hens live in the farmyard too, and they look at the gray duckling and call him strange, too big, and too gray, so the hens go away from him.
7-10: A girl who feeds the ducks and hens every day sees the gray duckling and kicks him away from the food, calling him an ugly duckling, so he goes hungry.
11-14: Even his mother grows tired of him and says she wishes he were far from here, and the duckling, hearing her words, walks away with his head down.

Running Away
1. The duckling left the farmyard.
2. No duck or hen wanted him near them.
3. He walked far from the farmyard.
4. He walked all night long.
5. In the morning, he came to a big marsh.
6. Wild birds lived in the marsh.
7. Tall reeds grew by the marsh.
8. The marsh was new to him.
9. He looked at the wide water.
10. He was tired from his long walk.
11. He sat down near the water to rest.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The duckling leaves the farmyard, since no duck or hen wants him near them, and he walks far from the farmyard, all night long.
5-11: In the morning he comes to a big marsh, where wild birds live and tall reeds grow by the marsh, all new to him; after he looks at the wide water, tired from his long walk, he sits down near the water to rest.

The Wild Marsh
1. Wild geese lived in the marsh too.
2. The duckling stayed near the reeds.
3. He said nothing to the wild geese.
4. The wild geese said nothing to him either.
5. One night, loud sounds came from the marsh.
6. Hunters came to the marsh with guns.
7. The hunters shot at the wild birds.
8. Big dogs ran through the reeds and water.
9. The duckling sat in the tall reeds.
10. A big dog came close to him.
11. The dog looked at him but did not bite him.
12. The duckling was so ugly that no dog would bite him.
13. At last, the hunters and dogs went away.
14. The duckling was alone in the marsh again.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Wild geese live in the marsh too, but the duckling stays near the reeds and says nothing to them, and the wild geese say nothing to him either.
5-9: One night, loud sounds come from the marsh: hunters come with guns and shoot at the wild birds, and big dogs run through the reeds and water, so the duckling sits still in the tall reeds.
10-14: A big dog comes close and looks at him but does not bite him, for the duckling is so ugly that no dog would bite him; at last the hunters and dogs go away, and the duckling is alone in the marsh again.

The Old Woman's Hut
1. The days grew cold.
2. The duckling was cold and alone.
3. One day, he left the marsh.
4. He walked all day.
5. At night, he came to a small hut.
6. An old woman lived in the hut.
7. The old woman found the duckling, tired and cold.
8. The old woman could not see well.
9. "You may stay here," the old woman said.
10. She thought the duckling could lay eggs.
11. A hen and a cat lived in the hut.
12. The duckling would stay with them.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The days grow cold, and the duckling, cold and alone, leaves the marsh and walks all day, coming to a small hut at night.
6-12: An old woman lives in the hut and finds the duckling, tired and cold; she cannot see well, so she tells him he may stay, thinking he could lay eggs, and the duckling would stay with her hen and cat.

The Hen and the Cat
1. The hen and the cat lived well in the hut.
2. The cat could arch his back and purr.
3. The hen could lay eggs every day.
4. "Can you lay eggs?" the hen asked the duckling.
5. "No, I cannot," said the duckling.
6. "Can you purr like the cat?" the hen asked.
7. "No, I cannot do that either," said the duckling.
8. "You have no right to talk," said the cat.
9. The duckling said nothing more.
10. He thought of the wild marsh again.
11. He wanted to swim in the water.
12. "Swimming is a foolish wish," the hen said.
13. The cat did not want him to swim either.
14. But the duckling still wanted to swim.
15. He knew he would leave the hut.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The hen and the cat live well in the hut, for the cat can arch his back and purr, and the hen can lay eggs every day; but when the hen asks if the duckling can lay eggs, he says no.
6-9: The hen asks if he can purr like the cat, and he says he cannot do that either, so the cat says he has no right to talk, and the duckling says nothing.
10-15: He thinks of the wild marsh again and wants to swim in the water, but the hen calls swimming a foolish wish, and the cat does not want him to swim either; still, the duckling wants to swim, and he knows he would leave the hut.

Alone on the Water
1. The duckling left the hut.
2. He did not go back to the marsh.
3. He found a big lake.
4. The lake had wide water.
5. The duckling swam into the water.
6. He dove under the water too.
7. No one there called him ugly.
8. The duckling swam every day.
9. He dove and swam alone.
10. Other birds saw him but did not come near.
11. The duckling stayed on the lake all day.
12. He loved the open water.
13. The leaves began to fall.
14. The autumn days grew colder.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The duckling leaves the hut, not going back to the marsh, and finds a big lake with wide water; he swims into the water and dives under it too, and no one there calls him ugly.
8-14: The duckling swims every day, diving and swimming alone, and though other birds see him, they do not come near; still, he stays on the lake all day and loves the open water, even as the leaves begin to fall and the autumn days grow colder.

The Swans Fly South
1. One autumn day, the duckling swam on the lake.
2. He saw big white birds in the sky.
3. The birds had long necks and white feathers.
4. They were swans, and they flew south.
5. The swans called to one another as they flew.
6. The swans flew near the lake.
7. The duckling had not seen birds so beautiful.
8. He watched the swans fly away.
9. The duckling wanted to follow the swans.
10. He did not know why.
11. The duckling swam alone again.
12. He thought of the beautiful white swans.
13. Soon the days grew even colder.
14. The water was cold and gray.

Paragraphs:
1-6: One autumn day, the duckling swims on the lake when he sees big white birds in the sky, birds with long necks and white feathers; they are swans, flying south and calling to one another near the lake.
7-10: The duckling has not seen birds so beautiful, and he watches them fly away, wanting to follow them though he does not know why.
11-14: He swims alone again, thinking of the beautiful white swans, as the days grow even colder and the water turns cold and gray.

Frozen Fast
1. Winter came.
2. Ice grew on the lake.
3. Every day, the ice grew thick.
4. Other birds flew away for the winter.
5. The duckling was alone on the lake.
6. He thought of the beautiful swans again.
7. Still, he swam to keep the water open.
8. But the water grew smaller every day.
9. The duckling grew tired.
10. One cold night, he grew too tired to swim.
11. Soon he was frozen fast in the ice.
12. He could not move.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Winter comes, and ice grows on the lake, thick every day, so that other birds fly away for the winter; the duckling is alone on the lake, and he thinks of the beautiful swans again.
7-12: Still, he swims to keep the water open, but the water grows smaller every day, and the duckling grows tired, until one cold night he grows too tired to swim; soon he is frozen fast in the ice, unable to move.

The Farmer's Rescue
1. A farmer walked by the frozen lake.
2. He saw the duckling frozen in the ice.
3. The farmer walked out on the ice.
4. He broke the ice with his shoe.
5. The farmer took the duckling.
6. The duckling could not move.
7. His feathers were wet and cold.
8. The farmer was kind to birds.
9. He carried the duckling to his wife.
10. "This bird is frozen," said the farmer.
11. His wife took the duckling.
12. She put the duckling near the fire.
13. The duckling did not want to be there.
14. He thought of the wild marsh.
15. Soon the duckling began to move again.

Paragraphs:
1-7: A farmer walks by the frozen lake and sees the duckling frozen in the ice; he walks out on the ice and breaks it with his shoe, then takes the duckling, whose feathers are wet and cold and who cannot move.
8-11: The farmer, who is kind to birds, carries the duckling to his wife, saying this bird is frozen, and his wife takes the duckling.
12-15: She puts the duckling near the fire, but the duckling does not want to be there and thinks of the wild marsh, until soon he begins to move again.

Trouble in the Farmhouse
1. The farmer had children.
2. The children ran to see the duckling.
3. "Look at that bird!" one child said.
4. The children wanted to play with him.
5. The duckling did not know the children.
6. He flew up on the table.
7. The milk fell to the floor.
8. The children called out.
9. The farmer's wife ran in.
10. The duckling flew into the flour.
11. White flour was on his gray feathers.
12. But the duckling ran from her.
13. He ran through the door.
14. He ran out into the cold.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The farmer has children, and they run to see the duckling, wanting to play with him, but the duckling does not know them, so he flies up on the table and knocks the milk to the floor.
8-14: The children call out, and the farmer's wife runs in, but the duckling flies into the flour, and white flour lands on his gray feathers; still, he runs from her, through the door, and out into the cold.

A Long Winter Alone
1. The duckling ran far away.
2. He found the reeds by the frozen lake.
3. He hid among the tall reeds.
4. The winter was long.
5. The duckling was cold every day.
6. He did not find much food.
7. No one came to see him.
8. He stayed alone among the reeds.
9. At last, the ice began to melt.
10. The sun grew warm again.
11. The warm sun was on his feathers.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The duckling runs far away and finds the reeds by the frozen lake, hiding among the tall reeds through a long winter, cold for him every day.
6-11: He does not find much food, and no one comes to see him, so he stays alone among the reeds; at last the ice begins to melt, the sun grows warm again, and the warm sun is on his feathers.

Spring and the Swans
1. The days grew warm again.
2. The duckling had grown much bigger.
3. He could fly well.
4. One day, he flew to the lake.
5. He saw swans on the water.
6. The swans were big and white.
7. The duckling wanted to swim with them.
8. But he thought he was still gray and big.
9. He thought of the farmyard and the marsh.
10. He thought of the hen and the cat too.
11. "The swans may kill an ugly bird like me," he thought.
12. "But I would rather die than be alone again," he thought.
13. He swam to the swans.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The days grow warm again; the duckling has grown much bigger and can fly well, and one day he flies to the lake and sees swans on the water.
7-13: The swans are big and white, and the duckling wants to swim with them, though he thinks he is still gray and big; he thinks of the farmyard, the marsh, the hen, and the cat, and thinks the swans may kill an ugly bird like him, but he would rather die than be alone again, so he swims to the swans.

The Most Beautiful of All
1. The duckling swam close to the swans.
2. He bowed his head and waited for them to attack.
3. But the swans did not attack him.
4. The swans swam near him.
5. He looked down into the water.
6. He saw his reflection in the water.
7. The reflection was not a gray duckling.
8. It was a big, white swan.
9. The duckling had become a swan too.
10. Children played by the water.
11. "Look at that new swan!" said one child.
12. "He is the most beautiful of all!" said another child.
13. The swan spread his wings.
14. He swam among the swans, one of them at last.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The duckling swims close to the swans and bows his head, waiting for them to attack, but the swans do not attack him and swim near him instead, so he looks down into the water and sees his reflection there.
7-10: The reflection is not a gray duckling but a big, white swan, for the duckling has become a swan too, and children playing by the water see him.
11-14: One child calls him a new swan, and another calls him the most beautiful of all; the swan spreads his wings and swims among the swans, one of them at last.
