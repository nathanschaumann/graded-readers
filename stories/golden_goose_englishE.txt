The Golden Goose

A Poor Family / The Eldest Son's Bad Luck / The Second Son's Bad Luck / The Youngest Son Sets Out
1. A poor father and mother lived near a forest.
2. The father and mother had three sons.
3. The eldest son and the second son could work.
4. The youngest son was kind.
5. His brothers called him a simpleton.
6. But the youngest son did not mind.
7. He shared what little he had.
8. The father let his sons go into the forest.
9. They cut wood there to earn money.
10. One day, the eldest son wanted to go first.
11. The eldest son wanted wood to earn money for his mother.
12. The mother baked a cake for the eldest son.
13. She gave him a cup of sweet milk too.
14. The eldest son walked into the forest with an axe.
15. He cut wood near a tree.
16. Then a gray man came out of the trees.
17. "May I have some cake and milk?" the gray man asked.
18. "No," said the eldest son.
19. The eldest son ate the cake alone and was not kind.
20. He picked up his axe again.
21. The axe slipped and cut his arm.
22. The eldest son could not work with a cut arm.
23. He walked home with no cake or wood for his mother.
24. The mother worried about her sons in the forest.
25. The mother baked a cake for the second son too.
26. The second son walked into the forest with an axe.
27. The same gray man came out of the trees.
28. "May I have some cake and milk?" the gray man asked.
29. "No," said the second son.
30. The second son ate the cake alone.
31. He stood up and picked up his axe again.
32. The axe slipped and cut his leg.
33. The second son could not walk with a cut leg.
34. His leg hurt, and he walked home without wood.
35. Both brothers came home hurt from the forest.
36. The father feared for his youngest son's safety.
37. "The forest is not safe," the father said.
38. But the youngest son wanted to try too.
39. "Please let me go," he said.
40. The father let him go.
41. The mother had little left to give him.
42. She baked a dry cake for him.
43. She gave him a cup of sour milk too.
44. The youngest son thanked his mother for the poor meal.
45. He walked toward the forest with the cake and milk.

Paragraphs:
1-5: A poor father and mother have three sons: a hardworking eldest son and second son, and a kind youngest son whom his older brothers call a simpleton.
6-10: The youngest son does not mind and shares what little he has, and since the family has little money, the father lets his sons go into the forest to cut wood and earn money, and one day the eldest son wants to go first.
11-15: The eldest son wants wood to earn money for his mother, who bakes him a cake and sweet milk, and he walks into the forest with an axe and cuts wood near a tree.
16-19: A gray man, who also walks alone in the forest, comes out of the trees and asks for some of his cake and milk, but the eldest son refuses, eats the cake alone, and is not kind.
20-23: He picks up his axe again, but it slips and cuts his arm, so he cannot work and walks home with no cake or wood for his mother.
24-26: The mother worries about her sons in the forest as she bakes the second son a cake and gives him milk, and he walks into the forest with an axe and cuts wood near a tree.
27-30: The same gray man asks to share his cake, but the second son refuses and eats alone.
31-34: He picks up his axe again, but it slips and cuts his leg, so he cannot walk, and with his leg hurting, he walks home without wood.
35-36: Both brothers come home hurt, and the father, fearing for his youngest son's safety, remembers that his other two sons were hurt.
37-40: The father does not want the youngest son to go into the forest, but the youngest son asks to try, and the father finally lets him go.
41-45: The mother has little left to give him, so she bakes a dry cake and gives him sour milk, and he thanks her and walks toward the forest with the food.

Sharing in the Forest / A Golden Reward / The Inn and the Three Daughters / Stuck Fast
1. But the youngest son wanted to help his family.
2. The same gray man came out of the trees.
3. "May I share your cake and milk?" the gray man asked.
4. "Yes, please share my meal," said the youngest son.
5. He gave the gray man the dry cake.
6. He gave him the cup of sour milk too.
7. The dry cake became a soft cake.
8. The sour milk became sweet milk too.
9. The gray man and the youngest son ate together.
10. "I will help you too," the gray man said.
11. "Cut down that tree by the rock," said the gray man.
12. "Look among its roots," the gray man said.
13. He found the tree by the rock.
14. He cut down the tree with his axe.
15. The tree fell down.
16. He looked among its roots.
17. A goose sat there with golden feathers.
18. The youngest son picked up the goose.
19. The youngest son put the goose under his arm.
20. He thanked the gray man for his help.
21. The gray man went back into the trees.
22. That night, the youngest son came to an inn.
23. An innkeeper lived there with his three daughters.
24. "May I stay for the night?" the youngest son asked.
25. "Yes, you may," said the innkeeper.
26. The three daughters saw the golden goose under his arm.
27. The daughters wanted a golden feather too.
28. The first daughter said, "I will take a feather."
29. "Wait for him to fall asleep," said the second daughter.
30. The third daughter wanted to try it too.
31. The youngest son ate some food and went to bed.
32. He put the golden goose in his room.
33. He fell asleep.
34. Later that night, the first daughter went to his room.
35. The first daughter reached out and pulled at a golden feather.
36. Her hand stuck fast to the goose.
37. She called quietly for her sister.
38. The second daughter came to help her.
39. She took hold of her sister's arm.
40. Her hand stuck fast to her sister's arm too.
41. Both daughters called for the third daughter, who came to help.
42. She took hold of the second daughter's hand.
43. She stuck fast too.
44. But the golden goose and the youngest son did not wake up.
45. All three daughters stood stuck together in a line.

Paragraphs:
1-2: The youngest son walks into the forest, remembering that his brothers came home hurt, but he still wants to help his family, and the same gray man appears from the trees.
3-6: The gray man asks to share his cake and milk, and the youngest son happily agrees, giving him the dry cake and the cup of sour milk.
7-10: The dry cake becomes a soft cake and the sour milk becomes sweet milk; the gray man and the youngest son eat together and smile, the youngest son is happy, and the gray man thanks him and promises to help him too.
11-13: The gray man tells the youngest son to cut down a certain tree by a rock and look among its roots, and the youngest son, still in the forest, finds the tree.
14-17: He cuts down the tree, and among its roots he finds a goose with golden feathers.
18-21: The youngest son picks up the goose and realizes he no longer has to earn money by cutting wood; this poor family now has a golden goose, and he puts it under his arm, thanks the gray man, and the gray man goes back into the trees.
22-25: That night the youngest son comes to an inn run by an innkeeper with three daughters, and the innkeeper lets him stay the night.
26-30: The three daughters see the golden goose and want a feather too, so they plan to take one once the young man falls asleep.
31-33: The youngest son eats, puts the goose in his room, and falls asleep.
34-36: Later that night the first daughter goes to his room and pulls at a golden feather, but her hand sticks fast to the goose and she cannot pull free.
37-40: She calls quietly for her sister, who comes to help and grabs her arm, but her hand sticks fast too.
41-45: The third daughter comes to help as well and sticks fast too, and though the golden goose and the youngest son do not wake up, all three daughters now stand stuck together in a line.

The Morning Departure / The Parson and the Sexton / The Silent Princess / The Princess Laughs / The King's Task / The Wedding
1. The youngest son woke up.
2. He did not mind the three daughters.
3. He picked up the golden goose and held it under his arm.
4. He left the inn with the goose.
5. The youngest son walked down the road toward the city.
6. The three daughters ran behind the goose.
7. "Stop!" the first daughter called out.
8. "Wait for us!" she said.
9. But the youngest son did not stop.
10. The three daughters ran after him in a field.
11. A parson stood in the field near the road.
12. He saw the three daughters running and stuck together.
13. "Why are you running after that young man?" the parson asked.
14. "You must stop now!" he said.
15. The parson reached for the third daughter's hand.
16. He wanted to pull her from the road.
17. His hand stuck fast to her hand too.
18. A sexton saw the parson running down the road.
19. "Where are you running to?" the sexton called.
20. "The church needs you now!" the sexton said.
21. He took hold of the parson.
22. He stuck fast too.
23. Five people ran behind the goose in a line.
24. A king and his young daughter lived in the city.
25. His daughter was a young princess who never laughed.
26. The king wanted the princess to laugh.
27. "Whoever makes the young princess laugh will marry her," said the king.
28. Many people in the city wanted to make her laugh.
29. But no man in the city could make the princess laugh.
30. The youngest son and his golden goose came near the city.
31. Five people still ran behind him and the goose, stuck fast.
32. The young princess sat at her window.
33. She heard a strange noise near the window.
34. She looked out of her window.
35. The princess saw the youngest son and his golden goose.
36. Behind him ran five people, all stuck fast together.
37. The parson and the sexton ran and fell down.
38. The three daughters pulled them back up.
39. The princess saw this strange thing and laughed.
40. She laughed and laughed and could not stop.
41. Everyone in the city heard the princess laugh.
42. As soon as the princess laughed, the five people came free.
43. The parson, the sexton, and the three daughters went home.
44. A servant ran to the king.
45. "The princess laughed!" the servant said.
46. The king came out to see the youngest son.
47. He saw the golden goose under the youngest son's arm.
48. The king did not want his daughter to marry him.
49. "You must do one more thing first," said the king.
50. "Bring me a man who is never full."
51. "He must eat and eat and never stop."
52. The youngest son walked back into the forest.
53. He looked for the gray man among the trees.
54. Soon he found the gray man again.
55. "I need a man who eats and eats," said the youngest son.
56. "I will do this myself," said the gray man.
57. "You shared your food with me before," he said.
58. The gray man walked with the youngest son to the palace.
59. At the palace, he ate and ate and never stopped.
60. The king was surprised.
61. "You have done what I asked," said the king.
62. "Now you may marry my daughter."
63. Soon the youngest son and the princess would marry.
64. The wedding was at the king's palace.
65. The golden goose lived with the youngest son.
66. The youngest son became king one day.
