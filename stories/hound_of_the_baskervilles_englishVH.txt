The Hound of the Baskervilles

The Stick
1. It was a gray morning in London, and I sat with Holmes at the table in our room on Baker Street.
2. A stick lay across the table, for a visitor had left it there the night before while we were out.
3. It was a thick stick with a round top, and a band of gold ran around the top.
4. On the band were the words "To Doctor Mortimer, from his friends at the hospital."
5. Holmes sat with his back to me and did not turn his head, but he began to speak.
6. "Watson, you are looking at that stick, so tell me what you think of the man who owns it," he said.
7. "How did you know that I was looking at it?" I asked.
8. "There is a silver pot on the table in front of me, and it shows me your face," Holmes said.
9. I looked at the stick for a long time, and then I told Holmes what I thought.
10. "He is an old doctor from the country, and his friends gave him this stick because they like him," I said.
11. "You are close, Watson, but I think that you are wrong about some of it," Holmes said.
12. "The band says that his friends were at a hospital, so he worked there before he went to the country."
13. "A young doctor leaves a hospital to work in the country, but an old doctor does not, so this man is young."
14. "The end of the stick is worn down, for he walks far."
15. "A doctor in the country must walk far to reach the people who are sick."
16. "Look at the top of the stick, for there are marks of teeth on it."
17. "A dog has carried this stick in its mouth, and I think that the dog is not a small one."
18. "How can you know that?" I asked.
19. "The marks are far apart, so the mouth of the dog is wide," Holmes said.
20. "He is a young doctor with a big dog, and he walks far in the country," I said.
21. "I hear a man on the stairs, and I think that he is the doctor," Holmes said.
22. "I am glad, for I want to ask him about his dog."
23. The door opened, and a tall thin man walked into the room.
24. He was young, with a long nose and gray eyes, and a dog ran in behind him.
25. "I am Doctor Mortimer, and I came here last night, but you were not at home," the man said.
26. "That is my stick, and I am glad to see it again."
27. "You have a big dog, and it carries your stick," Holmes said.
28. "You were a doctor at a hospital, and you left it to work in the country."
29. Doctor Mortimer looked at Holmes with surprise.
30. "Yes, that is true, but how do you know it?" he said.
31. "I have looked at your stick," Holmes said, "and now you must sit down and tell me why you came."
32. Doctor Mortimer sat down and was silent for a moment.
33. "I have come to you because I do not know what to do," he said.

The Old Story
1. Doctor Mortimer took a paper and sat down.
2. "This is an old story about the Baskervilles, and it is told in Devon," he said.
3. "I will read it to you, and then you will know why I came."
4. Holmes sat and did not say a word.
5. Doctor Mortimer read the paper to Holmes and me.
6. In old times Hugo Baskerville lived in the Hall, and he was a cruel man.
7. Near the Hall a girl lived on a farm with her father.
8. Hugo wanted the girl, but she was afraid of him.
9. One night Hugo and his friends took the girl from her father's farm to a room at the top of the Hall.
10. The girl was afraid, and she got out of the room and ran across the moor to her father's farm.
11. The farm was far away.
12. After a time Hugo went up to the room and found that the girl was gone.
13. He ran down to his friends and said, "I will give my soul to the devil if I do not get her!"
14. Hugo and his friends got on their horses, and the hounds ran with them.
15. The men rode out on the moor in the night.
16. On the moor the men met a shepherd, and they asked him if he had seen the girl.
17. "I saw a girl, and Hugo Baskerville rode after her, and a great black hound ran behind him," the shepherd said.
18. "I would not want a hound like that behind me."
19. The men did not stop, and they rode on across the moor.
20. Soon they saw the horse of Hugo run by with no man on its back.
21. The girl lay dead on the moor, for she was afraid and she had run so far.
22. Near her lay Hugo Baskerville, and he was dead.
23. Over him stood a great black hound.
24. The hound had torn out the throat of Hugo, and its eyes were red.
25. The hound looked at the men, and they rode away, and one of them died that night.
26. Doctor Mortimer looked at Holmes.
27. "Men see the hound on the moor, and it kills the men of the Baskerville family," he said.
28. "The paper ends with a warning to the sons: do not go on the moor at night."
29. Holmes looked at Doctor Mortimer and said, "That is a story for a child, but why do you read it to me?"
30. "I read it to you because a Baskerville is dead," Doctor Mortimer said.

Sir Charles
1. "Sir Charles Baskerville was my friend as well as my patient," Doctor Mortimer said.
2. "He was a good man, but his heart was weak, and he knew the story of the hound."
3. "It made him afraid of the moor at night, yet every night he walked alone on a path near the Hall."
4. "The path runs between two rows of old trees, and at the end of it there is a gate that opens onto the moor."
5. "One night he told me that he would go to London in the morning."
6. "That night he went out to his path as he did every night, but he did not come back."
7. "In the morning his servant Barrymore found him dead on the path, and he ran to my house to tell me."
8. "I went with him to the Hall, and there was no mark on Sir Charles."
9. "But his face was the face of a man who had been in great fear."
10. "I said that his heart was weak and that he had died of it, and the other men said the same."
11. "But I looked at the ground near the body and saw a mark, and no one else saw it," Doctor Mortimer said.
12. "What was the mark?" Holmes asked.
13. "It was the mark of a foot, and it was not the foot of a man," Doctor Mortimer said.
14. "The marks were larger than the marks of any hound that I know."
15. "You saw the marks of a hound near the body?" Holmes asked.
16. "Yes, and no one saw them but me," Doctor Mortimer said.
17. Holmes looked at me, and then he looked at Doctor Mortimer for a long time.
18. "You saw this on the night when Sir Charles died, and you have come to me only now," he said.
19. "Why did you not come to me at once?"
20. "I did not want men to say that a hound had killed a Baskerville, for they would have laughed at me," Doctor Mortimer said.
21. "But I have heard more since that night."
22. "Men on the moor have seen a great black hound in the dark, and their story is the story of the paper."
23. "They are afraid, and they will not walk on the moor at night."
24. "Were there other marks on the path?" Holmes asked.
25. "There were the marks of Sir Charles," Doctor Mortimer said.
26. "He stood at the gate for a long time, for I found the ash of his cigar on the ground."
27. "After that he went down the path, and the marks show that he ran."
28. "He ran from the hound," Holmes said, "and he was afraid, and his heart was weak."
29. "I think that is true," Doctor Mortimer said, "but I do not know what to do."

The Heir
1. "Sir Charles had no son," Doctor Mortimer said.
2. "The Hall will go to the son of his brother, and the young man is Sir Henry Baskerville."
3. "Sir Henry is in Canada, and he will come to London tomorrow."
4. "He is the last of the Baskervilles, and I do not want him to die."
5. "He will be at the station at 10 in the morning, and I will meet him."
6. "Must I take him to the Hall?"
7. "Every Baskerville who comes to the Hall is in danger, and Sir Charles is dead."
8. "Do you think the devil has done this?" Holmes asked.
9. "I do not know what to think," Doctor Mortimer said.
10. "Men on the moor have seen the hound, and I saw the marks with my own eyes."
11. "A hound from the devil would not leave the mark of a foot on the path," Holmes said.
12. "If a foot made the marks, then the hound is a hound like other hounds, and a man can find it."
13. "You think that the hound is a real hound?" Doctor Mortimer asked.
14. "I do not know yet," Holmes said, "and I must think about it."
15. "Meet Sir Henry at the station, and tell him nothing."
16. "Take him to a hotel, and bring him here the next day at 12, and I will tell you what to do."
17. "I will come at 12 with Sir Henry," Doctor Mortimer said.
18. "Thank you," Holmes said, and Doctor Mortimer went away.
19. Holmes sat in his chair and smoked all night.
20. In the morning the room was full of smoke, and Holmes was still in his chair.
21. "Watson, I have thought about the hound all night," he said.
22. "What will you do with Sir Henry?" I asked.
23. "I do not know yet, but he must not go to the Hall alone," Holmes said.
24. "I know that a man made the mark, for a hound from the devil would not leave one."
25. "But I do not know who the man is, and I do not know why he wants to kill a Baskerville."

Paragraphs:
1-7: Mortimer says the Hall will go to Sir Henry Baskerville, who is coming from Canada tomorrow, and asks whether he must take him to the Hall, where Baskervilles die.
8-12: Holmes asks if the devil did this. Mortimer does not know what to think, and Holmes says that a hound that leaves marks is a real hound that a man can find.

19-25: Holmes smokes all night. In the morning he says that a man must be behind the hound and that Sir Henry must not go to the Hall alone, though he does not know who the man is or why he wants to kill.

The Letter
1. Two days later, at 12, Doctor Mortimer came back to Baker Street with a young man.
2. The young man was Sir Henry Baskerville, a strong man with black eyes and a brown face, and he had lived in Canada.
3. "Sir Henry, I am Sherlock Holmes, and this is my friend Doctor Watson," Holmes said.
4. "Something strange happened to me this morning, and I want you to see it," Sir Henry said.
5. He took a letter from his coat and gave it to Holmes, and Holmes held it up to the window.
6. The letter was not written by hand, for a man had cut the words from a newspaper and put them on a paper.
7. The words said, "If you want to live, keep away from the moor."
8. "The man cut these words from the newspaper of yesterday," Holmes said.
9. "The word moor was not in the newspaper, so he wrote that word himself."
10. "There is a faint smell of scent on the paper, and it is a scent that a woman wears," Holmes said.
11. "Who knows that I am in London?" Sir Henry asked.
12. "I came here yesterday, and I have spent only one night at the hotel."
13. "Only Doctor Mortimer and I knew that you were coming," Holmes said.
14. "Someone has followed you to the hotel, and he does not want you to go to the Hall."
15. "There is one more thing," Sir Henry said.
16. "Last night I put my new brown boots outside the door of my room, and this morning one of them was gone."
17. "A man does not take one boot for nothing," Holmes said, and he looked at the letter again.
18. "I do not like this, Sir Henry, and I think that you are in danger."
19. "I am not afraid," Sir Henry said, "but I want to know who this man is and what he wants."
20. "What do you know about the Baskervilles and the moor?" Holmes asked.
21. "Nothing at all, for I have lived in Canada all my life."
22. "But Doctor Mortimer has told me that a hound killed my uncle, and I want to see the Hall."
23. Holmes looked at me, and then he looked at Sir Henry for some time.
24. "You will go to the Hall, Sir Henry, for it is your home, and I cannot tell you to stay away."
25. "But someone wants you to keep away from the moor, and you must not go there alone."
26. "Go to your hotel and eat your lunch, and say nothing about the letter."
27. "I will come to see you there this afternoon."

The Cab
1. Sir Henry and Doctor Mortimer had gone to the hotel to have lunch.
2. Holmes put on his coat and said, "Quick, Watson, we must follow them."
3. "Why?" I asked.
4. "Someone followed Sir Henry to the hotel, and I think he will follow him again," Holmes said.
5. We went down to the street, and we saw the two men walk away.
6. "Look, Watson, a cab is behind them," Holmes said.
7. A man sat in the cab, and he had a thick black beard.
8. His eyes were on Sir Henry, and the cab went slowly down the street.
9. Holmes ran into the street, and another cab stopped for him.
10. "Follow that cab!" Holmes said to the driver, and we got in.
11. "Who is the man, Holmes?" I asked.
12. "I do not know, but he wants to watch Sir Henry," Holmes said.
13. The first cab followed Sir Henry and Doctor Mortimer to the hotel and stopped down the street.
14. The man with the beard sat and looked at the door of the hotel.
15. After a moment he looked back and saw Holmes, and he said something to his driver.
16. The cab went away fast, and we did not catch it.
17. "I have seen the number of the cab, and it is 2704," Holmes said.
18. We went into the hotel and found Sir Henry with Doctor Mortimer.
19. Sir Henry was angry, and he had a boot in his hand.
20. "A man at the hotel has found my new brown boot, but now my old black boot is gone," he said.
21. "Someone took it in the night, and I do not know why."
22. "The man at the hotel says that he knows nothing about it," Doctor Mortimer said.
23. "First he took a new boot, and now he has taken an old one," Holmes said.
24. "I do not know why he wants them, Sir Henry, but I will find out."

The Train
1. That night the driver of the cab came to our room, for Holmes had sent for him.
2. "The man was a tall man with a black beard, and he told me to follow the two men," the driver said.
3. "I asked his name, and he said that his name was Sherlock Holmes."
4. Holmes laughed and said, "He is a clever man, Watson."
5. "He knew that we would follow him, and he wanted us to know his name."
6. "Who is he, and why does he follow Sir Henry?" I asked.
7. "I do not know," Holmes said, "but I think he has followed Sir Henry since the day he came to London."
8. "Someone wants him to stay away from the moor, and someone wants to watch him."
9. "You must go with Sir Henry to the Hall, Watson, and you must find this man."
10. "I will go on Saturday with Sir Henry and Doctor Mortimer," I said.
11. On Saturday Holmes came with us to the station.
12. "Do not leave Sir Henry alone on the moor at night, Watson," he said.
13. "Trust no one, and send me a letter every day."
14. The train went out of London, and I looked at Holmes until I could not see him.
15. In the afternoon we came to a small station, and two soldiers stood there with guns.
16. A driver took our bags and put them in a carriage.
17. "Why are the soldiers here?" Sir Henry asked.
18. "A man has run away from the prison at Princetown," the driver said.

21. "The farmers are afraid, for they know that he is a bad man."
22. "I am not afraid of one man," Sir Henry said.
23. "But I do not like the moor at night, and I think it is a bad place."
24. We got into the carriage, and the driver took us out onto the moor.

Baskerville Hall
1. The road went up and down, and the moor was all around us.
2. The moor was brown and gray, and the sun went down behind the hills.
3. "There are no houses here, and no one walks on this moor at night," Doctor Mortimer said.
4. Sir Henry looked at the moor for a long time and said nothing.
5. On a hill we saw a soldier on a horse, and he had a gun.
6. At last the carriage came to a gate, and we went in, and there was the Hall.
7. The Hall was a great old house with two towers, and it was dark.
8. Barrymore and his wife came out of the door to meet us.
9. Barrymore had a thick black beard, and he took our bags from the carriage.
10. "We are glad that you have come to the Hall, Sir Henry," Barrymore said.
11. Doctor Mortimer said good night and went to his house, and we went into the Hall.
12. The room was dark, and there were pictures of dead Baskervilles on the walls.
13. Sir Henry looked at the faces of his family for a long time.
14. "This is not a good house, Watson, and I do not like it," Sir Henry said.
15. "Sir Charles lived here alone, and he was afraid of the moor."
16. "I know why."
17. "Sir Henry, my wife and I want to leave the Hall now," Barrymore said.
18. "Sir Charles was good to us, and he left us gold when he died."
19. "We can have a small house now, and the Hall is not the same, for Sir Charles is dead."
20. "Stay here for some time," Sir Henry said, "for I want you."
21. "We will stay, Sir Henry," Barrymore said.
22. We ate that night in the great room, and then we went to our rooms.
23. I could not sleep, and I lay in the dark.

2. It was the sound of a woman who was crying.
3. I got up and listened at the door, but the sound was far away, and I could not tell where it was.

The Man with the Net
1. I walked on the moor for an hour, and I saw no one.
2. A man came after me, and he ran across the moor with a net in his hand.
3. He was a thin man with light hair, and he looked at me with a smile.

5. "I am Stapleton, and I live at Merripit House on the moor."
6. "Doctor Mortimer is my friend, and he told me that you have come to the Hall," Stapleton said.
7. "I catch butterflies on the moor, and I know every part of it."
8. "Is it a good place to walk?" I asked.
9. "It is a bad place, if you do not know it," Stapleton said.
10. "Look at that green place, Doctor Watson, for that is the Grimpen Mire."

24. "Come to Merripit House, and I will show you my butterflies."

The Woman on the Moor
1. We walked along a path across the moor, and Stapleton spoke of his butterflies.
2. A woman ran after us, and she was a young woman with dark hair and dark eyes.
3. She did not look at Stapleton, for her eyes were on me.
4. "Go back!" she said, and her voice was low and fast.
5. "Go back to London at once, and do not stay on this moor."
6. "Why must I go back?" I asked.
7. "I cannot tell you," she said, "but you must go tonight."
8. "Please go now, Sir Henry, and do not come to the moor again."
9. "Why is the moor a bad place for me?" I asked.
10. "I cannot say more," she said, and she looked at the moor.
11. Stapleton came up to us, and his face was red.
12. "Beryl, what are you saying?" Stapleton said.
13. "I made a mistake, Jack," the woman said.

The Hound
1. We waited behind the rocks for a long time, and the night grew colder.
2. Below us the fog lay on the mire, and slowly it came up toward the path.
3. "If the fog comes up to the path, we will not see Sir Henry," I said.
4. "We will hear the hound before we see it," Holmes said, and he did not speak again.
5. Lestrade held his gun in both hands and looked at the door of Merripit House.
6. At 10 o'clock the door of the house opened, and Sir Henry came out.
7. He walked along the path toward us, and he did not know that we were watching him.
8. The fog was thick now, and I could see only the lights of the house behind him.
9. Lestrade put his hand on my arm and whispered, "Do you hear that?"
10. I heard the feet of a big animal running fast on the stones, and the sound came from behind Sir Henry.

13. It was larger than any hound that I had ever seen, and it ran without a sound.
14. Sir Henry looked back and cried out, and then he ran along the path.
15. The hound ran after him, and it was faster than he was.

18. The hound ran on until it was close behind Sir Henry, and Holmes ran onto the path and shot again.

20. For a moment no one spoke, and we heard only the fog and our own breath.

26. He was alive, but he could not speak, and his face was white.
27. "He is safe, and the hound will not hurt him now," Lestrade said.
28. Holmes looked at the dead hound, and then he looked at Merripit House.
29. "We have the hound," he said, "but where is the man?"
