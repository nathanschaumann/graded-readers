Gulliver's Travels

The Storm at Sea
1. Gulliver was a man from England.
2. He went to sea on great ships, for he wanted to see far lands.
3. His ship sailed for many days over the wide sea.
4. A storm came in the night, and the wind drove the ship through the dark.
5. Waves came over the ship, and the masts fell.
6. The ship hit rocks, and the sea came in.
7. The men could not hold the ship on the water.
8. Gulliver and 6 of the men got away in a small boat.
9. But a great wave threw the boat over in the dark.
10. The boat and the other men were gone.
11. Gulliver could not see the land, but he swam on through the waves.
12. He swam and swam, and his arms grew weak.
13. His feet found ground under the water.
14. He walked through the shallow sea to a dark shore.
15. He saw no houses and no fire on the shore.
16. The grass under his feet was soft.
17. He lay down on the grass and slept.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Gulliver, a man from England who wants to see far lands, sails for many days until a storm catches the ship in the night.
5-10: The storm breaks the masts and drives the ship onto rocks; the men cannot hold the ship, and Gulliver and six others escape in a small boat, but a wave throws it over and the other men are lost.
11-15: Gulliver swims on without seeing land until his arms grow weak, finds ground under his feet, and walks through shallow water to a dark shore with no houses and no fire.
16-17: Too tired to look around, he lies down on the soft grass and sleeps all night, alone on a strange shore.

Tied to the Ground
1. Day came, and Gulliver woke.
2. He lay on the grass, but he could not move.
3. His arms, his legs, and his head were tied down.
4. More than 600 thin threads held him to the ground.
5. Small feet walked up his leg.
6. They came up over his chest, man after man.
7. Gulliver looked down and saw a man no taller than his hand.
8. The little man pointed an arrow at Gulliver's face.
9. 40 more little men came up and walked over him, from legs to head.
10. The men ran down to the ground when Gulliver shouted.
11. Gulliver pulled, and one arm came free of the threads.
12. 100 arrows, small as needles, came down on his hand.
13. The little arrows hurt, but they could not go deep.
14. When Gulliver moved his arm, more arrows came down.
15. He lay still on the grass and waited.
16. A lord of the little men climbed up on Gulliver's chest and spoke.
17. Gulliver could not understand the words.
18. Gulliver put one free hand to his mouth to show he wanted food.
19. The little people understood, and they were not afraid of the giant.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Gulliver wakes on the grass and finds he cannot move: his arms, legs, and head are tied down by hundreds of thin threads.
5-9: Small feet walk up his leg and over his chest, and Gulliver sees a man no taller than his hand pointing an arrow at his face, while forty more little men walk over him.
10-15: The men run down when Gulliver shouts; he pulls one arm free, but arrows small as needles come down on his hand, so he lies still and waits.
16-19: To the little men Gulliver is a giant; a lord climbs up and speaks words Gulliver cannot understand, and when Gulliver signs that he is hungry, the people are no longer afraid.

The Great Cart
1. Soon the little people brought food to the giant.
2. 100 men carried meat and bread up to Gulliver's mouth.
3. Gulliver ate it all and wanted more.
4. The people brought him a strange drink, and it was sweet.
5. Gulliver drank and drank, and soon he slept.
6. The little people had put a sleep powder in the sweet drink.
7. They wanted to take the giant to the great city in his sleep.
8. The people of the land built a great cart for the giant.
9. 900 men with ropes lifted Gulliver up on the cart.
10. 1500 small horses pulled the cart, and the horses were smaller than a hand.
11. They pulled the great cart for a night and a day to the city.
12. In the city was an old temple, and no house in the land was as great.
13. The people put 91 small chains on one of Gulliver's legs, and the chains held him.
14. When Gulliver woke, he could stand up and walk, but not far.
15. People came from all the land to look at the giant in the chains.
16. At night, Gulliver lay down in the old temple and slept.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The little people bring the giant meat and bread, which he eats hungrily, and a sweet drink with a sleep powder hidden in it, so Gulliver falls asleep.
7-11: To move the sleeping giant, the people build a great cart; 900 men lift him onto it with ropes, and 1500 tiny horses pull the cart to the city for a night and a day.
12-16: At an old temple, the greatest house in the land, they fix 91 small chains to Gulliver's leg; he wakes able to stand and walk a little, people come from all over to look at him, and at night he sleeps in the temple.

The King and the Pockets
1. The king of the land came to see the giant.
2. The land was called Lilliput.
3. The king stood a little taller than his lords.
4. He looked at the giant's face, at his hands, and at his feet.
5. The king told the farmers of the land to bring the giant food for 1000 men.
6. Each day, 40 carts of meat and bread came to the temple, and Gulliver ate it all.
7. In 30 days, Gulliver learned to speak the words of Lilliput.
8. Little people climbed up Gulliver's arm and stood on his hand.
9. "Set me free," Gulliver asked the king one day.
10. The king said that two lords must first look in Gulliver's pockets.
11. Gulliver lifted the two lords up in one hand to his pockets.
12. The lords found a knife and gold coins.
13. In one more pocket, the lords found Gulliver's watch.
14. The watch made a small noise over and over.
15. The lords stood on Gulliver's hand to look at the watch.
16. They thought the watch was the god of the giant.
17. The king let Gulliver have it all.
18. The king said Gulliver could be free after one more promise.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The king of Lilliput, a little taller than his lords, comes to the temple and looks the giant over from head to feet.
5-7: The king orders the farmers to bring food each day, and in thirty days Gulliver learns to speak the words of Lilliput, asking the people for more words every day.
8-11: The little people grow to trust Gulliver, climbing up to stand and laugh on his hand; when he asks the king to set him free, the king first sends two lords to look in his pockets, and Gulliver lifts them up himself.
12-18: The lords find a knife, gold coins, and a watch that makes a small noise over and over, which they think is the giant's god; the king lets Gulliver keep everything and says one more promise will make him free.

The Promise and the Egg War
1. The king came again to the temple with his men.
2. "I will walk with care in your city," said Gulliver.
3. "And I will help the land of Lilliput in war," he said.
4. The king's men took the 91 chains from Gulliver's leg.
5. He walked with great care over the little city.
6. He went over the city wall, for the wall was smaller than his leg.
7. Across the sea lay one more land, Blefuscu.
8. From the wall, Gulliver could see the ships of Blefuscu on the sea.
9. The two little lands were enemies.
10. Lilliput and Blefuscu had fought for years over which end of an egg to break first.
11. "The ships of Blefuscu will soon come over the sea," the king said to Gulliver.
12. "50 great ships of war lie on the sea at Blefuscu."
13. "Will you help Lilliput, as you promised?" asked the king.
14. Gulliver carried the king to the sea in his hand.
15. The sea between the two lands was not deep for a giant.
16. "I will bring you the ships of Blefuscu," said Gulliver.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Gulliver promises the king to walk with care in the city and to help Lilliput in war, and the king has the 91 chains taken from his leg.
5-10: Free at last, Gulliver walks carefully over the little city and crosses the low city wall; across the sea lies Blefuscu, an enemy land whose ships he can see from the wall, for the two lands have fought for years over which end of an egg to break first.
11-16: The king says fifty ships of war lie ready at Blefuscu and asks Gulliver to keep his promise; Gulliver carries the king down to the sea, sees the water between the lands is not deep for a giant, and promises to bring back the enemy ships, and the king shouts and laughs in his hand.

Gulliver Takes the Ships
1. The men of Lilliput brought iron, and Gulliver made 50 great hooks.
2. He tied one of the 50 ropes to each hook.
3. Gulliver walked into the sea between the two lands.
4. In the deep water, the sea came up over his chest.
5. He held the hooks and ropes over the water.
6. The men of Blefuscu saw the giant come through the sea.
7. They jumped from their ships into the sea and swam to the shore.
8. 1000 arrows came down on Gulliver's hands and face.
9. The little arrows hurt, but Gulliver walked on.
10. At the ships of Blefuscu, he put one hook on each ship.
11. But the ships did not move, for the anchors held.
12. Gulliver took his knife and cut the anchor ropes one by one.
13. He held the ends of all 50 ropes in one hand.
14. Gulliver turned and pulled the 50 ships after him through the sea.
15. The king of Lilliput stood on his shore and watched the ships come.
16. "One man took all the ships of Blefuscu!" said the king.
17. He made Gulliver the first lord of Lilliput that day.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Gulliver makes fifty iron hooks, ties a rope to each, and walks into the sea between the two lands, holding the hooks and ropes over the water where the sea comes up over his chest.
6-9: The men of Blefuscu jump from their ships and swim ashore when they see the giant coming, and though a thousand little arrows hurt his hands and face, Gulliver walks on.
10-14: Gulliver puts a hook on each ship, cuts the anchor ropes with his knife when the anchors hold the ships fast, gathers all fifty ropes in one hand, and pulls the ships after him through the sea.
15-17: The people of Blefuscu shout from their shore while the king of Lilliput watches the ships come; the king says one man has taken all the ships of Blefuscu, makes Gulliver the first lord of the land, and that night the people make great fires on the shore.

The Fire in the Palace
1. The king of Blefuscu sent men over the sea to ask for peace.
2. Lilliput and Blefuscu made peace, and the war of the eggs was over.
3. But the king of Lilliput wanted more than peace.
4. "Take all of Blefuscu for me," the king said to Gulliver.
5. "Their land will be my land, and their people will be my people."
6. Gulliver looked down at the little king.
7. "No," said Gulliver.
8. "The people of Blefuscu are a free people, and I will not take their land."
9. The king said no more, but from that day he did not laugh with Gulliver.
10. One night, the people of the city came calling to the temple.
11. "Fire, fire!" the people called.
12. "The queen's rooms are on fire!"
13. Gulliver ran to the palace with great care for the little houses.
14. The people threw little buckets of water on the fire, but the fire grew.
15. The sea was not far, and Gulliver had his great hat.
16. He filled the hat with sea water and threw the water on the fire.
17. He went to the sea and came back, again and again.
18. Soon the fire was out, and the palace still stood.
19. But sea water lay over all the rooms of the queen.
20. The queen did not laugh with Gulliver again.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Blefuscu asks for peace and the egg war ends, but the king of Lilliput wants more: he tells Gulliver to take all of Blefuscu so that its land and people will be his.
6-9: Gulliver looks down at the little king and says no, for the people of Blefuscu are a free people; the king says no more, but from that day he does not laugh with Gulliver.

Flight to Blefuscu
1. After the fire, some lords of the court wanted the giant gone.
2. "The giant eats the food of 1000 men," said one lord.
3. "He would not take Blefuscu for his king," said the queen.
4. The court said the giant must be tied down again, for all his days.
5. But one lord of the court was Gulliver's friend.
6. That night, the friend came alone in the dark to the temple.
7. The giant put the friend up on his hand.
8. "The court will put you in chains again," said the friend.
9. "Go from Lilliput this night."
10. Gulliver did not want a war with the little people of the land.
11. He thanked the friend and put him down on the ground.
12. In the dark, Gulliver walked into the sea and went over to Blefuscu.
13. The king of Blefuscu came to the shore with many of his little people.
14. He was glad to see the man that had not taken his land.
15. "You will be safe in Blefuscu," said the king of Blefuscu.
16. The people of Blefuscu brought the giant food each day.
17. But at night, Gulliver looked over the sea and wanted to go home to England.

The Real Boat
1. One day, Gulliver saw a real boat far out on the sea.
2. It was a boat from a great ship, and the sea had turned it over.
3. The wind drove the boat into the shallow water, and Gulliver swam out to it.
4. With 2000 men of Blefuscu and many ropes, he turned the boat over in the water.
5. The king of Blefuscu gave Gulliver wood, and soon the boat was good again.
6. The people of Blefuscu brought meat, bread, and milk for the long way home.
7. Gulliver took 6 little cows of Blefuscu into the boat too.
8. He wanted to show England the little cows.
9. Gulliver thanked the king of Blefuscu and sailed away.
10. After two days on the sea, Gulliver saw a great ship.
11. It was a ship from England, with men as great as Gulliver.
12. The men of the ship took Gulliver and his boat up from the sea.
13. Gulliver told the captain of Lilliput, and the captain laughed.
14. Gulliver went to his boat and took out one little cow.
15. He put the little cow in the captain's hand.
16. The captain looked at it and laughed no more.
17. The ship sailed home, and Gulliver came to his house in England.
18. His wife had thought the sea had taken him, and she laughed and cried to see him.
19. Gulliver stayed at home for 60 days.
20. But he still thought of the sea and far lands.

The Land of the Giants
1. When the 60 days were over, a new ship sailed from England.
2. Gulliver went with it, for he still wanted to see far lands.
3. Far out on the sea, a great storm drove the ship for many days.
4. No man on the ship knew the way home.
5. When the storm was over, the men saw a strange land.
6. The men wanted water, so the captain sent a boat to the shore.
7. Gulliver went with the boat and walked alone over the strange land.
8. He walked a mile from the shore and saw no man and no house.
9. When Gulliver came back, the boat was far out on the water.
10. The men in the boat rowed fast for the ship.
11. A giant man walked in the sea after the boat.
12. The sea came only to the giant's knees.
13. The giant was as tall as a ship's mast.
14. Gulliver called from the shore, but the boat did not come back.
15. He ran from the sea into the land.
16. Gulliver came to a great field of yellow grain.
17. The grain stood far over his head, as tall as the trees of England.
18. He heard great feet come through the grain, and he hid and waited.

The Giant Farmer
1. Giant men came through the field and cut down the tall grain.
2. Great feet came down in the tall grain by Gulliver's head.
3. Gulliver called and called up at the giant men.
4. The great feet stood still in the grain.
5. The farmer of the field looked down and saw the little man in the grain.
6. The farmer took Gulliver up between his finger and his thumb.
7. He lifted the little man high over the field to look at him.
8. Gulliver held the great thumb, for the ground was far down.
9. Gulliver bowed in the great hand and spoke, but the farmer could not understand the little words.
10. The farmer put Gulliver in his pocket and carried him home.
11. At the house, the farmer took Gulliver from the pocket and put him on the table.
12. The farmer's wife saw the little man, and her cup fell from her hand.
13. But Gulliver bowed to her too, and soon she laughed.
14. The farmer and his wife sat down at the table with bread and milk.
15. Gulliver drank milk from the smallest cup of the house, and the cup was as great as a bucket.
16. He fell over the bread on the table, and the giants laughed.
17. The farmer's daughter watched the little man with care.
18. That night, the daughter put soft cloth in a small box for Gulliver's bed.
19. In the box bed, Gulliver slept his first night in the land of the giants.

The Farmer's Daughter
1. The farmer's daughter took care of Gulliver in the great house.
2. She made him new clothes from soft cloth.
3. She set his food by his box bed and kept him safe.
4. Each day, the daughter gave Gulliver more words of the land.
5. She pointed at the bread, the milk, the cup, and the fire, and said each word.
6. He learned fast, for he had learned the words of Lilliput too.
7. Soon the little man could speak with the daughter.
8. "This land is Brobdingnag," said the daughter.
9. Gulliver told the daughter of England and of little Lilliput.
10. One day, an old farmer came to the house to see the little man.
11. Gulliver walked on the table, bowed, and spoke to him.
12. The old farmer looked and looked at the little man.
13. "Show the little man at the market for money," the old farmer said.
14. The farmer of the house heard him and thought of the money.
15. "The little man will go to the market," said the farmer of the house.
16. The daughter was afraid for Gulliver, so she said she would go too.
17. The daughter set more soft cloth in Gulliver's box for the market.

10. After that, the farmer showed Gulliver at market after market.
11. They went from town to town for many weeks.
12. He could not rest, and he could not sleep.
13. Gulliver grew thin and tired and weak.
14. In the end, the farmer took Gulliver to the great city of the land.
15. The king and queen of the giants lived there, in a palace of high walls.
16. Soon the queen heard of the little man and called for him to the palace.

15. The daughter lifted Gulliver out and made him dry with soft cloth.
16. The queen was angry at the dwarf.
17. She sent the dwarf away from the court that day.

The Monkey on the Roof
1. One warm morning, the queen called the daughter away.
2. The window of Gulliver's little room stood open to the garden.
3. A monkey lived in the trees of the palace garden.
4. The monkey came down, ran over the wall, and climbed in at the open window.
5. It found Gulliver in the little room.
6. The monkey took Gulliver up in one arm, as monkeys hold their young.
7. Gulliver saw the great teeth and could not get free.
8. It put food from its mouth into Gulliver's mouth.
9. The monkey jumped out of the window with Gulliver and climbed up the palace wall.
10. It ran up and up, to the top of the palace roof.
11. The garden lay far below the little man.
12. Great screams came from the garden below.
13. The daughter ran out and called for the little man.
14. Men came with long ladders and climbed to the roof.
15. The monkey saw the men, set Gulliver down on the roof, and ran away over the wall.
16. One of the queen's men put Gulliver in his pocket and climbed down the ladder.
17. The daughter held Gulliver, and her great tears fell on him.
18. Gulliver was sick for two days from the monkey's food.
19. After that day, the window of the little room stood open no more.

Paragraphs:
1-4: One warm morning the queen calls the daughter away, and while the window of Gulliver's room stands open, a monkey from the palace garden climbs in.
5-8: The monkey takes Gulliver up in one arm the way monkeys hold their young; Gulliver sees the great teeth and cannot get free, and the monkey even puts food from its mouth into his.
9-12: The monkey jumps out of the window, climbs the palace wall to the top of the roof with the garden far below, and people scream when they see the monkey and the man.
13-19: The daughter cries out while men climb with long ladders; the monkey sets Gulliver down and runs away, a queen's man carries Gulliver down in his pocket, the daughter's great tears fall on him, and after two days of being sick from the monkey's food, Gulliver finds the window is never left open again.

The Eagle and the Box
1. One day, the king and queen went down to the sea and took Gulliver too.
2. Gulliver had not seen the sea for two years, and he wanted to see the waves again.
3. But that day the daughter was sick and could not come down to the shore.
4. A boy carried Gulliver's box down to the rocks by the sea.
5. Gulliver was tired, and he lay down on the bed in the box.
6. The boy set the box on the rocks and walked away to look for birds' eggs.
7. Gulliver woke when great wings came down over the box.
8. All at once, the box rose fast into the air.
9. An eagle had taken the box by the great iron ring.
10. The eagle flew high over the sea with the box.
11. Gulliver looked out of the window and saw only air and sea.
12. More great wings came, and other birds flew at the eagle.
13. The eagle let the box go.
14. The box fell down and down through the air.
15. It hit the sea and went under, but the box came up again.
16. The box was good dry wood, and it did not sink.
17. Gulliver held fast in the box and was not hurt.
18. The waves lifted the box up and down on the great sea.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The king and queen go down to the sea and take Gulliver, who has not seen the waves for two years; the daughter is sick that day, so a boy carries the box to the rocks, where tired Gulliver lies down while the boy walks away to look for birds' eggs.
7-13: Gulliver wakes to great wings over the box, and all at once it rises fast into the air: an eagle has taken it by the iron ring and flies high over the sea, until other birds fly at the eagle and it lets the box go.
14-18: The box falls down and down, hits the sea, and comes up again, for it is good dry wood; Gulliver holds fast and is not hurt, and alone on the great sea the box rises and falls on the waves while he waits.

Home for Good
1. Men on a great ship of England saw the box on the waves.
2. Sailors came out with hooks and ropes.
3. The sailors put the hooks in the great ring and pulled the box to the ship.
4. Gulliver called from the box, and the sailors answered him.
5. The sailors cut a hole in the roof of the box.
6. Gulliver came out of the box into the wind of the sea.
7. The captain gave him hot food and hot drink.
8. Gulliver told the captain of the land of the giants.
9. The captain laughed, as the other captain had laughed at the little cows.
10. But the sailors looked at the box, at the ring, and at the strange wood.
11. "No man of England made this box," said the captain.
12. The ship sailed for England, week after week.
13. At home in England, Gulliver's wife laughed and cried again to see him.
14. But the people of England looked strange and small to Gulliver.
15. He walked with great care in the town, for the men looked small as children.
16. He bent his head at his own little door, for he had lived with giants.
17. His wife kissed him and asked him to go to sea no more.
18. Gulliver stayed home with his wife for good.
19. At night, by the fire, he told his children of little Lilliput and the giants of Brobdingnag.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Men on a ship of England see the box on the waves; sailors row out with hooks and ropes, pull the box in by its great ring, answer Gulliver's calls, and cut a hole in the roof so he can come out into the wind of the sea.
7-11: The captain gives Gulliver hot food and drink but laughs at his story of the giants, as the other captain once laughed at the little cows; then the sailors look at the great box with its ring and strange wood, and the captain says no man of England made it.
12-16: The ship sails for England, week after week, and at home Gulliver's wife laughs and cries again to see him; but for a time the people of England look strange and small, so Gulliver walks in the town with great care and bends his head at his own door.
17-19: His wife kisses him and asks him to go to sea no more; Gulliver stays home for good and never goes out on the great sea again, but at night, by the fire, he tells his children of little Lilliput and the giants of Brobdingnag.
