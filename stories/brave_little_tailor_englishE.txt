The Brave Little Tailor

Seven at One Blow
1. A little tailor sat in his small shop.
2. He sewed at his table.
3. One morning, he spread jam on fresh bread.
4. Flies smelled the jam.
5. Seven flies flew to his bread.
6. The tailor grabbed a big cloth from the corner.
7. He hit the flies hard.
8. Seven flies fell down dead.
9. The tailor saw seven dead flies on the table.
10. "Seven at one blow!" said the tailor.
11. He smiled at what he had done.
12. No one else saw it.
13. The tailor wanted the world to know.

Paragraphs:
1-6: A little tailor sews in his small shop, and one morning he spreads jam on fresh bread; the smell draws flies, and seven fly to his bread, so he grabs a big cloth from the corner.
7-13: He hits the flies hard and kills seven at once, then sees the dead flies and declares, "Seven at one blow!" smiling at what he has done; though no one else saw it, the tailor wanted the world to know.

A New Belt
1. The tailor sewed a belt from cloth.
2. He sewed words on the belt.
3. The words said, "Seven at one blow."
4. The little tailor put on his belt.
5. He walked out into the street.
6. People in the street read the words on his belt.
7. People believed the tailor had killed seven men.
8. No one knew the seven were only flies.
9. He wanted to see the big world.
10. "I will see the world," the tailor said.
11. He took bread and cheese for the road.
12. A bird came to him.
13. He grabbed the bird and put it in his coat.
14. The tailor walked out to see the world.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The tailor sews a belt from cloth and sews words on it that read, "Seven at one blow," then puts on his belt.
5-10: He walks out into the street, where people read the words and believe he has killed seven men, though the seven were only flies, and he wants to see the big world, saying he will see the world.
11-14: He takes bread and cheese for the road; a bird comes to him, and he grabs it, puts it in his coat, and walks out to see the world.

The Giant in the Forest
1. The tailor walked into a big forest.
2. A giant sat by the road.
3. The giant looked down at the little tailor.
4. "Who are you?" the giant asked.
5. The tailor showed the giant his belt.
6. The giant read the words on the belt.
7. The giant believed the tailor had killed seven men.
8. "Show me you are strong," the giant said.
9. The giant picked up a stone and squeezed it hard.
10. Water dripped from the stone.
11. "You try," the giant said.
12. The tailor took a cheese from his coat.
13. He squeezed the cheese hard.
14. Water ran from the cheese to the ground.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The tailor walks into a big forest, where a giant sits by the road; the giant looks down at the little tailor and asks who he is.
5-8: The tailor shows the giant his belt, and the giant reads the words and believes he has killed seven men, so the giant challenges him to show his strength.
9-14: The giant picks up a stone and squeezes it hard until water drips out, then tells the tailor to try; the tailor takes a cheese from his coat, squeezes it hard, and water runs from the cheese to the ground.

The Bird That Never Came Down
1. The giant picked up a big stone.
2. He threw the stone into the trees.
3. The stone flew and fell to the ground.
4. "Try again," the giant said.
5. The tailor took a small bird from his coat.
6. He threw the bird up.
7. The bird flew away and did not come down.
8. The giant looked up at the empty trees.
9. He moved back from the little tailor.
10. The giant believed the tailor had killed seven men.
11. He had another plan for the tailor.
12. "Come to my cave for the night," the giant said.
13. "I will come," said the tailor.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The giant picks up a big stone and throws it into the trees; it flies and falls to the ground, and the giant tells the tailor to try again.
5-8: The tailor takes a small bird from his coat and throws it up, and the bird flies away and does not come down; the giant looks up at the empty trees.
9-13: The giant moves back from the little tailor, believing he has killed seven men, and makes another plan for him, telling the tailor to come to his cave for the night; the tailor agrees to come.

A Night Among Giants
1. The giant took the tailor into the cave.
2. Other giants sat by a fire.
3. The giants gave the tailor a bed.
4. But the bed was too big for the little tailor.
5. He did not lie in the middle of the bed.
6. He slept in a corner.
7. At night, a giant came to the bed.
8. He wanted to kill the tailor.
9. The giant held a big bar.
10. He hit the middle of the bed hard.
11. But the bed was empty.
12. In the morning, the tailor walked out of the cave.
13. The other giants saw him alive.
14. The giants believed no one could kill the tailor.
15. They ran away into the forest.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The giant takes the tailor into the cave, where other giants sit by a fire; the giants give the tailor a bed, but it is too big for the little tailor.
5-9: He does not lie in the middle of the bed and sleeps in a corner instead; at night, a giant comes to the bed, wanting to kill the tailor, and holds a big bar.
10-15: He hits the middle of the bed hard, but the bed is empty; in the morning, the tailor walks out of the cave, and the other giants see him alive and believe no one could kill him, so they run away into the forest.

The King's New Soldier
1. The little tailor walked to a big city.
2. The king lived in the city.
3. People told the king about the tailor.
4. They told him about the belt.
5. The belt said, "Seven at one blow."
6. The king believed the tailor had killed seven men.
7. "I want this tailor in my army," said the king.
8. The king sent for the tailor.
9. The tailor came to the king's castle.
10. He showed the king his belt.
11. "You may join my army," the king said.
12. Strong soldiers saw the tailor at the castle.
13. They read his belt and were afraid.
14. The soldiers did not want to fight him.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The tailor walks to a big city, where the king lives among many people, and word of the tailor reaches the king, who hears about his belt.
5-9: The belt reads, "Seven at one blow," so the king believes the tailor has killed seven men and wants him in the army, sending for him; the tailor comes to the castle.
10-14: He shows the king his belt, and the king says he may join the army, but soldiers who see him at the castle read the belt, are afraid, and do not want to fight him.

A Plan to Get Rid of Him
1. The soldiers went to the king.
2. "This tailor is too dangerous," they said.
3. "He may kill one if he is angry," a soldier said.
4. The king still needed his army, but not an angry tailor.
5. The king thought and asked his men what to do.
6. "Give him a very hard task," one man said.
7. "Two giants live in a dark forest," the man said.
8. "They kill people in the kingdom," he said.
9. "Send the tailor to kill the two giants," the man said.
10. The king wanted this plan.
11. The king sent for the tailor again.
12. "Kill the two giants in the dark forest," the king said.
13. "If you do this, you may marry my daughter," the king said.
14. "You will have half my kingdom," the king said.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The soldiers go to the king and say this tailor is too dangerous, since he may kill one of them if he is angry; the king still needs his army, but not an angry tailor.
5-9: The king asks his men what to do, and one man suggests giving the tailor a very hard task — killing two giants who live in a dark forest and kill people in the kingdom.
10-14: The king wants this plan and sends for the tailor again, telling him to kill the two giants in the dark forest; if he does this, he may marry the king's daughter and will have half the kingdom.

The Two Giants
1. The tailor walked to the dark forest.
2. He saw two giants by a big tree.
3. The two giants were still asleep.
4. The tailor climbed the big tree.
5. He sat on a branch.
6. The tailor had stones with him.
7. He wanted to trick the two giants.
8. If the giants fought, they could kill each other.
9. The tailor picked up a stone.
10. He threw the stone at the first giant.
11. The stone hit the first giant hard.
12. The first giant was angry.
13. "You hit me!" the first giant said.
14. "I did not hit you," the second giant said.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The tailor walks to the dark forest, where he sees two giants asleep by a big tree; he climbs the tree and sits on a branch.
6-9: The tailor has stones with him and wants to trick the two giants, thinking that if they fought, they could kill each other, so he picks up a stone.
10-14: He throws the stone at the first giant, and it hits him hard; the first giant is angry and says, "You hit me!" but the second giant says he did not hit him.

The Giants Fight
1. It was morning now.
2. "You hit me," the first giant said again.
3. The tailor dropped another stone from the tree.
4. The stone hit the second giant.
5. The second giant was very angry now.
6. "You started this fight!" the second giant said to the first.
7. The two giants started to fight and hit each other hard.
8. They fought until they fell down dead.
9. No more giants lived in the forest.
10. The tailor climbed down from the tree.
11. He walked back to the king's castle.
12. "How did you kill the giants?" the king asked.
13. "They killed each other," the tailor said.
14. The king was very surprised.
15. The king wanted one more task done first.

Paragraphs:
1-5: It is morning now; the first giant insists the second hit him, and the tailor drops another stone from the tree, hitting the second giant, who grows very angry.
6-10: The second giant accuses the first of starting the fight, and the two giants start to fight and hit each other hard until they fall down dead, and no more giants live in the forest.
11-15: The tailor climbs down from the tree and walks back to the king's castle; he tells the king the giants killed each other, and the king, very surprised, wants one more task done before he pays the reward.

The Unicorn in the Forest
1. "Catch a unicorn in the forest," the king said.
2. The tailor went to the forest again.
3. He saw a unicorn.
4. It had one long horn and white fur.
5. The unicorn put its head down to charge.
6. The unicorn ran at the tailor fast.
7. The tailor jumped behind a big tree.
8. Its horn went into the tree.
9. The horn was stuck in the tree trunk.
10. It was trapped and could not pull the horn out.
11. The tailor came out from behind the tree.
12. He tied a rope around its neck.
13. The tailor led it to the castle.
14. "This is the unicorn," the tailor said to the king.
15. The king had never seen a unicorn.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The king tells the tailor to catch the unicorn in the forest, and the tailor goes there again; he sees a unicorn with one long horn and white fur, and it puts its head down to charge.
6-10: The unicorn runs at the tailor fast, but he jumps behind a big tree, and its horn goes into the tree, stuck fast in the trunk, so it is trapped and cannot pull the horn out.
11-15: The tailor comes out from behind the tree, ties a rope around its neck, and leads it to the castle; he tells the king, "This is the unicorn," and the king has never seen a unicorn.

The Wild Boar
1. "Catch a wild boar in the forest," the king said.
2. The little tailor went into the forest.
3. Soon he saw a small chapel with benches.
4. It had one small window.
5. The wild boar ran out of the trees with two tusks.
6. It charged at the tailor fast.
7. He ran into the chapel, and the boar ran in too.
8. The tailor jumped over a bench and out of the window.
9. He ran around the chapel fast and shut the door.
10. The wild boar was now shut inside.
11. The chapel bell rang.
12. The tailor walked back to the big castle.
13. "The boar is in the chapel," the tailor told the king.
14. The king was surprised again.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The king tells the tailor to catch the wild boar in the forest, so he goes into the forest; he soon sees a small chapel with benches and one small window.
5-8: The wild boar runs out of the trees with two tusks and charges at the tailor fast; he runs into the chapel, with the boar running in too, and jumps over a bench and out of the window.
9-14: He runs around the chapel fast and shuts the door, trapping the wild boar inside as the chapel bell rings; the tailor walks back to the big castle and tells the king the boar is in the chapel, and the king is surprised again.

Half the Kingdom
1. The tailor did the third task.
2. The king had no more tasks to give.
3. The king had given his word.
4. "You may marry my daughter now," the king said.
5. Soon the tailor married the king's daughter.
6. All the people in the kingdom came to the wedding.
7. Bells rang in the kingdom.
8. The king gave the tailor half the kingdom.
9. The tailor now had half the kingdom.
10. The tailor was now very rich.
11. He never sewed again.
12. The tailor was still strong and lived in the big castle.
13. People still said he was the little tailor with the belt.
14. No one forgot "Seven at one blow."
15. The tailor and the king's daughter danced all night.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The tailor completes the third task, and the king, with no more tasks to give and having given his word, tells the tailor he may now marry his daughter.
5-9: Soon the tailor marries the king's daughter, and all the people in the kingdom come to the wedding as bells ring in the kingdom; the king gives him half the kingdom, which the tailor now has.
10-15: The tailor is now very rich and never sews again, though he is still strong and lives in the big castle, and people still say he is the little tailor with the belt, never forgetting "Seven at one blow"; the tailor and the king's daughter dance all night.

A Secret at Night
1. One night, the king's daughter heard the tailor in his sleep.
2. "Sew the coat, boy," the tailor said in his sleep.
3. The king's daughter knew he was a tailor.
4. She did not want people to know.
5. The king's daughter told the king.
6. That night, the king's guards would take the tailor away.
7. But one guard warned the tailor first.
8. That night, the tailor pretended to sleep.
9. The guards came to his bed.
10. "Seven at one blow!" the tailor said.
11. "I killed two giants and caught a unicorn," he said.
12. "I shut a wild boar in a chapel too," he said.
13. The guards heard this and were afraid.
14. They ran away and did not come back.
15. The little tailor stayed happy.

Paragraphs:
1-5: One night, the king's daughter hears the tailor in his sleep, telling a boy to sew a coat, and she knows he is a tailor; not wanting people to know, she tells the king.
6-9: That night, the king's guards plan to take the tailor away, but one guard warns the tailor first, so the tailor pretends to sleep when the guards come to his bed.
10-15: The tailor says he killed seven at one blow, killed two giants, caught a unicorn, and shut a wild boar in a chapel too; the guards hear this, grow afraid, and run away and do not come back, and the little tailor stays happy.
