Thumbelina

A Wish for a Child / A Girl from a Flower / Stolen by the Toad / The Fish Set Her Free
1. A woman lived alone in a small home.
2. The woman wanted to have a little child.
3. One day, she went to see an old witch.
4. "Please give me a child," the woman said.
5. The witch listened and gave her a small seed.
6. "Put this seed in a pot by the window," said the witch.
7. "A flower will grow from this seed," she said.
8. The woman thanked the witch and went home.
9. She put the seed in a pot by the window.
10. She gave it water every morning and every night.
11. Soon a green plant grew, and a large bud sat on it.
12. The woman looked at the dark red bud every day.
13. One morning, the red bud opened in the warm sun.
14. In the flower sat a tiny girl.
15. She was as small as the woman's thumb.
16. "My pretty girl!" said the woman.
17. The woman named her Thumbelina.
18. She made Thumbelina a little bed from a walnut shell.
19. Thumbelina slept in the shell by the window.
20. Soft petals covered her at night.
21. Every morning, Thumbelina sang in the sun by the window.
22. On warm nights, the woman left the window open.
23. Thumbelina looked at the dark sky from her shell.
24. One night, an old toad came in at the open window.
25. The old toad looked at the pretty girl.
26. The toad wanted a wife for her son.
27. She took the walnut shell with Thumbelina in it.
28. The toad hopped away into the dark night.
29. The old toad carried her away to a stream.
30. She put the shell on a lily pad in the water.
31. In the morning, Thumbelina woke up on the lily pad.
32. Water was all around the lily pad.
33. Thumbelina could not get back to the land.
34. The old toad came back with her son.
35. "Croak, croak," was all the son could say.
36. "You will live with my son in the mud," said the toad.
37. The toads hopped away to make a home in the mud.
38. She began to cry in the morning sun.
39. She did not want to live in the mud with the son.
40. Little fish swam in the stream by the lily pad.
41. The fish heard Thumbelina cry all day.
42. "She should not live with the ugly toad," said one fish.
43. The little fish began to bite the green stem of the pad.
44. They bit the stem again and again.
45. Soon the stem broke free from the mud.
46. The lily pad floated away down the stream.
47. Thumbelina thanked the little fish and smiled.

Paragraphs:
1-4: A woman lives alone and wants a child, so she goes to an old witch and asks for one.
5-8: The witch gives her a small seed, tells her to put it in a pot by her window, and says a flower will grow from it; the woman thanks her and goes home.
9-12: She plants the seed by the window and waters it until a green plant grows a large, dark red bud, which she looks at every day.
13-17: One morning the bud opens in the sun, and in the flower sits a tiny girl as small as a thumb; the delighted woman names her Thumbelina.
18-21: The woman makes her a bed from a walnut shell by the window, covers her with soft petals at night, and Thumbelina sings every morning.
22-23: The woman loves to hear her sing and leaves the window open on warm nights, and Thumbelina looks at the night sky from her shell, the woman happy with her tiny girl.
24-27: One night an old toad comes in at the open window, sees the pretty girl asleep, and takes her shell away because she wants a wife for her son.
28-31: The toad hops off into the dark, and Thumbelina sleeps on as she is carried to a stream and set on a lily pad, where she wakes in the morning.
32-36: Water is all around, and Thumbelina cannot get back to land; the toad returns with her croaking son and says Thumbelina will live with him in the mud.
37-39: The toads hop off to make a home in the mud, and Thumbelina sits alone on the lily pad and cries, not wanting to live there with the son.
40-42: Little fish hear her cry all day, look up at her, and decide she should not have to live with the ugly toad.
43-47: The fish bite through the stem until it breaks free, and the lily pad floats down the stream, carrying Thumbelina away from the toad; she thanks the fish and smiles.

The Beetle and the Daisy / Summer and Snow / The Field Mouse's Home
1. Thumbelina floated on the lily pad for a long time.
2. The sun went down, and night came to the water.
3. In the morning, a beetle saw her from a tall tree.
4. He flew down to the lily pad.
5. The beetle took Thumbelina up into the tall tree.
6. The beetle thought Thumbelina was pretty.
7. Other beetles came to look at the tiny girl.
8. "She has only two legs," said one beetle.
9. "She has no feelers," said the other beetles.
10. "She is not pretty at all," they all said.
11. He did not want Thumbelina now.
12. The beetle flew down and put her on a daisy.
13. He flew away and left her all alone.
14. All summer, Thumbelina lived alone in the green woods.
15. She made a small bed of petals under the daisy.
16. She lived on the water from the flowers every day.
17. A swallow sang in a tree over her.
18. Thumbelina loved to hear the swallow sing every day.
19. She was happy, but she thought of the woman at home.
20. Soon the days turned cold in the woods.
21. The leaves turned yellow and fell to the ground.
22. Snow began to fall from the dark sky.
23. The snow covered the ground and all the flowers.
24. Thumbelina was cold, and she had no warm home now.
25. She could not keep her small bed warm in the snow.
26. She looked for a warm home under the ground.
27. At last, she came to a little hole in the ground.
28. A field mouse lived in the warm hole under the ground.
29. "Please give me a little food," said Thumbelina.
30. "I have had no food for two days."
31. "Come in and get warm," said the kind field mouse.
32. The field mouse gave her food and a warm bed.
33. "You may stay here with me for the winter," said the mouse.
34. "But you must keep my home and sing for me."
35. Thumbelina thanked the kind field mouse.
36. She kept the little home warm all winter.
37. Every morning, Thumbelina sang for the field mouse.
38. Thumbelina thought of the sun and the flowers.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Thumbelina floats on for a long time, through a night, and in the morning a beetle sees her from a tall tree, flies down, and carries her up into the tree.
6-10: The beetle thinks she is pretty, but the other beetles come to look and say she has only two legs, no feelers, and is not pretty at all.
11-13: The beetle listens to them and does not want her now, so he flies her down, puts her on a daisy, and leaves her all alone.
14-19: All summer Thumbelina lives alone in the green woods, sleeping in a bed of petals under the daisy, living on water from the flowers, and listening to a swallow sing over her, happy but thinking of the woman at home.
20-23: The days turn cold, the leaves turn yellow and fall, and snow covers the ground and the flowers.
24-27: Cold and with no warm home, Thumbelina cannot keep warm in the snow, so she looks for a home under the ground and at last comes to a little hole.
28-31: A field mouse lives in the warm hole, and when Thumbelina begs for food after two days with none, the kind mouse asks her in to get warm.
32-35: The mouse gives her food and a warm bed and says she may stay the winter if she keeps the home and sings for her, and Thumbelina thanks her.
36-38: Thumbelina keeps the home warm and sings every morning, and the mouse loves to hear her; the hole is warm while the snow is cold, but Thumbelina thinks of the sun and the flowers.

The Mole and the Swallow / A Choice in Spring / The Wedding Day
1. The field mouse had a rich neighbor, an old mole.
2. The mole lived under the ground and did not love the sun.
3. One day, the mole came to see the mouse.
4. "Sing for my neighbor," said the field mouse.
5. Thumbelina sang for the mole in her soft voice.
6. The mole loved her voice and wanted Thumbelina for his wife.
7. "You should marry the rich mole," the mouse said.
8. The mole made a long tunnel under the ground.
9. One day, he took Thumbelina into the dark tunnel.
10. A bird was down on the ground in the tunnel.
11. It was a swallow, cold and still.
12. "I do not love birds," said the mole.
13. He went on into the dark.
14. But Thumbelina thought of the swallow that sang in the summer.
15. At night, Thumbelina went back to the tunnel all alone.
16. She covered the cold swallow with hay.
17. Thumbelina felt the swallow, and it was a little warm.
18. The swallow was not dead at all.
19. All that winter, Thumbelina took hay and water to the swallow.
20. She kept the swallow a secret from the mole.
21. Soon the swallow was strong again.
22. Warm winds came, and spring came back to the fields.
23. The swallow could fly again in the spring.
24. "Thank you for the hay and the water," said the swallow.
25. "Come away with me to a warm land," he said.
26. Thumbelina wanted to go away and see that warm land.
27. But she thought of the kind field mouse and her warm home.
28. "I will not leave her now," Thumbelina said to the swallow.
29. The swallow flew away all alone into the spring sky.
30. Soon the field mouse wanted a wedding for the fall.
31. "You will marry the mole in the fall," said the mouse.
32. The days turned cold, and the leaves turned yellow again.
33. The wedding day came at last.
34. The field mouse made Thumbelina a wedding dress.
35. "You will live with the mole under the ground," said the mouse.
36. But Thumbelina did not want to live down under the ground.
37. "Could I go out and see the sun one last time?"
38. The field mouse said yes.
39. Thumbelina went out and looked up at the warm sun.
40. She heard the swallow, and he flew down to her.
41. "I will marry the mole," she said to the swallow.
42. "Come away with me to the warm land," said the swallow.
43. Then she got on the swallow's back, and they flew away.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The mouse has a rich old neighbor, a mole who lives under the ground and does not love the sun; when he visits, Thumbelina sings for him, and he loves her voice and wants her for his wife, so the mouse says she should marry him.
8-11: The mole takes Thumbelina into his long, dark tunnel, where a swallow lies on the ground, cold and still.
12-14: The mole says he does not love birds and goes on, but Thumbelina thinks of the swallow that sang in the summer and looks back at the still bird.
15-21: At night Thumbelina returns to the tunnel alone, covers the cold swallow with hay, and feels that it is a little warm — not dead; all winter she takes it hay and water in secret from the mole, and soon it is strong again.
22-25: When warm winds come and spring returns, the swallow can fly again; he thanks Thumbelina for the hay and the water and asks her to come away with him to a warm land.
26-29: Thumbelina wants to see that land, but she thinks of the kind field mouse and will not leave her now, so the swallow flies away alone into the spring sky.
30-34: The field mouse wants a fall wedding for Thumbelina and the mole, and when the days turn cold and the leaves turn yellow again, the wedding day comes and the mouse makes Thumbelina a wedding dress.
35-38: The mouse says Thumbelina will live with the mole under the ground, but Thumbelina does not want that life and asks to go out and see the sun one last time; the mouse says yes.
39-43: Outside in the sun she hears the swallow, who flies down to her; she tells him she is to marry the mole, he asks her again to come to the warm land, and after one last thought of the kind mouse, she gets on his back and they fly away.

The Prince of the Flowers / Wings for Thumbelina
1. The swallow flew on with Thumbelina over woods and cold water.
2. At last, they came to a warm land of flowers.
3. The swallow put Thumbelina on a large flower.
4. In the large flower sat a tiny man.
5. He was as small as Thumbelina, and he looked at her.
6. The man had white wings on his back.
7. He had a small crown.
8. He was the prince of all the flowers.
9. In every flower lived a tiny man or a tiny woman.
10. The prince looked at Thumbelina and smiled.
11. "Will you stay here and be my wife?" asked the prince.
12. Thumbelina looked at the little prince and said yes.
13. The tiny men and women all came out of the flowers.
14. They came to the wedding of Thumbelina and the little prince.
15. One tiny woman put white wings on Thumbelina's back.
16. Thumbelina could now fly from flower to flower.
17. She flew over the warm land of flowers with the prince.
18. The tiny men and women sang for the wedding all night.
19. The swallow watched the wedding from his tree by the flowers.
20. He sang one last song for Thumbelina.
21. The swallow flew away to his home over the cold water.
22. Thumbelina watched him go from her flower.
23. She lived with the prince as queen of the flowers.
24. The little girl from the flower was home at last.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The swallow flies Thumbelina over woods and cold water to a warm land of flowers and puts her on a large flower, where a tiny man as small as her sits.
6-12: The man, with white wings and a crown, is the prince of all the flowers, and tiny people live in every flower; he smiles at Thumbelina and asks her to be his wife, and she says yes.
13-18: The tiny men and women come out of the flowers for the wedding, one of them puts white wings on Thumbelina's back so she can fly over the land with the prince, and they all sing for the wedding all night.
19-24: The swallow watches from his tree and sings one last song, then flies away to his home over the cold water; Thumbelina watches him go, then lives with the prince as queen of the flowers, sleeping in a white flower at night, home at last.
