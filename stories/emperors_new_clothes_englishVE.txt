The Emperor's New Clothes

The Emperor Who Loved Clothes
1. Long ago, a rich emperor loved fine new clothes.
2. The emperor had a new coat for each hour.
3. He spent all his gold on new clothes.
4. The emperor did not care about his town.
5. He did not care about his people.
6. Clothes were the only thing the emperor loved.
7. Every morning, the emperor put on a fine coat.
8. Then the emperor walked through the streets of the town.
9. "Look at my new clothes!" the emperor said.
10. The people looked at the grand new coat.
11. "Your clothes are so beautiful," the people said.
12. But the emperor always wanted more new clothes.

Paragraphs:
1-6: A rich emperor loves fine clothes above all — he spends all his gold on new ones and cares nothing for his town or people.
7-12: Each morning the emperor dresses up and walks through town, where the people praise his clothes, and he always wants more.

The Two Men and Their Cloth
1. One day, two men came to the town.
2. The two men said they could make beautiful cloth.
3. "Our cloth is very beautiful," the men said.
4. "But our cloth is also special," said one man.
5. "Only clever people can see this cloth," the man said.
6. "A foolish person sees nothing at all," he said.
7. The emperor heard about the special cloth.
8. "I must have this special cloth!" the emperor said.
9. "Then I will see who is foolish," he said.
10. The emperor wanted the cloth more than his gold.
11. The emperor told the two men to make it.
12. "I will give you lots of gold," the emperor said.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Two strangers come to town claiming to make beautiful cloth that only clever people can see, while foolish people see nothing at all.
7-12: The emperor longs for the cloth to find out who is foolish, and tells the two men to make it and promises them lots of gold.

Empty Looms
1. The emperor gave the two men a bag of gold.
2. He also gave them fine silk and gold thread.
3. The two men made two looms for the cloth.
4. But the two men put nothing on the looms.
5. The looms were empty all day and night.
6. The two men only pretended to work.
7. They kept the silk and gold.
8. The two men did no work at all.
9. The emperor wanted to see his beautiful cloth.
10. But the emperor was afraid to look.
11. "Can I see the cloth?" the emperor thought.
12. The emperor sent his old minister to look.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The emperor pays the two men with a bag of gold, fine silk, and thread, but they put nothing on the looms.
5-8: The looms stay empty day and night — the men only pretend to work and keep the silk and gold.
9-12: Longing to see the cloth yet afraid he cannot, the emperor sends his old minister to look first.

The Minister Sees Nothing
1. The old minister came to see the two men.
2. The minister looked hard at the two looms.
3. But the minister saw no cloth on the looms.
4. "These looms are empty!" the minister thought.
5. "Am I a foolish old man?" the minister asked.
6. But he did not want to seem foolish.
7. "What fine colors this cloth has!" the minister said.
8. The two men pointed at the empty looms.
9. Then the minister hurried back to the happy emperor.
10. "The new cloth is so beautiful," the minister said.
11. The emperor was very glad to hear this.
12. Now the emperor wanted to see the cloth himself.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The old minister visits the two men but sees no cloth on the empty looms.
5-8: Afraid to seem foolish, the minister praises the cloth's fine colors, and the two men point at the empty looms.
9-12: The minister hurries back and tells the emperor the cloth is beautiful; the glad emperor now wants to see it himself.

The Emperor Sees Nothing
1. The next day, the emperor went to the looms.
2. Many of his men came with him.
3. But the emperor saw nothing on the looms!
4. The emperor was afraid to seem foolish.
5. "Oh, what wonderful clothes!" the emperor said.
6. "Yes, the cloth is so beautiful!" his men said.
7. But his men also saw nothing at all.
8. The men did not want to seem foolish.
9. Then the two men dressed the emperor with care.
10. The emperor looked at himself in the mirror.
11. But the emperor saw no clothes on his body.
12. "These new clothes look so fine," the emperor said.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The next day the emperor and his men come to the looms, but the emperor sees nothing and is afraid to seem foolish.
5-8: The emperor and his men all praise the cloth none of them can see, because no one wants to seem foolish.
9-12: The two men dress the emperor, who looks in the mirror but sees no clothes on his body, yet says they look fine.

A Child Tells the Truth
1. The next morning, the emperor walked through the town.
2. But the emperor had no clothes on his body.
3. All the people came out to the street.
4. "Look at the grand new clothes!" the people said.
5. "The fine colors are so beautiful!" a man said.
6. No one wanted to seem foolish.
7. Every person said the cloth looked wonderful.
8. Then a young child saw the emperor.
9. "But the emperor has no clothes on!" the child cried.
10. The people heard the young child and looked.
11. "The child tells the truth!" they all said.
12. The emperor knew the truth but walked on.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The emperor parades through town with no clothes on, and the people line the street praising his grand new clothes because no one wants to seem foolish.
7-12: A young child cries out that the emperor has no clothes; the people look and take up the truth, yet the emperor walks proudly on.
