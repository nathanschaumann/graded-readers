Great Expectations

The Man in the Graveyard
1. Pip had no mother and no father, and his five little brothers were dead too.
2. They all lay in graves on the marshes, and Pip had never known them.
3. Pip's home was a small house, and he shared it with his sister, Mrs Joe, and her husband, Joe.
4. Joe was a blacksmith, and he was a kind man.
5. Mrs Joe was not kind, and she hit Pip.
6. One cold night Pip went to the graves alone.
7. He stood by his mother's grave and cried, because he was alone.
8. A man came up from behind a grave.
9. The man was big and cold, and he had a chain on his leg.
10. He had no hat, and his head was cut.
11. The man took hold of Pip.
12. "Be quiet, or I will cut your throat!" the man said.
13. "Please do not cut my throat, sir," said Pip.
14. "Tell me your name," the man said.
15. "Pip, sir," said Pip.
16. "Show me your home," the man said.
17. Pip pointed to the house of Joe.
18. "Have you any food?" the man asked.
19. "No, sir," said Pip.
20. "Who lives in that house?" the man asked.
21. "I live there with Mrs Joe and Joe the blacksmith," said Pip.
22. "A blacksmith," the man said, and he looked at the chain on his leg.
23. "Now hear me, boy," the man said.
24. "Bring me a file and some food in the morning."
25. "Bring them to the old house on the marshes, and tell no one."
26. "A young man is hiding here, and he is not like me."
27. "That young man will cut out your heart and eat it, if you tell anyone."
28. "Say that you will bring the file and the food," the man said.
29. "I will bring the file and the food, sir," said Pip.
30. "And you will tell no one," the man said.
31. "I will tell no one, sir," said Pip.
32. Pip was afraid, and he ran home.
33. Once he looked back and saw the man walk away with the chain on his leg.

Mrs Joe
1. Mrs Joe was at the door, and she was angry.
2. "Where have you been, you bad boy?" she said.
3. "I was at the graves," said Pip.
4. "At the graves!" said Mrs Joe.
5. "If I did not work for you, you would be dead like the rest."
6. She hit Pip with a stick, and Pip went to sit by the fire.
7. Joe sat by the fire too, and Mrs Joe cut bread for Joe and for Pip.
8. Joe was big and kind, but he was afraid of Mrs Joe.
9. Mrs Joe was hard, and Pip was afraid of her too.
10. Pip could not eat, because he thought about the man and the chain.
11. When Joe looked away, Pip hid the bread down his leg.
12. Joe looked back and saw that Pip's bread was gone.
13. "Pip, you ate that bread too fast," Joe said.
14. "That is bad for a boy."
15. "I will not eat fast again, Joe," said Pip.
16. That night they heard a gun on the marshes.
17. "What is that?" Pip asked.
18. "A convict has run away," said Joe.
19. "They fire a gun when a convict runs away," said Joe.
20. "What is a convict?" Pip asked.
21. "A convict is a bad man who lives in a prison," said Mrs Joe.
22. "Where is the convict now?" Pip asked.
23. "He is on the marshes, and they will take him back to the prison," said Joe.
24. Pip knew now that the man on the marshes was a convict.
25. Pip went to bed, but he did not sleep.
26. He thought about the man, and the young man, and the marshes.
27. "The young man will cut out my heart," Pip thought.
28. "The man will wait for the file and the food," Pip thought.
29. Pip knew that he must take the food and the file before the morning.
30. Pip lay in bed and waited for the morning to come.

The File and the Food
1. Before the morning came, Pip went down to the food in the house.
2. Mrs Joe and Joe were in bed, and no one heard him.
3. He took some bread and some cheese and a big pie.
4. He put water in a bottle.
5. Pip went to the forge, where Joe did his work, and he took a file.
6. He ran out to the cold marshes, and he was afraid.
7. Pip saw a man with his back to him.
8. Pip thought that it was the man from the graves, and he took hold of him.
9. The man looked at Pip, and Pip saw that he was not the man from the graves.
10. This man was young, and his face was cut.
11. The man hit at Pip, and then he ran away.
12. "That was the young man," Pip thought, and he ran on.
13. At the old house Pip found the man with the chain on his leg.
14. "Have you brought the file and the food?" the man asked.
15. "Yes, sir," said Pip, and he gave the man the bread and the cheese and the pie.
16. The man ate the food fast, and he did not stop to speak.
17. "I am glad that you like it, sir," said Pip.
18. "You are a good boy," the man said.
19. "Thank you, sir," said Pip.
20. "Sir, I saw a young man on the marshes, and his face was cut," said Pip.
21. "Show me where he went," the man said.
22. Pip pointed, and the man took the file and sat down.
23. "Will they take you back to the prison?" Pip asked.
24. "They will not take me, if I have the file," the man said.
25. He began to work at the chain on his leg with the file, and he did not look at Pip.
26. Pip ran home to the house, and no one saw him.

The Christmas Dinner
1. On Christmas day, Pumblechook and Wopsle came to dinner.
2. Pumblechook was Joe's uncle, and he was a rich man with a shop.
3. Wopsle was the clerk of the church, and he liked the sound of his own voice.
4. Pip sat at the table next to Pumblechook, and he was afraid.
5. Pip had taken the pie, and he knew that Mrs Joe would look for it.
6. He held the table with his hand, and he could not eat.
7. Mrs Joe gave the best food to her guests, and she gave Pip a bad piece.
8. "Boys are bad," Pumblechook said.
9. "A boy must thank the people who work for him."
10. "Pip must thank Mrs Joe, for she works for him."
11. "That is true," said Wopsle, in a loud voice.
12. "I do thank her," said Pip.
13. "It is true, and Pip is a bad boy," said Mrs Joe.
14. Joe was kind, and he gave Pip some of his own food.
15. "Pip, do not eat too fast," Joe said.
16. "I do not eat fast, Joe," said Pip.
17. "Now you must eat some of my pie, Uncle," Mrs Joe said.
18. "It is a good pie, and it is a big one," Pumblechook said.
19. Mrs Joe went for the pie.
20. "My pie is gone!" cried Mrs Joe.
21. "Where is my pie?" she asked.
22. Pip stood up, and he ran to the door.
23. At the door, Pip saw a soldier, and the soldier had handcuffs in his hand.
24. "Is the blacksmith here?" the soldier asked.
25. "I am the blacksmith," said Joe.
26. "Two convicts have run away, and we must look for them on the marshes," the soldier said.
27. "The handcuffs are broken, and we need a blacksmith to mend them."
28. "Bring them to the forge, and I will mend them," said Joe.
29. Pip was afraid, and he thought the soldier had come to take him to prison.
30. "They have come for me," Pip thought.
31. Pip said nothing, and Mrs Joe was too busy with the soldiers to look at him.
32. Joe took the handcuffs to the forge, and the soldiers went with him.

The Two Convicts
1. Joe made a fire in the forge, and he began to mend the handcuffs.
2. Pip kept his face down, so that the soldiers would not see it.
3. The soldiers did not look at Pip.
4. When the work was done, Joe said, "I will come with you, and I will see the end."
5. "Come, then, and bring the boy," the soldier said.
6. Wopsle wanted to see the end too, and he came with them.
7. Joe took Pip on his back, and they went out to the cold marshes.
8. They went on and on, and the soldiers were quiet.
9. It was now night, and the soldiers held lights in their hands.
10. On the way, they heard a shout, and they ran to it.
11. On the ground, two men fought.
12. One was Pip's man, and the other was the young man with the cut face.
13. The soldiers took hold of both men.
14. "I have taken him," said Pip's man.
15. "He is a bad man, and he would not go back to the prison."
16. "That man wanted to cut my throat," said the young man.
17. "It is a lie," said the man with the chain.
18. The soldiers put handcuffs on both of them.
19. "I took some food from the blacksmith's house," the man with the chain said.
20. "I took bread and cheese and a pie."
21. "You hear that, blacksmith?" said the soldier.
22. "God knows that you are welcome to it," said Joe.
23. "We would not let a man go with no food."
24. Pip looked at the man, and the man did not look at Pip.
25. The soldiers took the two men to a boat on the river.
26. Joe carried Pip home on his back, and Pip was very tired, and he was cold.
27. Pip wanted to tell Joe about the file, but he was afraid, and he said no more.
28. Pip knew that the man had told a lie to save him.

Satis House
1. One day, Pumblechook said that a rich lady in the town wanted a boy to play at her house.
2. The name of the lady was Miss Havisham, and her house was called Satis House.
3. Mrs Joe said, "You must be good in the house of the lady, boy."
4. Pumblechook took Pip to the house, and Pip walked next to him.
5. At the gate stood a girl, and she was young and pretty.
6. Pip had never seen a girl so pretty, and he did not know where to look.
7. "My name is Estella," the girl said.
8. Estella looked at Pip, and she did not smile.
9. Pumblechook wanted to come in, but Estella did not let him.
10. "Miss Havisham did not ask for you," Estella said.
11. "Come with me, boy."
12. Estella held a light, and she took Pip into the house.
13. The house was old and dark, and Pip was afraid.
14. They came to a room, and there was no sun in it.
15. In the room sat a lady in a white dress, and the dress was old.
16. It was a wedding dress, and all the things in the room were old and white.
17. On a big table there was a wedding cake, and the cake was bad.
18. A clock stood in the room, and it did not go.
19. Miss Havisham looked at Pip, and Pip looked at her.
20. "Who is this?" the lady asked.
21. "This is the boy, Miss Havisham," said Estella.
22. "Come near to me, boy," said Miss Havisham.
23. "Are you afraid of me?"
24. "No, Miss," said Pip, but that was a lie.
25. "I am tired, and I want to see a boy play," said Miss Havisham.
26. "Play, boy!"
27. "I cannot play here, Miss," said Pip.
28. "Then Estella will play cards with you," said Miss Havisham.
29. "Do you know any game, boy?"
30. "I know only one, Miss," said Pip.
31. "Then you may play it with Estella."
32. "Yes, Miss," said Pip.

The Coarse Boy
1. Pip and Estella played cards next to the wedding cake, and Miss Havisham looked at them.
2. Estella won the game, and she did not smile.
3. Pip did not play well, because he could not stop looking at her.
4. "What coarse hands he has!" Estella said to Miss Havisham.
5. "And what thick boots he has!"
6. Miss Havisham was quiet, but she looked at Estella, and she was glad.
7. Pip had never thought about his hands and his boots before.
8. Now Pip thought that his hands were coarse and his boots were thick, and he did not like them.
9. "What do you think of her, boy?" Miss Havisham asked.
10. "I think she is very proud, Miss," said Pip.
11. "What else do you think?"
12. "I think she is very pretty, Miss," said Pip.
13. "What else?"
14. "I think she is not kind to me, Miss, and I want to go home," said Pip.
15. "You may go home soon," said Miss Havisham.
16. "Estella, bring the boy some food."
17. Estella went out and came back with bread and meat.
18. She put the food on the ground, and Pip took it.
19. Pip looked at Estella, and he could not speak.
20. Estella saw his tears, and she smiled.
21. When Estella went out, Pip went to the wall of the house.
22. He hit the wall with his hand, and he cried.
23. "I am a coarse boy," Pip said to himself.
24. "I have thick boots, and I have coarse hands, and I am not a good boy."
25. Estella came back, and she let Pip out of the gate.
26. "You may come again in 6 days," Estella said.
27. "Miss Havisham said that you must come."
28. Pip looked at Estella, but she did not look at him.
29. He walked home over the marshes, and he thought about the girl and the room.
30. Pip did not like his home now, and he did not like his hands.

The Lie
1. At home, Mrs Joe and Pumblechook asked Pip about Miss Havisham.
2. "What is she like, boy?" Mrs Joe asked.
3. Pip did not want to tell the truth, and he told a lie.
4. "Miss Havisham sits in a black coach," said Pip.
5. "There are 4 big dogs, and they eat meat from a basket."
6. "Estella and I play with flags," said Pip.
7. "Is it true?" said Mrs Joe.
8. "It is true," Pip said, and his face was hot.
9. Pip wanted to say the truth, but Pumblechook was there, and Pip said no more.
10. Pumblechook looked at Mrs Joe, and Mrs Joe looked at Pumblechook.
11. "That lady is rich," Pumblechook said.
12. "She will do something for the boy."
13. "Pip will be rich, Mrs Joe."
14. "You must go to her house again, Pip," said Mrs Joe.
15. That night, Pip sat with Joe by the fire in the forge.
16. "What is the matter, Pip?" Joe asked.
17. Pip could not look at Joe, and he looked at the fire.
18. "Joe, I told a lie," Pip said.
19. "There was no coach, and there were no dogs."
20. "Miss Havisham sits in a dark room, and Estella said that I am a coarse boy."
21. "I am coarse, and Estella is proud and pretty."
22. "I want to be a gentleman, Joe."
23. Joe looked at Pip, and he was not angry.
24. "Pip, a lie is a lie, and a lie is bad," said Joe.
25. "One lie will bring another lie, and they will not go away."
26. "The 4 dogs were a big lie, old boy."
27. "Do not tell lies again."
28. Joe put his hand on Pip, and Pip was not afraid.
29. "You are not coarse, Pip, and you are a good boy."
30. "I am glad that you told me the truth."
31. "Then I will not tell lies again," said Pip.
32. "That is good, old boy," said Joe.
33. Pip went to bed, and he thought about Estella.
34. He thought that she was pretty, and he thought that his hands were coarse.

The Pale Young Gentleman
1. Pip went to Satis House again in 6 days, and he went many times after that.
2. He played cards with Estella, and she won every game.
3. Miss Havisham sat in a chair, and Pip pushed the chair around the room.
4. "Push me, boy," said Miss Havisham.
5. "Push me again."
6. The wedding cake was still on the table, and the clock still did not go.
7. Every day was the same, with the same dark room, the same cake, and the same cards.
8. Miss Havisham looked at Estella, and she was glad when Estella was proud.
9. Pip did not know why Miss Havisham wanted him.
10. He knew only that the house was dark, and that Estella was there.
11. One day, Estella said, "A boy is in the garden, and he wants to see you."
12. Pip went to the garden, and he found a pale young gentleman there.
13. "Come and fight me," said the pale young gentleman.
14. "Do you want to fight me?" Pip asked.
15. "I do," said the pale young gentleman.
16. They fought, and Pip hit the pale young gentleman again and again.
17. The pale young gentleman fell down many times, and every time he stood up.
18. At last he sat on the ground, and he did not stand up.
19. "You have won," he said.
20. "Now I will go home."
21. He was pale and cut, but he was kind, and Pip liked him.
22. Estella stood at the door, and she looked at the fight.
23. "You may kiss me," Estella said to Pip.
24. Pip kissed her face, and Estella did not move.
25. She did not smile, and she went back into the house.
26. Pip walked home over the marshes, and he thought only about Estella's face.
27. He did not know why Estella had let him kiss her.
28. The pale young gentleman was never at Satis House again.
29. Pip went to Satis House for many months, and he grew big and strong.

The Stranger at the Inn
1. One night, Pip went to the inn to find Joe.
2. Joe sat by the fire, and a stranger sat next to him.
3. The stranger looked at Pip, and he did not smile.
4. "Is this your boy?" the stranger asked Joe.
5. "This is Pip," said Joe.
6. "He is a good boy," said Joe.
7. "A good boy is a good thing to have," the stranger said.
8. The stranger had a drink, and he stirred it with a file.
9. Pip knew that file, for it was the file that he had given to the convict, and he was afraid.
10. When Pip went home, the stranger gave him a paper.
11. "For you, boy," the stranger said.
12. Inside the paper was some money.
13. Pip gave the money to Mrs Joe, and she put it in a pot.
14. Pip thought about the man with the file, and he thought about the convict.
15. Many months later, Pip was bigger.
16. One day, Miss Havisham said, "Pip, you are a big boy now."
17. "You must work in the forge with Joe."
18. "Bring Joe to me, and I will give him money for you."
19. Joe came to Satis House in his best clothes, and he did not know what to do with his hat.
20. "Pip has been a good boy," said Miss Havisham.
21. "This is for Pip, and it is all that he will have."
22. "You are very kind, Miss," said Joe.
23. Miss Havisham gave Joe the money.
24. Pip was to work in the forge with Joe, and Joe was to teach him.
25. Estella did not speak to Pip, and she did not look at him.
26. Pip walked home with Joe, and Joe was glad.
27. "Pip, you will be a blacksmith like me," said Joe.
28. "Yes, Joe," said Pip, but he was not glad.
29. Pip wanted to be a gentleman, and a boy in a forge could not be a gentleman.

Orlick and Biddy
1. For 4 years, Pip did the work of a blacksmith in the forge.
2. Joe was a good teacher, and Pip loved Joe, but he did not love the work.
3. The forge was hot, and the work was hard.
4. Orlick was a big, strong man who did work for Joe.
5. Orlick did not like Pip, and he did not like Mrs Joe.
6. "Orlick is a bad man," Mrs Joe said to Joe.
7. "He must go from the forge."
8. "He is a good man for the work," said Joe.
9. Orlick said that Pip was too good for the forge, and he hit the hot iron hard.
10. Every night, Pip thought about Estella and about Satis House.
11. "If Estella saw me now, she would say that my hands are coarse," Pip thought.
12. Pip was ashamed of the forge, and he was ashamed of his home.
13. Pip did not tell Joe, because Joe was kind, and Pip did not want to hurt him.
14. Biddy was a girl with no mother and no father, and she taught the children in the village.
15. Biddy was not proud, and she was not rich, but she was good.
16. Pip went to Biddy to learn, and Biddy was kind to him.
17. One day, Pip told Biddy about Estella and about Satis House.
18. "I want to be a gentleman, Biddy," said Pip.
19. "Why do you want it?" Biddy asked.
20. "I want it for Estella," said Pip.
21. "If Estella cannot like you as you are, she is not a good girl," said Biddy.
22. Pip knew that he would be glad if he loved Biddy, but he loved Estella.
23. "I wish that I could love you, Biddy," said Pip.
24. "You never will," said Biddy.
25. Biddy went back to her work with the children, and Pip went back to the forge.
26. Pip thought about her words, but he thought more about Estella.

17-20: Estella says she did not know what love was before and now knows what she did to Pip.
21-24: Estella thinks of what Miss Havisham did to them and hopes Pip will forgive her, and Pip says he did long ago.
25-28: Estella asks if they are friends, Pip says they are, and they leave the garden together, without thinking they will part.
