Jack and the Beanstalk

Jack Discovers the Beanstalk
1. Jack and his mother really needed money.
2. Jack sold their cow for some beans.
3. His mother was angry and threw the beans outside.
4. Overnight, the beans grew into a tall beanstalk.
5. Jack climbed up the tall beanstalk.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Jack and his mother are poor, so Jack sells their cow for beans; his mother angrily throws them outside, but overnight they grow into a tall beanstalk, and Jack climbs up.

Inside the Giant's House
1. At the top, Jack saw a house.
2. It was the home of a giant.
3. The giant's wife came to the door.
4. "I have no money and no food," said Jack.
5. "Please may I have some food?"
6. The wife gave Jack some food.
7. "But my husband eats boys, so hide," she said.
8. Then Jack heard the giant come home.
9. The wife hid Jack in the oven.
10. The giant came in and sat down.
11. The giant looked around.
12. "I smell a boy in this house!"

Paragraphs:
1-4: At the top of the beanstalk Jack finds a house; it is the home of a giant, and the wife answers the door to Jack, who has no money or food.
5-7: Jack asks for food and the giant's wife gives him some, but warns that her husband eats boys and Jack must hide.
8-10: Jack hears the giant coming, so the wife hides him in the oven; the giant comes in and sits down.
11-12: The giant looks around the room and declares that he smells a boy in the house.

Jack Steals the Gold
1. "There is no boy here," said the wife.
2. The giant sat down and ate his food.
3. Then the giant took out his gold.
4. He counted the gold again and again.
5. The giant had many bags of gold.
6. The giant fell asleep.
7. Jack came out of the oven.
8. He took one bag of the giant's gold.
9. Then Jack ran to the tall beanstalk.
10. He climbed down with the heavy bag.
11. "Mother, look at this gold!" said Jack.
12. Now Jack and his mother had money again.

Paragraphs:
1-2: The wife tells the giant no boy is there, and the giant sits down and eats his food.
3-6: The giant takes out his gold, counts the many bags again and again, and then falls asleep.
7-10: Jack creeps out of the oven, takes one bag of the giant's gold, and runs to the beanstalk to climb down.
11-12: Jack shows his mother the gold, and they have money again.

Jack Climbs Back
1. Jack gave the gold to his mother.
2. But one day all the gold was gone.
3. "I will climb the beanstalk again," said Jack.
4. Jack climbed up to the giant's house.
5. The giant's wife was at the door.
6. "You took our gold!" said the wife.
7. "Please let me hide again," said Jack.
8. The kind wife gave Jack some food.
9. Then they heard the giant come home.
10. The wife hid Jack in a big pot.
11. The giant came in for his food.

Paragraphs:
1-3: Jack gives the gold to his mother, but one day it is all gone, so Jack decides to climb the beanstalk again.
4-6: Jack climbs back to the giant's house, where the wife meets him at the door and accuses him of taking their gold.
7-8: Jack begs to hide again, and the kind wife gives him food.
9-11: They hear the giant come home, so the wife hides Jack in a big pot; the giant comes in looking for his food.

The Hen of Golden Eggs
1. "No boy is here," said the wife again.
2. The giant ate all of his food.
3. Then the giant brought out a little hen.
4. "Lay!" said the giant to the hen.
5. The hen laid an egg of gold.
6. The giant looked at his egg of gold.
7. Soon the giant fell asleep again.
8. Jack came out and took the hen.
9. He ran to the tall beanstalk with the hen.
10. Jack climbed down to his mother.
11. "This hen lays eggs of gold!" said Jack.
12. Now Jack and his mother were rich.

Paragraphs:
1-2: The wife again says no boy is there, and the giant eats all of his food.
3-7: The giant brings out a little hen, commands it to lay, and it lays an egg of gold; the giant looks at the egg and then falls asleep.
8-10: Jack takes the hen, runs to the beanstalk, and climbs down to his mother.
11-12: Jack tells his mother the hen lays eggs of gold, and now they are rich.

One Climb Too Many
1. Now Jack had the hen and the gold.
2. But Jack wanted to climb up once more.
3. "Stay home, Jack," said his mother.
4. But Jack climbed the beanstalk again.
5. Soon he was at the giant's house.
6. The giant's wife saw Jack come in.
7. "You are here again!" said the wife.
8. But she let Jack hide once more.
9. The wife hid Jack in a cupboard.
10. Soon the giant came home for food.
11. "I smell a boy here!" said the giant.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Jack has the hen and the gold, but he wants to climb once more; his mother tells him to stay home, yet Jack climbs the beanstalk again.
5-6: Jack reaches the giant's house, and the wife sees him come in.
7-9: The wife is surprised to see Jack again but lets him hide, putting him in a cupboard.
10-11: The giant comes home looking for food and announces that he smells a boy.

Jack and the Singing Harp
1. Again the wife said no boy was there.
2. The giant ate his big dinner.
3. Then the giant brought out a gold harp.
4. "Sing for me!" said the giant.
5. The gold harp sang a soft song.
6. Soon the music made the giant sleep.
7. Jack jumped up and grabbed the harp.
8. But the harp called out to the giant.
9. "Master! Master!" cried the gold harp.
10. The giant woke up in a rage.
11. He ran after Jack with big steps.
12. Jack raced down the tall beanstalk.

Paragraphs:
1-2: The wife again denies any boy is there, and the giant eats his big dinner.
3-6: The giant brings out a gold harp and tells it to sing; the harp sings a soft song until the music sends the giant to sleep.
7-9: Jack jumps up and grabs the harp, but it cries out for its master.
10-12: The giant wakes in a rage and runs after Jack; Jack races down the beanstalk.

The Chase and the Fall
1. Jack climbed down the tall beanstalk fast.
2. The giant climbed down after him.
3. "Mother, bring me the axe!" called Jack.
4. His mother ran out with a big axe.
5. Jack took the axe and began to cut.
6. He cut and cut the tall beanstalk.
7. The tall beanstalk broke in two.
8. The giant fell to the ground and died.
9. Jack and his mother were safe at last.
10. They still had the gold, the hen, and the harp.
11. Jack and his mother were rich for life.

Paragraphs:
1-2: Jack climbs down the tall beanstalk fast, with the giant climbing down after him.
3-6: Jack calls for an axe; his mother brings it, and Jack begins to cut the beanstalk again and again.
7-8: The beanstalk breaks in two and the giant falls to the ground and dies.
9-11: Jack and his mother are safe at last; they still have the gold, the hen, and the harp, and they are rich for life.
