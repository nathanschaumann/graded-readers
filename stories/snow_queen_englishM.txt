The Snow Queen

The Troll's Mirror / Kai and Gerda / The Splinters
1. Long ago, a troll made a mirror.
2. This troll loved to trick and laugh at people.
3. The mirror changed how things looked.
4. Good things looked bad in the mirror.
5. A kind face looked cruel in the mirror.
6. The mirror made bad things look worse.
7. The troll carried the mirror from town to town.
8. He looked at what the mirror showed and laughed.
9. One day, the troll climbed a mountain with the mirror.
10. He held the mirror up to look at the whole world.
11. The mirror grew heavy in his hands.
12. It slipped and fell.
13. The mirror broke into small splinters on the rocks.
14. A cold wind carried the splinters across the world.
15. A splinter in the eye made everything look bad.
16. A splinter in the heart made it cold.
17. In a small town, Kai and Gerda lived in two houses, side by side.
18. Each house had a box of roses by its window.
19. The two boxes had a board between them.
20. Kai and Gerda climbed across the board each day.
21. They loved each other like brother and sister.
22. In winter, Kai's grandmother told them of the Snow Queen by the fire.
23. The Snow Queen lived in the snow and cold.
24. "She looked in at windows and made them cold with frost," said grandmother.
25. "She had a palace of ice, up in the north," grandmother said.
26. Kai looked at the frost on his window.
27. Kai said he would put the Snow Queen by the warm fire.
28. Gerda laughed at this.
29. Kai and Gerda looked at their roses each day.
30. One cold winter night, Kai sat by his small window.
31. A cold wind blew a small splinter into Kai's eye.
32. Kai rubbed his eye with his hand, but the splinter stayed.
33. That night, another splinter went into Kai's heart, and it stayed.
34. The splinter in his eye made Kai see only what was bad.
35. The splinter in his heart made it cold, like the wind and the snow.
36. Kai's heart was cold like the snow, so he no longer loved Gerda.
37. That day, Kai looked at Gerda's roses and said they were bad.
38. He pulled the roses from their box and threw them.
39. "Stop!" Gerda said to Kai, but Kai only laughed.
40. Gerda did not want this bad change, but Kai would not stop.
41. Kai no longer wanted to sit by the roses with Gerda.
42. Gerda did not know why Kai had changed.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Long ago, a trickster troll made a magic mirror that turned good things bad, even a kind face cruel, and made bad things worse.
7-10: The troll carries the mirror town to town, laughing at what it shows, until he climbs a mountain to view the whole world.
11-16: The mirror grows heavy, slips, and breaks into splinters; a cold wind scatters them, and a splinter in the eye or heart makes things bad and cold.
17-21: In a small town, neighbors Kai and Gerda live side by side, each house with a rose box joined by a board they climb daily, loving each other like siblings.
22-25: In winter, grandmother tells them of the Snow Queen, who lives in the cold, frosts windows she looks in at, and has an ice palace far north.
26-29: Kai jokes he would put the Snow Queen by the fire, making Gerda laugh, and they look at their roses.
30-33: One winter night, a cold wind blows a splinter into Kai's eye as he sits by his window, and another goes into his heart that night.
34-36: The splinter in his eye makes Kai see only bad, and the one in his heart turns it cold, so he no longer loves Gerda.
37-38: That day, Kai calls Gerda's roses bad and pulls them from their box.
39-42: Kai only laughs when Gerda tells him to stop, no longer wanting to sit by the roses with her, and she cannot see why he changed.

The Snow Queen's Sledge / Gerda Sets Out / The Prince and Princess
1. Winter came, and snow lay on the town.
2. Boys carried their sleds to the town square.
3. Kai went to the square with his sled.
4. A white sledge came fast into the square.
5. A driver in white fur sat in the sledge.
6. Kai tied his sled to the white sledge.
7. The sledge flew out of the square and into the town.
8. Kai wanted to go home, afraid of the fast ride.
9. He thought of his warm house and grandmother by the fire.
10. The sledge went out of the town, into the snow.
11. Kai's hands were too cold to move, and the cold wind hurt his face.
12. Kai called out, afraid, but no one heard him.
13. The sledge stopped, and the driver looked at Kai.
14. The driver was the Snow Queen, in white fur.
15. Her eyes were as cold as the snow and the wind.
16. The Snow Queen put Kai into her sledge and wrapped him in fur.
17. In the cold night, the Snow Queen kissed Kai, and the cold left him.
18. The kiss made Kai forget Gerda, his grandmother, and his warm home.
19. The sledge flew north, and Kai was gone from the cold town.
20. Grandmother and Gerda stayed by day and by night, but Kai did not come home.
21. People in the cold town said that Kai had fallen and was gone from home.
22. Gerda did not believe this, for she had not seen it.
23. Spring came, and the snow melted from the town and the small house.
24. Their roses bloomed in the window boxes that spring.
25. Gerda looked at the roses in the window and thought of Kai and of home.
26. "I will go and find Kai now," Gerda said to grandmother by the fire.
27. Grandmother did not think Gerda would find Kai.
28. Gerda put on warm clothes and left the small house alone that cold day.
29. She went for days, north, into the cold snow, to look for Kai.
30. Gerda walked on and came to a great town with a palace.
31. Gerda had walked for many days, and she was tired and cold.
32. That night, Gerda went into the palace to look for Kai.
33. She came to a room where a prince and princess were asleep.
34. Gerda called out for Kai, and the prince and princess woke.
35. But the prince was not Kai, only a boy that looked like him.
36. Gerda told the prince and princess about Kai and the Snow Queen.
37. The prince and princess were kind, and they listened to her tale.
38. They believed her tale of the cold Snow Queen.
39. The next day, they gave Gerda warm clothes for her walk north.
40. They gave her a golden coach to ride north, too.
41. Gerda thanked the prince and princess and rode north in the coach.
42. The golden coach carried Gerda north, into the cold and snow.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Winter comes, and boys carry sleds to the town square; Kai goes too, and when a white sledge driven by a white-furred figure enters, he ties his sled to it, and it flies out of the square.
8-10: Afraid, Kai wants to go home and thinks of his warm house and grandmother as the sledge leaves town.
11-16: Kai's hands are too cold to move, his face hurt by wind, so he calls out but no one hears; the sledge stops, its driver the Snow Queen, who puts Kai in and wraps him in fur.
17-22: In the cold night the Snow Queen kisses Kai, and the cold leaves him but the kiss makes him forget Gerda, his grandmother, and home, and the sledge flies north with him gone; though people say he fell and is lost, Gerda refuses to believe it, not having seen it herself.
23-25: Spring comes and the snow melts; the roses bloom again, and Gerda looks at them and thinks of Kai and home.
26-29: Gerda tells grandmother she will find Kai, and despite grandmother's doubt, she puts on warm clothes, leaves alone, and walks north for days into the cold to find him.
30-34: Gerda walks on, tired and cold, to a great town with a palace, where that night she looks for Kai and finds a sleeping prince and princess who wake when she calls his name.
35-38: Gerda tells the prince and princess about Kai and the Snow Queen, and they are kind, so they listen and believe her tale.
39-42: The next day they give Gerda warm clothes and a golden coach for the road north, and she thanks them and rides into the cold and snow.

Robbers in the Forest / The Robber Girl / Freed to the North
1. The golden coach went into a forest, and Gerda was cold and afraid.
2. Many robbers came out of the dark forest and stopped the coach.
3. An old robber woman looked into the coach at Gerda.
4. She pulled out a long knife to hurt Gerda for the golden coach.
5. But the old robber woman had a daughter.
6. The daughter was a rough girl that carried a knife.
7. "Stop! I want her!" the daughter said to her mother.
8. The daughter stopped her mother's knife with her hand.
9. The robber girl was rough, but she smiled at Gerda.
10. The robber girl had Gerda now, not her old mother.
11. The old robber woman did not want this, but she let Gerda go.
12. The robber girl led Gerda to a castle in the forest.
13. "You will live with me in this castle now," the robber girl said.
14. Night fell as they came to the castle, and Gerda stayed close to the girl.
15. The robber girl's castle held many robbers.
16. The robber girl led Gerda to a corner of the castle.
17. She kept many animals in this castle, from the forest.
18. Wood pigeons sat in a cage in the castle.
19. A reindeer stood tied by a rope in the corner.
20. "The reindeer came from the north," the robber girl said.
21. She held her knife near the reindeer, and it did not move.
22. That night, Gerda and the robber girl lay on a bed of straw.
23. The robber girl kept her knife in her hand, near Gerda, that night.
24. "If anyone comes near you, I will use this," she said to Gerda.
25. Gerda was afraid to move in this castle, but not afraid of the robber girl.
26. "Why do you want to find this boy?" the robber girl asked.
27. Gerda told her of Kai, the splinters, and the Snow Queen's sledge that night.
28. The robber girl listened and said nothing.
29. The next day, Gerda asked the wood pigeons if they had seen Kai.
30. "We saw a sled tied to a white sledge," the pigeons said.
31. "It flew over the forest and went north," they said.
32. The reindeer said he knew the way to the north.
33. "The robbers tied me in this castle," the reindeer said.
34. "Go with the reindeer," the robber girl said.
35. She cut the rope from the reindeer and helped him go.
36. The robber girl gave Gerda warm mittens and warm clothes for the way.
37. She helped Gerda climb onto the reindeer's warm back, with the mittens.
38. "If you do not find your Kai, come back to me," she said.
39. Gerda thanked the robber girl and the reindeer for her help, and her mittens.
40. "Go and find your Kai," the robber girl said, and smiled.
41. The reindeer ran out of the castle and into the forest, to the north.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The golden coach enters a forest, and Gerda is cold and afraid when many robbers stop it; an old robber woman looks in and pulls a knife to hurt her for the coach.
6-11: But the woman's rough daughter, who carries a knife, says she wants Gerda and stops her mother's knife with her hand; smiling despite her roughness, the girl now has Gerda instead of her unwilling mother, who lets her go.
12-14: The rough girl leads Gerda to a forest castle, saying she will live there now, and as night falls Gerda stays close to her.
15-19: Inside the noisy castle, the robber girl takes Gerda to a corner where she keeps forest animals, with wood pigeons in a cage and a reindeer tied by a rope.
20-22: The reindeer came from the north, she says, kept still by her knife, and that night she and Gerda lie on straw.
23-28: That night the girl keeps her knife near Gerda, warning she'll use it on anyone near, and though Gerda is afraid to move she isn't afraid of the girl, who asks why she seeks this boy, hears of Kai and the Snow Queen's sledge, and says nothing.
29-32: The pigeons tell Gerda they saw a sled tied to a white sledge fly north over the forest, and the reindeer says he knows the way.
33-36: The reindeer, tied here by the robbers, is freed by the girl, who cuts his rope and gives Gerda warm mittens and clothes for the way.
37-41: She helps Gerda onto the reindeer's back, tells her to come back if she doesn't find Kai, and after Gerda thanks them both, sends her off with a smile as the reindeer runs out into the forest, north.

Northward with the Reindeer / The Wise Woman of the North / The Palace of Ice
1. The reindeer ran north through the cold night.
2. Gerda put on the warm mittens from the robber girl, and was not afraid.
3. Lights of many colors moved across the night sky.
4. "The lights come from the cold north, from the Snow Queen," the reindeer said.
5. "I saw her palace of ice," he said.
6. "It stood on ice, cold and white," said the reindeer.
7. Gerda asked the reindeer if Kai was in the palace of ice.
8. The reindeer did not know.
9. Gerda listened to the reindeer and grew a little afraid of the cold north.
10. But she did not turn back.
11. For many days and nights, the reindeer ran on.
12. At last, they came to a small house in the cold world.
13. A wise woman lived there.
14. The reindeer stopped at her house.
15. The wise woman looked out at Gerda and the reindeer.
16. The house was small and warm, inside and out.
17. "Come inside, out of the cold," she said.
18. Gerda told her the tale, from the mirror to the Snow Queen.
19. The wise woman listened to Gerda and said nothing.
20. "The Snow Queen's palace is many cold days north," she said.
21. "Kai sits in that palace now, his heart cold from the splinter," she said.
22. "Will you give Gerda magic to help her?" the reindeer asked.
23. "I have no more power to give her," the wise woman said.
24. "Her strength is her loving heart," said the wise woman.
25. "That is more than any magic in the north," she said.
26. "Leave her before the palace," she told the reindeer.
27. "From there, she will go on alone," the wise woman said.
28. The reindeer thanked her, and they went on into the cold.
29. The reindeer ran for two more days, and the cold grew worse and worse.
30. The reindeer came to the last of the cold world, and stopped.
31. "I will go no farther than this," the reindeer said.
32. Gerda climbed down and thanked the reindeer, and he stopped there in the cold.
33. She walked alone, through the wind and the snow.
34. The cold wind came at her from the north.
35. Her hands and feet grew cold in the wind, but she did not turn back.
36. At last, Gerda saw the walls of ice before her.
37. This was the Snow Queen's great palace of ice, cold and quiet.
38. Gerda walked through a door of ice and into the palace.
39. The floor was one great lake of frozen ice.
40. Gerda called out for Kai, but no one called back.
41. She walked from room to room in the palace to look for Kai.
42. She came at last to a great hall of ice.

Paragraphs:
1-3: The reindeer runs north through the cold night as Gerda dons warm mittens, and colored lights move across the sky.
4-8: The reindeer says the lights come from the Snow Queen and describes her ice palace, cold and white, but cannot say if Kai is there when Gerda asks.
9-11: Gerda grows afraid but will not turn back, and the reindeer runs on for days.
12-14: A small house appears at last, home to a wise woman; the reindeer stops.
15-19: The wise woman invites Gerda and the reindeer into her small, warm house, and after Gerda tells the whole tale from mirror to Snow Queen, the woman listens and says nothing.
20-23: She says the palace lies many cold days north, where Kai sits with a cold heart, and when the reindeer asks for magic, she has none left to give.
24-28: Since Gerda's strength is her loving heart, greater than any magic, the woman tells the reindeer to leave her before the palace to go on alone, and they go on.
29-32: After two more days of worsening cold, the reindeer stops at the edge of the cold world, says he can go no farther, and Gerda climbs down to thank him.
33-37: Gerda walks on alone through wind and snow from the north, her hands and feet growing cold, until at last she sees the walls of the Snow Queen's great ice palace, cold and quiet.
38-42: She walks through a door of ice into the palace, its floor one great frozen lake, and calling for Kai with no answer, searches room to room to a great hall of ice.

The Ice Puzzle / The Splinters Melt / Roses Again
1. Kai sat alone on the frozen lake.
2. Kai was blue with cold, but he did not move.
3. He did not feel the cold on his hands.
4. Many pieces of ice lay by him, and Kai moved them, one by one.
5. The Snow Queen had made Kai a promise.
6. Kai had to form one word to go free.
7. The Snow Queen had promised him the world, and new skates.
8. The word was "forever," but Kai did not form it.
9. He moved the pieces, and moved them back.
10. Kai's heart was cold, and he did not feel afraid.
11. Gerda ran across the ice and called out to him.
12. Kai looked up, but his face did not change.
13. He did not know her.
14. Gerda sat down by him and took his cold hands.
15. "Kai, it's me, Gerda," she said, but he only looked at his ice pieces.
16. Gerda held Kai and cried, for his heart was cold.
17. Her warm tears melted the splinter on his heart.
18. Kai's heart was warm again, and he looked at Gerda.
19. His eyes had tears, and he cried.
20. His tears washed the splinter out of his eye.
21. "Gerda!" Kai said, for he did not feel cold.
22. Kai did not remember the Snow Queen, or the ice palace.
23. She held him again, and did not let go.
24. The ice pieces moved by themselves.
25. One by one, the pieces went into the word.
26. The word was "forever."
27. "We must go before the Snow Queen comes back," Gerda said.
28. Kai took Gerda's hand, and they ran out of the palace.
29. The reindeer stood in the snow before the palace.
30. Kai and Gerda climbed onto his back, and he ran south.
31. Day by day, the snow grew thin.
32. The snow was gone, and grass grew.
33. At last, the reindeer took them to their town.
34. It was summer, and the sun was warm.
35. Kai and Gerda thanked the reindeer.
36. They walked to their house by the window.
37. Grandmother sat in the sun, as before.
38. Their roses bloomed by the window, as they did each summer.
39. Kai looked at Gerda and saw she had grown taller.
40. Gerda looked at Kai and saw he had grown taller.
41. They had grown up, but in their hearts, they still felt like children.
42. Kai and Gerda sat by the roses again.

Paragraphs:
1-4: Kai sits alone on the frozen lake, blue with cold and unable to feel his hands, moving the many ice pieces by him one by one.
5-9: The Snow Queen promised him the world and new skates if he could form one word, 'forever,' but he cannot, and only moves the pieces back and forth.
10-15: Kai's cold heart feels no fear; Gerda runs to him, takes his cold hands, and says her name, but his face does not change, for he no longer knows her, and he goes back to his pieces.
16-19: Gerda holds Kai and cries, and her warm tears melt the splinter on his heart until it grows warm again.
20-22: His tears wash out the splinter; seeing Gerda, he feels no cold, and forgets the Snow Queen.
23-25: She holds him tight, and the ice pieces move by themselves into the word.
26-28: The word is done; Gerda says they must go, and Kai takes her hand to run.
29-33: The reindeer runs south with them on his back, the snow thinning until grass grows, and at last brings them to their town.
34-38: It is summer; after thanking the reindeer, Kai and Gerda walk home, where grandmother sits in the sun as before and their roses bloom again.
39-42: Kai and Gerda see they have both grown taller, though in their hearts they still feel like children, and they sit by the roses again.
