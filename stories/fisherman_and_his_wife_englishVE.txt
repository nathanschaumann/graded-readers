The Fisherman and His Wife

A Poor Fisherman
1. A poor fisherman lived by the sea.
2. He lived there with his wife.
3. Their home was a small hut.
4. The hut had just one room.
5. Every day, the fisherman went to fish.
6. He sat by the water with a hook and line.
7. He waited there for a fish to bite.
8. Some days he caught a fish or two.
9. They ate the fish for their supper.
10. Other days he caught nothing at all.
11. But the fisherman and his wife lived on.

Paragraphs:
1-4: A poor fisherman lives by the sea with his wife in a small, one-room hut.
5-11: Every day he fishes with a hook and line, and though he does not always catch something, the fisherman and his wife live on.

The Fish That Could Talk
1. One day, the fisherman went to fish.
2. He sat by the sea with his hook and line.
3. Something big pulled on the line.
4. A fish came up out of the water.
5. The fish began to talk.
6. "Put me back in the sea," said the fish.
7. "Free me," it said.
8. "I can give you a wish," said the fish.
9. The fisherman did not want anything.
10. "I do not need a wish," he said.
11. "Call me if you need it," said the fish.
12. The fisherman let the fish go into the sea.

Paragraphs:
1-6: One day the fisherman catches something big on his line, and a fish comes up out of the water and begins to talk, asking him to put it back in the sea.
7-12: The fish offers to grant a wish, but the fisherman wants nothing, so the fish tells him to call again if he ever needs it, and he lets it go.

A Wish for a House
1. The fisherman went home and told his wife.
2. "The fish can talk and grant wishes," he said.
3. "But you did not ask for anything?" said the wife.
4. "Go back and ask for a nice house," she said.
5. So the fisherman walked to the sea again.
6. "Fish, fish, come to me!" he called.
7. The fish came up out of the calm water.
8. "What do you want?" it asked.
9. "My wife wants a nice house," said the fisherman.
10. "Go home, and your wish is done," said the fish.
11. The fisherman walked home again.
12. There, a nice house stood where the hut had been.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The fisherman tells his wife about the talking fish, and she is upset that he asked for nothing, so she sends him back to ask for a nice house.
7-12: He calls the fish, asks for the house, and walks home to find a nice house standing where the old hut had been.

A Wish for a Castle
1. The house was nice, but the wife wanted more.
2. "I want a great castle," she told the fisherman.
3. "A house is too small for us," she said.
4. But the fisherman walked back to the sea for her.
5. This time the water was not calm.
6. Small waves moved across the gray sea.
7. "Fish, fish, come to me!" he called.
8. The fish came up through the waves.
9. "What do you want?" it asked.
10. "My wife wants a great castle," said the fisherman.
11. "Go home, and your wish is done," said the fish.
12. He walked home and saw a great castle.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The wife is not satisfied with the house and demands a great castle instead, so the fisherman walks back to a sea that is no longer calm, with small waves on the gray water.
7-12: He calls the fish, asks for the castle on his wife's behalf, and walks home to find a great castle standing there.

A Wish to Be King
1. The castle was great, but the wife wanted more.
2. "I want to be king," she told the fisherman.
3. "I do not want a castle," she said.
4. The fisherman walked back to the sea.
5. The sky grew dark, and the wind blew.
6. He was afraid, but he called out.
7. "Fish, fish, come to me!" he called.
8. The fish came up out of the dark water.
9. "What do you want?" it asked.
10. "My wife wants to be king," said the fisherman.
11. "Go home, and your wish is done," said the fish.
12. He walked home, and his wife wore a gold crown.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The wife wants to be king instead of just ruling a castle, so the fisherman, afraid, walks back to the sea as the sky grows dark and the wind blows.
7-12: He calls the fish and asks for his wife to be king, and when he gets home she is already wearing a gold crown.

The Biggest Wish
1. As king, the wife still wanted more.
2. "I want the sun and the moon too," she said.
3. The fisherman went to the sea.
4. Black clouds filled the sky, and thunder came.
5. Lightning flashed, and huge waves rose.
6. He was afraid, but he called out.
7. "Fish, fish, come to me!" he called.
8. The fish came up through the huge waves.
9. "What do you want?" it asked.
10. "My wife wants the sun and moon," said the fisherman.
11. "That is too much to give," said the fish.
12. "Go home now," it said.

Paragraphs:
1-6: Even as king, the wife still wants more and demands the sun and moon too, so the fisherman goes to the sea through a terrible storm of black clouds, thunder, lightning, and huge waves.
7-12: He calls the fish, which comes up through the waves, but when he asks for the sun and moon this time, the fish says that is too much to give and tells him to go home.

Back to the Old Hut
1. The fisherman walked home through the storm.
2. The wind grew quiet as he walked.
3. When he came home, the castle was gone.
4. The small hut stood there.
5. His wife stood there too.
6. She no longer wore the gold crown.
7. "We are back where we started," she said.
8. "We asked for too much," said the fisherman.
9. The fisherman and his wife went into the hut.
10. It was small, but it was theirs.
11. They lived there again, as before.
12. They never called the fish again.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The fisherman walks home through the calming storm to find the castle gone, replaced by their old small hut, where his wife stands without her gold crown.
7-12: They agree they asked for too much and go inside the small hut to live as before, and they never call the fish again.
