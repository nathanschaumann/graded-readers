The Gingerbread Man

The Old Woman's New Cookie
1. An old woman lived in a small house.
2. She lived with an old man.
3. She wanted to bake something.
4. She mixed flour, sugar, and ginger in a bowl.
5. She shaped the dough into a little man.
6. She gave him eyes and a smile.
7. She named him the Gingerbread Man.
8. She put him into the oven.
9. A smell filled the small kitchen.
10. She opened the oven door.
11. The Gingerbread Man jumped up and ran out the door!

Paragraphs:
1-7: An old woman and an old man live in a small house, and she decides to bake something, mixing dough into a little man she names the Gingerbread Man.
8-11: She bakes him, a smell fills the kitchen, and when she opens the oven door, the Gingerbread Man jumps up and runs out.

Run, Run, As Fast As You Can
1. The old woman chased him out of the kitchen.
2. The old man chased him out of the house.
3. "Come here!" called the old woman.
4. But the Gingerbread Man only ran faster.
5. "Run, run, as fast as you can!" he called.
6. "You can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"
7. The old woman was not fast enough.
8. She was soon out of breath.
9. The old man was not fast enough.
10. His hat fell into the mud.
11. They both stopped by the road, tired.
12. The Gingerbread Man laughed and ran alone.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The old woman and the old man chase the Gingerbread Man out of the house, but he runs faster and calls back his running rhyme.
7-12: The old woman and the old man are too slow and worn out to catch him, so they stop by the road while the Gingerbread Man laughs and runs on alone.

The Cow in the Field
1. The Gingerbread Man ran down a long road.
2. He soon came to a green field.
3. A cow stood in the field and ate grass.
4. She saw the Gingerbread Man run past.
5. "Stop! I want to eat you!" said the cow.
6. But the Gingerbread Man did not stop.
7. "I ran from an old woman and an old man."
8. "Run, run, as fast as you can!" he called.
9. "You can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"
10. The cow ran after him but could not catch him.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The Gingerbread Man runs down a road to a green field, where a cow eating grass sees him and wants to eat him too.
6-10: He will not stop, and he calls back that he already outran an old woman and an old man, then his rhyme, and the cow cannot catch him.

The Horse by the Barn
1. The Gingerbread Man ran past a barn.
2. A brown horse stood by the barn door.
3. The horse saw the Gingerbread Man run by.
4. "Stop and play with me!" called the horse.
5. But the Gingerbread Man did not stop.
6. "I ran from an old woman and an old man."
7. "I ran from a cow too," he called.
8. "Run, run, as fast as you can!"
9. "You can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"
10. The horse ran fast but could not catch him.
11. Dust rose up from the dry road.
12. The Gingerbread Man came to a wide river.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The Gingerbread Man runs past a barn, where a brown horse sees him and calls for him to stop and play, but he will not stop.
6-12: He lists the old woman, the old man, and the cow he outran, calls his rhyme, and the horse fails to catch him as dust rises from the road before he reaches a wide river.

The Fox by the River
1. The wide river was too deep to cross.
2. The Gingerbread Man could not swim across it.
3. Water would make him soft and ruin him.
4. A fox came out of the woods.
5. "Why do you stop by the river?" said the fox.
6. "The water will make me soft," said the Gingerbread Man.
7. "I can carry you across the river," said the fox.
8. The Gingerbread Man did not want to get caught.
9. "You will not eat me?" said the Gingerbread Man.
10. "I only want to help you cross," said the fox.
11. The Gingerbread Man climbed onto the fox's back.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The Gingerbread Man reaches a river too deep to cross, and water would ruin him, so when a fox comes out of the woods, he stops.
5-11: The fox offers to carry him across, the wary Gingerbread Man asks if he will be eaten, the fox says he only wants to help, and the Gingerbread Man climbs onto his back.

The Fox's Trick
1. The fox came into the cold river.
2. The water rose past his legs.
3. "The water is getting too deep," he said.
4. "Climb up onto my head to stay dry," he said.
5. The Gingerbread Man climbed onto the fox's head.
6. The fox wanted to swim across the river.
7. "Climb onto my nose," he said.
8. The Gingerbread Man climbed onto the fox's nose.
9. The fox opened his mouth wide.
10. He ate the Gingerbread Man in one bite.
11. The fox was full and not hungry anymore.
12. The chase by the river was over.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The fox wades into the cold river, and as the water rises past his legs and grows deeper, he tells the Gingerbread Man to climb onto his head to stay dry.
7-12: The fox tells him to climb onto his nose, then opens his mouth and eats him in one bite, ending the long chase by the river.
