The Golden Bird

The Golden Apple Tree
1. In the king's garden stood a tree of golden apples.
2. Every night, the thief took one golden apple from the tree.
3. Each morning, the king counted his golden apples.
4. One morning, an apple was gone.
5. The king called his three sons into the garden.
6. King: A thief takes an apple from my tree every night.
7. King: One of my sons must watch the tree tonight.
8. Eldest: Father, I am the eldest son.
9. Eldest: I will watch the tree tonight.
10. King: Good.
11. King: Come and wake me if you see the thief.
12. Second: Father, I will watch the tree too.
13. King: Tonight your brother will watch, and you will watch tomorrow.
14. Night came, and the eldest son sat under the tree.
15. He watched the tree, and he waited for the thief.
16. He watched, but no thief came.
17. At midnight, he grew tired, and he fell asleep.
18. In the morning, another golden apple was gone.

Paragraphs:
1-7: The king's golden tree loses an apple every night, so he calls his three sons and tells them to watch it.
8-13: The eldest son offers to watch first, and the king tells the second son he will watch the next night.
14-18: The eldest son watches the tree, but at midnight he grows tired and falls asleep, and another apple is stolen.

Two Failed Nights
1. King: You fell asleep, and the thief came again.
2. Eldest: I am sorry, Father.
3. Second: Father, let me watch the tree tonight.
4. King: Do not fall asleep like your brother.
5. Night came again, and the second son sat under the tree.
6. He watched the tree, and he waited for the thief.
7. He watched, but no thief came.
8. Near midnight, he grew tired, and he fell asleep.
9. In the morning, another golden apple was gone.
10. King: Both of my elder sons have failed me, and I am worried.
11. Youngest: Father, let me watch the tree tonight.
12. Eldest: You will fail like your brothers.
13. King: Let him try.
14. King: He may do better than both of you.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The king scolds the eldest son for sleeping, and the second son asks for a turn to watch.
5-9: The second son watches the tree, but near midnight he too grows tired and falls asleep.
10-14: Another apple is stolen, so the youngest son asks for a turn, and the king agrees to let him try.

The Youngest Son's Watch
1. That night, the youngest son sat under the golden tree.
2. He did not fall asleep this time.
3. He watched the tree closely, and he waited for the thief.
4. He watched, but no thief came.
5. Near midnight, a golden bird came to the tree.
6. The bird took an apple from the tree.
7. The youngest son shot at the golden bird.
8. He only won one feather, and the bird flew away.
9. In the morning, the youngest son brought the feather to the king.
10. Youngest: Father, I saw a golden bird take the apple.
11. Youngest: I could not catch it, but here is a feather.
12. The king held the feather and looked at it closely.
13. King: I must have the golden bird.
14. King: My eldest son will go and find this golden bird.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The youngest son stays awake all night and watches the tree closely under the moon.
5-9: Near midnight, a golden bird comes to the tree, and the youngest son's shot only wins him one feather.
10-14: He brings the feather to the king, who is amazed by it and sends the eldest son to find the bird.

The Fox and the Two Brothers
1. The eldest son rode out to find the golden bird.
2. At the edge of a forest, a fox stepped onto the road.
3. Fox: Where are you going in a hurry?
4. Eldest: I do not need your help, fox.
5. Fox: Listen to me, and I will help you.
6. Fox: Tonight you will find two inns in a village.
7. Fox: Stay at the poor inn, not the fine one.
8. Eldest: I do not need advice from an animal.
9. The eldest son rode on and forgot the fox.
10. That night, he came to the village with two inns.
11. Loud music came from the fine inn.
12. The eldest son went inside and forgot his father's bird.
13. Days passed, and the eldest son did not come home.
14. King: My eldest son has not come home.
15. King: My second son must go and find the bird now.
16. The second son rode out on the same forest road.
17. The same fox stepped onto the road before him.
18. Fox: Stay at the poor inn, not the fine one.
19. Second: I do not need advice from an animal.
20. The second son also went into the fine inn.
21. Days passed, and the second son forgot the golden bird too.

Paragraphs:
1-9: A fox meets the eldest son on the road and warns him to choose the poor inn, but he will not listen.
10-13: The eldest son stays at the loud, merry inn and never returns home.
14-17: The king sends the second son, and the same fox gives him the very same warning.
18-21: The second son also refuses to listen to the fox and is lost at the merry inn.

The Youngest Son Meets the Fox

2. Youngest: Father, let me find the golden bird.

14. That night, he came to the village.
15. Loud music came from the fine inn.

Paragraphs:

The Golden Cage
1. In the morning, the fox stood on the road.
2. Fox: Today you will come to a castle.
3. Fox: A golden bird sits in a wooden cage.
4. Fox: Take the bird, but take the wooden cage too.
5. Fox: Do not put the bird in the golden cage beside it.
6. Youngest: I will take the wooden cage, fox.
7. The youngest son came to the castle that night.
8. He found the golden bird in its wooden cage.
9. A golden cage stood on the wall beside it.
10. He thought the bird looked poor in the plain cage.
11. He opened the golden cage and put the bird inside it.
12. The golden bird gave a loud cry.
13. Guards ran into the castle and caught the youngest son.
14. Bird King: Why did you try to steal my bird?
15. Youngest: I wanted to bring it to my father.
16. Bird King: I will spare your life for a promise.
17. Bird King: Bring me the golden horse, and you may have the bird.
18. The youngest son agreed, and the guards let him go.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The fox warns the youngest son to take the bird in its wooden cage, not the golden one.
7-12: At the castle, he finds the bird but puts it in the golden cage instead, against the fox's advice.
13-18: Guards catch him, and the Bird King spares his life if he brings back the golden horse.

The Golden Princess
1. The youngest son left the castle without the horse.
2. The fox stood on the road again.
3. Fox: You did not listen to me.
4. Youngest: I am sorry, fox.
5. Youngest: I will listen now.

11. That night, the youngest son took the princess's hand.
12. Princess: Where are you taking me?
13. Youngest: I must bring you to a castle.
14. Princess: Let me say goodbye to my father.
15. Youngest: The fox told me not to let you do that.

17. Her father woke and ran to the gate.
18. Guards ran out and caught the youngest son at the gate.
19. Golden King: Why are you taking my daughter away?
20. Youngest: I am sorry.
21. Youngest: I only did what she asked.
22. Golden King: You may have her if you pass one test.

Paragraphs:

The Fox Levels the Hill
1. Golden King: A hill stands outside my window.
2. Golden King: Remove the hill in eight days, and you may take my daughter.
3. The youngest son looked at the hill, afraid.
4. He left by the gate, afraid and alone.
5. The fox stood outside the gate.
6. Fox: Why do you look so afraid, my friend?
7. Youngest: The king wants me to remove a hill.
8. Fox: Go and rest.
9. Fox: I will move the hill for you.
10. Youngest: I cannot move a hill alone.
11. Fox: Trust me, and rest tonight.
12. The youngest son ate and fell asleep.
13. That night, the fox called to foxes.
14. A thousand foxes came and dug at the hill.
15. By morning, the hill was gone.
16. Golden King: You have passed the test, as I promised.
17. Golden King: Take my daughter.
18. The youngest son and the princess rode away.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The Golden King demands the hill be removed in eight days, and the youngest son feels afraid.
6-12: The fox promises to move the hill and tells him to rest and trust it.
13-15: Through the night, the fox and other foxes dig away the hill before morning comes.
16-18: The Golden King keeps his promise, and the youngest son rides away with the princess.

The Fox's Two Tricks
1. The fox rode along beside the princess and the youngest son.
2. Fox: We must visit the Horse King, as you promised him.
3. Youngest: I promised him the princess for the golden horse.
4. Fox: I will become the princess for one day.
5. The fox spoke a strange word and turned into a princess.
6. The real princess hid in a quiet cave and waited.
7. The youngest son brought the fox to the Horse King.
8. The Horse King looked closely, but he saw no trick.
9. Horse King: You kept your promise, so take the golden horse.
10. The youngest son rode away on the golden horse.
11. That night, the fox changed back and found the princess.
12. Fox: Now we must visit the Bird King, as you promised him.
13. Youngest: I promised him the horse for the golden bird.
14. Fox: This time, I will become the golden horse.
15. The real horse stayed safe near the princess.
16. The fox spoke a strange word and turned into a horse.
17. The youngest son brought the fox to the Bird King.
18. The Bird King checked the horse but noticed nothing wrong.
19. Bird King: You kept your promise, so take the golden bird.
20. That night, the fox slipped past the guards and ran.
21. The fox changed back into itself, safe in the forest.
22. Youngest: Fox, you have saved me three times now.
23. Fox: We are not finished yet, my friend.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The fox agrees to become the princess so the youngest son can keep his promise to the Horse King.
7-11: He trades the fox for the horse, and the fox later escapes and finds the real princess again.
12-16: The fox then agrees to become the golden horse, and the real horse stays safe nearby.
17-21: He trades the fox for the bird, and the fox again slips away and changes back.

14. Second: We should have listened to the fox.
15. The youngest son let his brothers ride with him.
