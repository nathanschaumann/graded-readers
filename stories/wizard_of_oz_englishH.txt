The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

The Cyclone / The Good Witch of the North / The Scarecrow
1. Dorothy was on a farm in Kansas with Uncle Henry and Aunt Em.
2. Toto was her little dog, and he went with her.
3. Uncle Henry looked at the sky, and a storm was coming.
4. "A cyclone is coming!" he called.
5. "Get to the cellar!" he said to Aunt Em and Dorothy.
6. Aunt Em ran down into the cellar under the house.
7. But Dorothy ran to find Toto, who was hidden under the bed.
8. She pulled Toto out as the wind grew loud.
9. The cyclone came before Dorothy could get down to the cellar.
10. The house spun and rose into the air.
11. Dorothy sat down with Toto.
12. The house did not fall.
13. It went up and up into the sky.
14. Then the wind grew still, and the house came down soft.
15. Dorothy got up and went to the door.
16. She opened the door and looked out.
17. Dorothy walked out of the house.
18. Little people came out to look at her.
19. The house had come down on the Wicked Witch of the East.
20. The Wicked Witch of the East was dead.
21. The little people were glad, for the witch had ruled them.
22. An old woman came out and walked up to Dorothy.
23. "I am the Good Witch of the North," she said.
24. "Your house made my people free," said the old woman.
25. "In Oz, a good witch rules the North, but a wicked witch still rules the West," the old woman said.
26. The little people took the silver shoes out from under the house.
27. They gave the silver shoes to Dorothy.
28. The old woman kissed Dorothy.
29. "No one will hurt you, for my kiss will keep you safe on the road," she said.
30. "Go to the Emerald City, and ask the Great Oz to help you get home."
31. "The road of yellow brick will take you there."
32. Dorothy thanked the old woman, and the little people were glad as she went down the road.
33. Dorothy went down the road of yellow brick with Toto.
34. She came to a pole.
35. A Scarecrow made of straw hung on the pole.
36. He winked at her.
37. Dorothy stopped, for she did not know a scarecrow could talk.
38. "Will you help me down from the pole?" the Scarecrow asked.
39. Dorothy pulled him down.
40. "Thank you," said the Scarecrow.
41. "I have wanted to get down."
42. "A farmer made me to scare crows from the corn," the Scarecrow said.
43. "But I have no brains, so I cannot think."
44. "I wish I had brains," said the Scarecrow.
45. "I am going to ask the Great Oz to help me get home," said Dorothy.
46. "Do you think he would give me brains?" the Scarecrow asked.
47. "I do not know, but you may come and ask him," said Dorothy.
48. The Scarecrow got up and went down the road with Dorothy and Toto.

Paragraphs:
1-5: Dorothy lives on a Kansas farm with Uncle Henry, Aunt Em, and her dog Toto, and one day Uncle Henry sees a storm coming and calls everyone to the cellar.
6-9: Aunt Em runs down into the cellar, but Dorothy runs back for Toto, who is hidden under the bed, and the cyclone hits before she can get down.
10-13: The house begins to spin and rises into the air, but it does not fall, and Dorothy sits and holds Toto close as it climbs into the sky.
14-16: The house moves on through the air with the storm, then the wind grows still and the house comes down soft, and Dorothy gets up and opens the door to look out.
17-21: Dorothy walks out of the house to find little people watching her, and learns that her house has come down on the Wicked Witch of the East, who ruled over them and is now dead.
22-25: An old woman, the Good Witch of the North, tells Dorothy that her house has set her people free, and that in Oz a good witch rules the North while a wicked witch rules the West.
26-29: The little people give Dorothy the dead witch's silver shoes, and the old woman kisses her so no one will dare hurt her.
30-32: The old woman tells Dorothy to follow the yellow brick road to the Emerald City and ask the Great Oz for help getting home.
33-36: Dorothy travels down the yellow brick road with Toto until she comes to a pole, where a Scarecrow made of straw winks at her.
37-39: Dorothy stops in surprise, since she did not know a scarecrow could talk, and when he asks for help, she pulls him down.
40-44: The Scarecrow thanks her, glad to be down at last, and explains that a farmer made him to scare crows, but he has no brains and wishes he had some.
45-48: Dorothy tells him she is going to ask the Great Oz to help her get home, and when the Scarecrow wonders if Oz would give him brains, she invites him to come and ask.

The Tin Woodman / The Cowardly Lion
1. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, and Toto went into a great forest.
2. They found a Tin Woodman standing still with an old axe.
3. Rust was on him.
4. "Oil my mouth," the Tin Woodman said.
5. Dorothy found an oil can and put oil on his mouth, and he could move it.
6. "Oil my arms and legs too, for I have not moved in a year," he said.
7. Dorothy put oil on his old arms and legs, and he could move them.
8. "Thank you," said the Tin Woodman.
9. "Why were you standing still?" Dorothy asked.
10. "I was a man who cut wood in this forest," he said.
11. "An old witch put a curse on my axe."
12. "The axe cut off my arms and legs, and a man made me new arms and legs of tin."
13. "The axe cut me, and I lost my heart."
14. "Now I have no heart, and I cannot love."
15. "I am going to ask the Great Oz to help me get a new heart," said the Tin Woodman.
16. "You may come with me to ask for brains and a home too," said Dorothy.
17. The Tin Woodman took his old axe, and they all went on down the road.
18. They went on in the forest.
19. A great Lion jumped out into the road with a loud roar.
20. The Lion ran at Toto.
21. Dorothy went to Toto and hit the Lion on the nose.
22. "Do not bite Toto!" she said.
23. "You should not bite a little dog!" she said to the Lion.
24. The Lion stopped and looked at Dorothy.
25. "I did not bite him," said the Lion.
26. "I wanted to scare him."
27. "You are a coward to scare a little dog," said Dorothy.
28. "I know I am a coward," said the Lion.
29. "A beast comes at me, and I run."
30. "Your roar is loud," said the Scarecrow.
31. "I wish I had the courage of a king of beasts," said the Lion.
32. "We are going to ask the Great Oz for help," said Dorothy.
33. "The Scarecrow wants brains, and the Tin Woodman wants a heart."
34. "Do you think Oz would give me courage?" the Lion asked.
35. "You may come with me and ask him," said Dorothy.
36. The Lion was glad, and he went with them.

Paragraphs:
1-3: Dorothy, the Scarecrow, and Toto enter a great forest, where they find a Tin Woodman standing still with rust on him and an old axe.
4-7: The Tin Woodman asks Dorothy to oil his mouth, then his arms and legs, and she frees him to move again after a year stuck still.
8-11: The Tin Woodman thanks Dorothy, and when she asks why he stood still, he tells her he was once a man until an old witch cursed his axe.
12-17: The axe cut off his limbs until a man made him new ones of tin, and at last it cut him and took his heart, so now he cannot love; he wants the Great Oz to give him a new heart, and he joins Dorothy and the Scarecrow on the road.
18-21: As they continue through the forest, a great Lion jumps out with a loud roar and runs at Toto, but Dorothy stands by the dog and hits the Lion on the nose.
22-26: Dorothy scolds the Lion for trying to bite a little dog, and the Lion says he only wanted to scare Toto, not bite him.
27-31: Dorothy calls him a coward, and the Lion agrees, explaining that he runs whenever a beast comes at him despite his loud roar, and he wishes he had the courage of a king of beasts.
32-36: Dorothy tells the Lion they are going to ask the Great Oz for help, and when the Lion wonders if Oz would give him courage, she invites him to come along, and he joins them gladly.

1. Soon they came to a ravine.
2. The ravine was too wide to jump.
3. "The Tin Woodman can cut down a tree to make a bridge," said the Scarecrow.
4. The Tin Woodman cut down a tree with his axe.
5. The tree fell across the ravine.
6. Dorothy walked across, with Toto behind her.
7. The Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman walked across too.
8. But the Lion was afraid to cross the narrow tree.
9. "Do not be a coward," said Dorothy.
10. The Lion walked across the tree, and it did not break.
11. Then two Kalidahs ran out of the forest behind them.
12. The Kalidahs had the heads of tigers and the bodies of bears.
13. "The beasts want to eat us!" said the Scarecrow.
14. The Tin Woodman ran back across the tree with his axe.
15. He cut the tree, and it fell into the ravine.
16. The two Kalidahs fell down into the ravine too.
17. "I thank you," said the Lion.
18. They went on and soon came to a wide river.
19. "We must cross the river," said the Scarecrow.
20. The Tin Woodman cut down trees and made a raft.
21. They got on the raft and went out on the river.
22. The water was strong and pushed the raft.
23. The Lion jumped into the water and swam by the raft.
24. He pushed the raft to the other side of the river.
25. They went on and came to a field of flowers.
26. The flowers had a strong smell, and it made Dorothy sleepy.
27. Toto and the Lion grew sleepy too, and soon they fell asleep.
28. But the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman were not sleepy at all.
29. "The Scarecrow and I do not breathe, so the flowers cannot hurt us," said the Tin Woodman.
30. The Tin Woodman took Toto, and the Scarecrow took Dorothy.
31. They went out of the field with Dorothy and Toto.
32. Dorothy and Toto woke up.
33. But the Lion was too big to take.
34. "We must find a way to save the Lion," said Dorothy.

The Queen of the Field Mice / The Emerald City
1. The Tin Woodman and the Scarecrow sat by the field of flowers.
2. The Tin Woodman heard a small cry in the grass.
3. A wildcat was after a little mouse.
4. The Tin Woodman cut down the wildcat with his axe.
5. The little mouse stopped shaking and looked up at him.
6. "Thank you," said the mouse.
7. "I am the Queen of the Field Mice."
8. "I will help you," said the Queen.
9. "The Lion is a friend, and he is asleep in the field of flowers," said the Tin Woodman.
10. "I will call my mice to help you," said the Queen.
11. The Queen gave a small cry, and mice came to the field.
12. "We must build a cart to take the Lion," said the Scarecrow.
13. The Tin Woodman cut wood and made a big cart.
14. The mice pulled the cart to the Lion in the field of flowers.
15. The Tin Woodman put the Lion on the cart.
16. The mice pulled the cart with the Lion out of the field.
17. Away from the flowers, the Lion woke up, and he and Dorothy thanked the little mice.
18. The Queen gave Dorothy a small whistle to call her mice.
19. They came to the gates of the Emerald City.
20. The gates were green.
21. A man came to them at the gates.
22. He wore a green coat and had a long beard.
23. "I am the Guardian of the Gates," he said.
24. "Why have you come to the Emerald City?"
25. "We must see the Great Oz," said Dorothy.
26. "You must all wear green glasses in this city," said the Guardian.
27. "The city is so bright that it would hurt you."
28. The Guardian put green glasses on Dorothy, Toto, and her friends.
29. He locked the glasses with gold.
30. The Guardian opened the gates.
31. Dorothy and her friends walked into the city.
32. Every house in the city was green.
33. The people of the city wore green clothes too.
34. The Guardian took them to a green palace.
35. "You may rest," he said.
36. "The Great Oz will see you soon."
37. Dorothy and her friends waited in the palace for the Great Oz.

The Great Oz / Into the West
1. Dorothy was called to see the Great Oz.
2. Dorothy walked into a big room.
3. In the room, she saw only a head.
4. The head could talk, but it had no arms and no legs.
5. "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible," the head said loudly.
6. "Why have you come to see me?"
7. "I want to go home to Kansas," said Dorothy.
8. "My friends want brains, a heart, and courage."
9. "I will help you, but first you must help me," said the head.
10. "You must kill the Wicked Witch of the West."
11. "It is my only rule," said the head. "Now go."
12. Oz called the Scarecrow into the big room next.
13. But now, Oz was not a head.
14. Oz was a lady.
15. She told the Scarecrow the rule too: kill the witch first.
16. Oz called the Tin Woodman and the Lion in next, one at a time.
17. To the Tin Woodman, Oz was a terrible beast.
18. To the Lion, Oz was fire.
19. The four friends went west to find the Wicked Witch.
20. The Wicked Witch of the West had one eye that could see far.
21. She saw the four friends come into her land.
22. She was angry, and she called many wolves.
23. "Go and kill them!" she said to the wolves.
24. The wolves ran at the four friends with sharp teeth.
25. The Tin Woodman cut down the wolves with his axe.
26. Not one wolf got away.
27. The witch was still angry, so she called many crows.
28. "Go and hurt their eyes!" she said to the crows.
29. The crows flew at the four friends.
30. The Scarecrow stopped each crow.
31. Not one crow got away.
32. The witch called many black bees.
33. "Go and sting them all!" she said to the bees.
34. The Scarecrow pulled out his straw and put it on Dorothy, Toto, and the Lion.
35. The straw kept the bees away.
36. The bees flew at the Tin Woodman, but their stings broke on his tin body.

22. One silver shoe came off her foot.

24. Dorothy was angry, for it was her shoe.
25. A bucket of water was in the kitchen.
26. Dorothy took the bucket and threw the water at the witch.
27. The witch gave a great cry.
28. "You wicked one!" said the witch.
29. The witch melted away.
30. Dorothy took her silver shoe and put it on.
31. The people of the castle came in and saw that the witch was gone.
32. "You have set us free!" said one of them.
33. They were glad, for the witch had ruled them for many years.
