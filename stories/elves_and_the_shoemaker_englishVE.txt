The Elves and the Shoemaker

The Last Piece of Leather
1. A poor shoemaker had only one piece of leather.
2. He lived with his wife in a small shop.
3. That evening, he cut the leather into shapes for shoes.
4. He left the pieces on the table.
5. The shoemaker went to bed.
6. In the morning, he came back to the shop.
7. Two perfect shoes stood on the table.
8. Soon a rich man came into the shop.
9. He tried on the shoes and loved them.
10. He paid the shoemaker a lot of gold for them.

Paragraphs:
1-5: A poor shoemaker lives with his wife in a small shop, and one evening he cuts his last piece of leather into shapes for shoes before going to bed.
6-10: In the morning he finds two perfect shoes already made, and a rich man who comes into the shop tries them on and pays well for them.

More Shoes, More Gold
1. The shoemaker bought leather for two pairs of shoes.
2. That night, he cut the leather into shapes again.
3. He left the pieces on the table.
4. In the morning, two pairs of shoes stood there.
5. Two more customers came and bought them.
6. Soon the shoemaker had gold for leather for four pairs.
7. Every night, he cut leather for more shoes.
8. Every morning, shoes stood on the table.
9. People came from other towns to buy his shoes.
10. The shoemaker and his wife became rich from the shoes.
11. But the shoemaker never saw who made the shoes.
12. He wanted to know who helped him every night.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The shoemaker uses the gold to buy leather for two pairs of shoes, cuts it that night, and finds two pairs standing on the table by morning.
5-8: More customers buy the shoes, the shoemaker soon has gold for leather for four pairs, and new shoes stand on the table every morning.
9-12: People come from other towns to buy the shoes, the shoemaker and his wife grow rich, and he wonders who has been helping him every night.

Two Little Elves
1. "Let us hide and know who helps us," he said.
2. That night, he cut leather again.
3. He left the pieces on the table.
4. Then the shoemaker and his wife hid.
5. They waited quietly for midnight.
6. At midnight, two little elves ran into the shop.
7. The elves had no clothes on.
8. They climbed on the table.
9. The elves cut and sewed the leather into shoes.
10. Before the sun came up, the elves ran out.

Paragraphs:
1-5: The shoemaker asks his wife to hide with him and know who helps them, so that night he cuts leather as always and they hide to wait for midnight.
6-10: At midnight two little elves with no clothes climb onto the table and cut and sew the leather into shoes before running out as the sun comes up.

A Gift for the Elves
1. "We must thank the two little elves," said the wife.
2. "They have no clothes, even in the cold," she said.
3. She took cloth and sewed tiny shirts and coats.
4. The shoemaker cut leather for two shoes to match.
5. That night, they put the clothes on the table.
6. They put the tiny shoes with the clothes.
7. This time, they left no leather on the table.
8. Then the shoemaker and his wife hid and watched again.
9. They waited quietly for the elves to come.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The wife says they must thank the elves for having no clothes even in the cold, so she sews tiny shirts and coats while the shoemaker cuts leather for shoes to match.
5-9: That night they put the clothes and tiny shoes on the table instead of leather, then hide and watch again, waiting quietly for the elves.

The Elves Dance Away
1. At midnight, the two elves ran into the shop.
2. But no leather was on the table.
3. They saw tiny shirts, coats, and shoes.
4. The shoemaker and his wife watched.
5. The elves put on the shirts and the coats.
6. They put the shoes on.
7. The two elves laughed and danced around the room.
8. They danced out the door.
9. The elves ran out and never came back.
10. The shoemaker did not need help again.
11. He stayed rich and lived for many years.

Paragraphs:
1-4: At midnight the two elves find no leather on the table, only tiny shirts, coats, and shoes, and the shoemaker and his wife watch to see what happens.
5-11: The elves put on the little clothes and shoes, laugh and dance around the room, and run out the door, never to return, while the shoemaker stays rich and lives well for many years.
