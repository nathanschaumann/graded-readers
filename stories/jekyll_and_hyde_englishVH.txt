Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

The Story of the Door
1. Mr Utterson was a lawyer in London, and he was a man who did not talk much.
2. He had one friend, Richard Enfield, who was his cousin.
3. Every Sunday the two men walked together in the streets of the city.
4. One Sunday they came to a small street, and Enfield stopped and looked at a door.
5. "Do you see that door?" Enfield said.
6. "I know a strange story about it."
7. "I went home at 3 in the morning, and it was dark," Enfield said.
8. "I saw a small man who walked fast, and I saw a girl who was running to the same corner."
9. "They met at the corner, and the man walked over the girl, and he did not stop."
10. "The girl screamed, and I ran to them and took the man by the arm."
11. "He was not afraid, but he was ugly, and I wanted to kill him."
12. "People came out of the houses, and the family of the girl came, and a doctor came."
13. "The doctor was white, and he wanted to kill the man too."
14. "We said that the man must pay money to the family of the girl, or we would tell all London what he had done."
15. "The man said, 'I will pay,' and he went to this door, and he opened it with a key."
16. "He came out with some money and a paper, and the paper was a check for 100 pounds."
17. "The name on the check was a good name that all London knows," Enfield said.
18. "I will not say it, Utterson, but the name of the man was Hyde."
19. "Is this house his home?" Utterson asked.
20. "No, this is a door at the back, and the house is in the next street," Enfield said.
21. "But Hyde has a key, and I think that he knows some bad thing about the man who signed the check."
22. "What is Hyde like?" Utterson asked.
23. "He is not easy to tell about," Enfield said.
24. "There is something wrong with him, and I never saw a man that I liked so little."
25. "I do not know why, but I could not look at him."
26. Utterson was quiet for a time, and he looked at the door.
27. "Are you sure that he used a key?" he asked.
28. "I am sure," Enfield said.
29. "I know the name on the check, Enfield, and I ask you to say no more about it," Utterson said.

The Search for Mr Hyde
1. That night Utterson went home, and after dinner he took a paper from his safe.
2. It was the will of Doctor Jekyll, who was his friend and his client.
3. The will said that if Doctor Jekyll died, all that he had would go to Edward Hyde.
4. It also said that if Doctor Jekyll went away for 3 months, Hyde would take his place.
5. Utterson had not liked the will before, because he did not know Hyde.
6. He liked it less now, because he knew what Enfield had said of Hyde.
7. The next day Utterson went to see Doctor Lanyon, who was a friend of Jekyll.
8. "Have you heard of a man named Hyde?" Utterson asked.
9. "No, I do not know him," Lanyon said.
10. "Jekyll and I do not talk now, because he went wrong in his mind, and he speaks of things that are not science."
11. "We had a quarrel about it, and I have not seen him for 10 years."
12. That night Utterson could not sleep, and he had a bad dream.
13. In the dream he saw a man walk over a girl in the street, and he could not see the face of the man.
14. He saw the same man by the bed of Jekyll, and Jekyll got up and did what the man said.
15. "If he hides, I will seek him," Utterson said in the morning.
16. Every day Utterson went to the door, and he waited there in the morning, at noon, and at night.
17. At last, one night, he heard footsteps, and a small man came to the door and took out a key.
18. "Mr Hyde?" Utterson said.
19. Hyde stepped back from him.
20. "That is my name," Hyde said.
21. "What do you want?"
22. "I am a friend of Doctor Jekyll," Utterson said.
23. "I would like to see your face."
24. Hyde looked at him for a time, and then he showed his face in the light.
25. Hyde was pale and small, and he had a low voice, and it was hard to hear him.
26. "I will know you again," Hyde said, and he gave Utterson his address in Soho.
27. "How did you know me?" Hyde asked.
28. "Doctor Jekyll told me of you," Utterson said.
29. "That is a lie!" Hyde said, and he opened the door and went in.
30. Utterson stood in the street, and there was something wrong with Hyde that he could not name.
31. Utterson thought that there was some bad thing in the mind of the man, and that it showed in his face.
32. "Poor Jekyll," he said to no one, "I am afraid that you are in bad trouble."

The Carew Murder
1. A year after that, a maid saw a bad thing from her window in the night.
2. There was light in the lane, and she could see all of it.
3. A kind old man walked down the lane, and a small man came to meet him.
4. The maid knew the small man, because he had come to the house once, and it was Hyde.
5. The old man spoke to Hyde and asked him the way.
6. Hyde did not speak to him, and he hit the old man with a cane.
7. The old man fell, and Hyde walked over him and hit him again and again.
8. The maid heard the bones break, and she was so afraid that she fell down.
9. When she woke, it was 2, and she called the police.
10. The old man was dead, and one half of the cane lay by him.
11. In his pocket there was a letter to Mr Utterson.
12. The police took the letter to Utterson, and he came to see the body.
13. "It is Sir Danvers Carew," he said, "and he was my client."
14. "Do you know this cane?" the police asked.
15. "I know it," Utterson said, "because I gave it to Jekyll years before."
16. "I know the house of Hyde," Utterson said.
17. "Come with me to Soho."
18. A fog lay on the streets, and the morning was dark.
19. The street in Soho was small, and the people there were poor.
20. A woman opened the door, and she said that Hyde was not at home.
21. "He came home in the night, and then he went out again," she said.
22. The rooms were bad to see, and the papers in them were burned.
23. Behind the door the police found the other half of the cane.
24. "Hyde is gone," the police said, "and we will find him."
25. But no one could find Hyde, and no man knew his family.

14. "I hid my face and went through the house, and the servants looked at me."
15. "I drank, and in an hour I was Jekyll again."
16. "I saw that Hyde could take me when he wanted."
17. "I said that I would give up Hyde, and for 2 months I was Jekyll, and I was good."
18. "But I did not like to be only Jekyll, and I wanted to be free."
19. "At last I wanted to be Hyde again, and I drank."
20. "That night Hyde was worse than before."
21. "A kind old man spoke to him in the street, and Hyde hit him with a cane until he was dead."
22. "Hyde was glad with every hit, and I did not stop him."
23. "When it was done, I was afraid, and I ran to Soho, and I burned my papers."
24. "I drank, and I was Jekyll again, and I wept, and I said that I would never be Hyde again."
25. "That night I sat in my room, and I saw the face of the old man again and again."

Paragraphs:
1-4: Jekyll likes being Hyde, who is free, and drinks to become Jekyll again whenever he is tired of him.
5-9: Hyde does bad things and grows stronger, and Jekyll pays for a girl Hyde hurt and makes a bank for him.
10-15: Jekyll wakes as Hyde without the drink and must hurry through the house to change back.
16-19: Jekyll gives up Hyde for two months but drinks the drink again.
20-25: Hyde kills the old man in the street, and Jekyll burns his papers, changes back, and swears to give up Hyde.

The Last of Jekyll
1. "For 2 months I was a good man, and I helped many poor men."
2. "But Hyde was still in me, and he wanted to come out."
3. "One day I sat in a park in the sun, and I thought that I was a good man."
4. "I felt sick and cold, and I looked at my hand, and it was the hand of Hyde."
5. "I was Hyde, and all the police in England wanted to find me."
6. "I had no drink, and my room was far away."
7. "I took a coach to an inn, and I wrote a letter to Lanyon, and I wrote to Poole too."
8. "I did not dare to go out until it was night."
9. "That night I walked in the streets, and men ran from me, and I was afraid."
10. "When Lanyon gave me the drawer, I drank in his room, and he saw me change."
11. "I went home to my room, and I did not sleep."
12. "In the morning I was Hyde again, and I had to drink to be Jekyll."
13. "Every day it was harder to be Jekyll, and every day Hyde came without the drink."
14. "One day I sat at my window, and Utterson and Enfield spoke to me from below."
15. "I began to change while I spoke to them, and I shut the window and went in."
16. "The powder began to run out, and I sent Poole to get more."
17. "I drank the new powder, and nothing happened."
18. "I saw that the first powder had something in it that I did not know, and now I could not make it again."
19. "I write this in my room with the last of my powder."
20. "If I change while I write, Hyde will burn this paper."
21. "Hyde will be afraid of the police, and he will die by his own hand, or the judges will kill him."
22. "I do not know how long I will be Jekyll, and I write fast."
23. "Here I end the life of Henry Jekyll."

Paragraphs:
1-4: Jekyll is good for two months, but one day in a park his hand turns into Hyde's.
5-9: Now Hyde without the drink, Jekyll hides in an inn, writes to Lanyon and Poole, and walks the streets at night.
10-13: After Lanyon, every day is harder for Jekyll.
14-18: Jekyll changes at his window in front of his friends, and the powder runs out.
19-23: Jekyll writes with the last of his powder, expecting Hyde to die, and ends his story.
