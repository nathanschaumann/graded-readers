Snow White

A Wish for a Child
1. A king and queen lived in a castle.
2. For many years, they had no child.
3. The queen wanted a child of her own.
4. She wanted white skin, red lips, and black hair for the baby.
5. Soon the queen had a baby daughter.
6. The baby had white skin, red lips, and black hair.
7. The king and queen called her Snow White.
8. One morning, the queen fell sick.
9. She died when Snow White was still a baby.
10. The king was alone with his baby daughter.
11. For some years, he raised her alone.

Paragraphs:
1-7: A king and queen live in a castle with many people on the land of their kingdom, but for years they have no child; the queen wants a baby with white skin, red lips, and black hair, and soon has a baby daughter just as she wanted, so they call her Snow White, and servants help care for her.
8-11: But the queen falls sick and dies when Snow White is still a baby, leaving the king alone with his baby daughter, whom he loves and raises alone for some years.

The New Queen
1. When Snow White was still young, the king married again.
2. His new wife, the queen, was beautiful.
3. The king told Snow White to love the new queen.
4. Snow White grew up in the castle.
5. The new queen owned a magic mirror.
6. The mirror could answer the queen.
7. Every morning, the queen stood before the mirror.
8. "Mirror, mirror, who is the fairest in the land?" she asked.
9. "You are the fairest in the land," the mirror answered.
10. The queen smiled at this answer.

Paragraphs:
1-7: When Snow White is still young, the king marries a beautiful new queen and tells Snow White to love her; Snow White grows up in the castle, where the new queen owns a magic mirror that can answer her, and every morning she stands before it.
8-10: Every morning the queen asks the mirror who is the fairest in the land, and it always gives the same answer; many servants work in the castle and care for Snow White, but the people never see the mirror.

The Mirror's Answer
1. Years passed, and Snow White grew into a kind young woman.
2. She still had white skin, red lips, and black hair.
3. The people of the land loved Snow White.
4. One morning, the queen stood before her mirror again.
5. This time, the mirror gave a new answer.
6. "Snow White is the fairest in the land now," said the mirror.
7. Her face went red at this answer.
8. "Snow White will not be the fairest," she said.
9. The queen wanted to be the fairest in the land.
10. The queen wanted Snow White gone from the king's castle.
11. She called for her huntsman at the castle.

Paragraphs:
1-3: Years pass, and Snow White grows into a kind young woman who still has white skin, red lips, and black hair; the people of the land love her, and the queen is still beautiful too.
4-6: One morning the queen asks her mirror again who is the fairest in the land, and this time it gives a new answer: Snow White is the fairest now.
7-11: The queen says nothing, her face goes red, and she says Snow White will not be the fairest, for she wants to be the fairest in the land; wanting Snow White gone from the castle, she calls for her huntsman.

The Queen's Command
1. The huntsman came before the queen.
2. "Take Snow White into the forest," said the queen.
3. "Kill her there, and bring me her heart," she said.
4. The huntsman did not want to do this.
5. He looked at the queen and said nothing.
6. But he did what the queen asked.
7. He took Snow White into the forest.
8. Snow White believed the huntsman.
9. She did not know of the queen's plan.
10. Birds sang in the tall trees around them.
11. The huntsman stopped in the forest.
12. He raised his knife.

Paragraphs:
1-6: The mirror still gives the same answer every morning, so the huntsman is called before the queen, who orders him to take Snow White into the forest, kill her, and bring back her heart, smiling at him; he does not want to do this, and he says nothing.
7-12: But he does as the queen asks and leads Snow White into the forest, where she believes him and knows nothing of the queen's plan; as birds sing around them, the huntsman stops and raises his knife.

Sparing Snow White
1. Snow White saw the knife and moved back.
2. "Do not kill me," she said.
3. "I will run away and never come back," she said.
4. The huntsman looked at her kind face.
5. He could not kill her with the knife.
6. "Run, and never come back to the castle," he said.
7. Snow White ran into the forest alone.
8. The huntsman wanted a heart for the queen.
9. He found a deer in the forest.
10. The huntsman killed the deer, not Snow White.
11. He went back to the castle in the evening.
12. He carried the deer's heart to the queen.
13. "Here is Snow White's heart," he told the queen.
14. The queen believed Snow White was dead, and she smiled.

Paragraphs:
1-7: Snow White begs the huntsman not to kill her and promises to run away and never come back; he cannot do it, so he tells her to run and never come back, and she runs alone into the forest as the birds still sing around her.
8-14: The huntsman, wanting a heart for the queen, finds and kills a deer in her place, goes back to the castle in the evening, and tells the queen it is Snow White's heart; the queen believes Snow White is dead, and she smiles.

Alone in the Forest
1. Snow White walked through the forest alone.
2. But no huntsman was there to help her.
3. Tall trees stood all around her in the forest.
4. Animals moved in the trees.
5. But the animals of the forest did not hurt her.
6. Some animals walked near her in the trees.
7. Snow White ate nothing in the forest.
8. Evening came, and the forest was black.
9. It was black all around her now.
10. Snow White found no house and no bed there.
11. She walked on, and the morning came.
12. She saw the door of a small cottage in the trees.

Paragraphs:
1-3: Snow White walks through the forest alone, away from the queen and the castle now, but with no huntsman there to help her, past tall trees standing all around.
4-7: Animals move in the trees but do not hurt her, and some walk near her; she eats nothing in the forest and wants to see people again.
8-12: Evening comes and the forest is black all around her, and she finds no house and no bed there; she walks on, and in the morning she sees the door of a small cottage in the trees.

The Little Cottage
1. Snow White walked up to the small cottage.
2. Birds sang all around her in the morning.
3. Snow White called at the door.
4. No one answered the door of the cottage.
5. Snow White opened the door and looked inside.
6. She saw a small table with seven small plates.
7. She ate a little food from all seven small plates.
8. Upstairs, she found seven small beds.
9. Snow White was tired.
10. She lay down on the smallest bed.
11. Soon Snow White fell asleep.

Paragraphs:
1-2: Snow White walks up to a small cottage that stands alone in the forest, with birds singing all around her in the morning, while the queen is away in her castle.
3-7: Snow White calls at the door, but no one answers and no one is home, so she opens the door and looks inside, finding a small table with seven small plates, and she eats a little food from all seven.
8-11: Upstairs, she finds seven small beds; tired, she lies down on the smallest bed and soon falls asleep.

The Seven Dwarfs
1. That evening, seven dwarfs came home from the mine.
2. They found their small door open.
3. They found food gone from their small plates.
4. They found Snow White asleep upstairs.
5. Snow White woke and saw the seven dwarfs.
6. She told them about the queen and the huntsman.
7. "Stay here with us," said the oldest dwarf.
8. "Keep house for us when we work," he said.
9. There was food and a bed for Snow White.
10. The dwarfs wanted Snow White to stay with them.
11. Snow White said she wanted to stay.
12. She kept the small house for them.
13. Snow White made food for them in the morning.
14. "Never open the door to a stranger," the dwarfs warned.

Paragraphs:
1-6: That evening, seven dwarfs come home from the mine and find their door open, food gone from their plates, and Snow White asleep upstairs; she wakes and tells them about the queen and the huntsman, so the dwarfs now know about the queen and her mirror.
7-14: The oldest dwarf offers to let her stay and keep house when they work, and there is food and a bed for her; the dwarfs want her to stay and she says she wants to stay, so she keeps their small house and makes food for them in the morning, and they warn her never to open the door to a stranger.

The Tight Laces
1. The queen's mirror said Snow White was still alive.
2. She disguised herself as an old woman.
3. She carried pretty laces in a basket to the cottage.
4. The seven dwarfs were still away at the mine in the forest.
5. Snow White did not know the old woman at the door.
6. Snow White opened the cottage door for the old woman.
7. "I have pretty laces for you," said the old woman.
8. The old woman wanted to hurt Snow White with the laces.
9. The old woman pulled the dress laces too tight at the cottage.
10. Snow White could not breathe, and she fell to the floor.
11. That evening, the dwarfs returned and found Snow White on the floor.
12. "Snow White's laces are too tight for the dress," said one dwarf.
13. The dwarfs cut the tight laces from the dress.
14. Snow White could breathe again in the small cottage.
15. "Never open the door to anyone," the dwarfs warned.

Paragraphs:
1-8: The queen's mirror says Snow White is still alive, so she disguises herself as an old woman, and carries pretty laces in a basket to the cottage while the seven dwarfs are away at the mine; Snow White, not knowing the old woman, opens the door for her, and the old woman means to hurt her.
9-15: The old woman pulls the dress laces too tight, and Snow White falls to the floor unable to breathe; that evening the dwarfs return, find her, cut the tight laces from the dress, and she can breathe again, so they warn her never to open the door to anyone.

The Poisoned Comb
1. The queen learned that Snow White was still alive.
2. She made a poisoned comb and disguised herself again.
3. She carried the comb in a basket to the cottage.
4. Snow White remembered the dwarfs and did not open the door.
5. "Look at this pretty comb," said the old woman.
6. Snow White looked through the window at the comb.
7. She opened the door at the cottage just a little.
8. The old woman ran the comb through Snow White's hair.
9. The poison in the comb worked, and she fell to the floor.
10. That evening, the dwarfs returned and found Snow White on the floor.
11. "There is a comb in Snow White's hair," said one dwarf.
12. They pulled out the poisoned comb from her hair.
13. Snow White woke up again in the cottage.
14. "Do not open the door, not even a little," the dwarfs warned.

Paragraphs:
1-4: The queen learns that Snow White is still alive, makes a poisoned comb, disguises herself again, and carries it to the cottage, but this time Snow White remembers the dwarfs and does not open the door.
5-9: The old woman calls out with the pretty comb, and Snow White, looking through the window, opens the door just a little; the old woman means to hurt her again and combs the poison into her hair, and she falls down.
10-14: That evening the dwarfs return, find her, pull out the poisoned comb, and she wakes up again; this time they warn her not to open the door even a little.

The Poisoned Apple

12. Snow White believed the old woman's apple was safe.

7-11: The old woman offers an apple at the window, but Snow White wants none from a stranger, so the old woman cuts the apple in half and eats the white half herself to show it is safe.
12-15: Snow White believes the apple is safe now, opens the door to the old woman, takes the red half, bites into it, and falls to the floor.

The Glass Coffin

2. They found Snow White on the floor.

4. The dwarfs could not wake Snow White again.
5. They believed Snow White had died, and the seven dwarfs were sad.
6. But Snow White still did not look dead.
7. The dwarfs did not want to bury Snow White.
8. They made a coffin of glass for Snow White.
9. The dwarfs put Snow White inside the glass coffin.
10. They carried the glass coffin to a hill in the forest.
11. Birds and animals came to the hill to see Snow White.
12. Years passed on the hill, and Snow White did not change.
13. She still looked as if she were asleep.

Paragraphs:
1-6: That evening the dwarfs return, find Snow White on the floor with no laces and no comb to blame this time, and cannot wake her, so they believe she has died, and they are sad.
7-10: But Snow White does not look dead, so the dwarfs will not bury her; instead they make a coffin of glass, put her inside it, and carry it to a hill in the forest.
11-13: The dwarfs want to see Snow White again, but she does not wake, and birds and animals come to see her too; years pass, and Snow White does not change, still looking as if she were asleep.

The Prince in the Forest

2. At the hill, he saw the glass coffin.
3. Snow White lay inside the glass coffin, asleep.
4. The prince stopped at the hill and looked at her.
5. He asked the seven dwarfs about her.
6. "We believe she died, but she never changes," said one dwarf.
7. The prince wanted to see Snow White every day.
8. "Let me stay here beside her," he asked the dwarfs.
9. The seven dwarfs saw his sad face.
10. They let the prince come near the glass coffin.
11. He opened the glass coffin with care.
12. The prince kissed Snow White.

Paragraphs:
1-4: One morning, a prince comes through the forest, sees the glass coffin at the hill, where Snow White lies asleep, and stops to look at her.
5-8: He asks the seven dwarfs about her, and they say they believe she died, though she never changes; unable to look away, the prince wants to see her every day and asks to stay beside her.
9-12: The seven dwarfs see his sad face and let him come near the glass coffin, where birds and animals still come to the hill; he opens the coffin with care and kisses Snow White.

Snow White Wakes
1. As the prince kissed Snow White, he raised her from the coffin.
2. The poisoned apple fell from Snow White's mouth.
3. Snow White opened her eyes at last.
4. She saw the prince near her.
5. "Who are you?" Snow White asked.
6. "You are safe with me now," said the prince.
7. He told Snow White about the glass coffin and the seven dwarfs.
8. Snow White sat up and saw the seven dwarfs near her.
9. "You slept for many years," said one dwarf.
10. "Will you come to my castle with me?" the prince asked.
11. "Will you be my wife?" he asked Snow White.
12. Snow White looked at the prince's eyes and smiled.
13. "I will," said Snow White.
